# Blog Structure Patterns

This document defines 5 structural patterns for blog posts. Each pattern includes the section-by-section structure, when to use it, and tips for execution.

---

## Pattern 1: How-To Post

### When to Use
- The reader wants to accomplish a specific task
- The content follows a sequential process
- Keywords include "how to," "guide," "tutorial," "step by step"

### Structure

```
# How to [Accomplish Specific Result]

[Introduction: state the problem, promise the solution, preview the steps]

## What You'll Need / Prerequisites (optional, for technical posts)
[Tools, accounts, skills required before starting]

## Step 1: [Action Verb] + [Specific Task]
[Explanation of this step]
[Example or screenshot]
[Common mistake to avoid]

## Step 2: [Action Verb] + [Specific Task]
[Explanation]
[Example]

## Step 3: [Action Verb] + [Specific Task]
[Explanation]
[Example]

[...continue for each step...]

## Common Mistakes to Avoid (optional but valuable)
[2-4 pitfalls with quick fixes]

## Conclusion / Next Steps
[Summary of what they've accomplished]
[What to do after completing these steps]
[CTA]
```

### Execution Tips
- **Number your steps.** Readers skim to find where they are. "Step 3" is scannable; "Next, you should..." isn't.
- **Start each step with a verb.** "Configure your DNS settings" not "DNS settings configuration."
- **Include the expected outcome of each step.** "After this step, you should see a green checkmark on your dashboard."
- **One action per step.** If a step requires two distinct actions, split it into two steps.
- **Include a "Common Mistakes" section.** This is SEO gold — it captures "why isn't [thing] working?" searches.

### Length: 1,000-2,000 words
Shorter for simple tasks (5-7 steps). Longer for complex processes (10+ steps).

## Pattern 2: Listicle

### When to Use
- The content is a collection of tips, tools, resources, or ideas
- No inherent order is required (any item could stand alone)
- Keywords include "best," "top," "[number] ways to," "tips for"

### Structure

```
# [Number] [Noun] to [Benefit/Result]

[Introduction: why this list matters, who it's for]

## 1. [Item Name]
[Why this item made the list]
[Practical application or example]
[Key takeaway in bold]

## 2. [Item Name]
[Why]
[Application]
[Takeaway]

[...continue for each item...]

## Bonus: [Extra Item] (optional — adds perceived value)
[Brief explanation]

## Which [Items] Are Right for You? / Conclusion
[Help the reader choose from the list based on their situation]
[CTA]
```

### Execution Tips
- **Front-load the best items.** Put your strongest 2-3 items first. Readers who leave early still get value.
- **Vary the depth.** Not every item needs 200 words. Some deserve a paragraph; others need three. Monotonous length feels robotic.
- **Include a practical example for at least half the items.** "Use the Pomodoro technique" is generic. "Set a 25-minute timer before your next deep work session" is actionable.
- **Don't pad the list.** 7 strong items beats 15 weak ones. Readers can smell filler.
- **Add a "Bonus" item.** It exceeds the promise of the title and feels generous.

### Choosing the Right Number
- **Odd numbers perform slightly better in headlines** (7, 9, 11)
- **Specific numbers feel more credible** than round ones (7 > 10)
- **Don't go below 5** (feels thin) or above 20 (feels exhausting)
- **Match the number to the topic.** "3 steps" works for simple processes. "15 tools" works for resource roundups.

### Length: 800-1,500 words
~100-150 words per item. Scale with the number of items.

## Pattern 3: Deep-Dive / Comprehensive Guide

### When to Use
- The topic requires thorough explanation and nuance
- You want to be the definitive resource on a subject
- Keywords include "complete guide," "everything you need to know," "in-depth," "[topic] explained"
- The audience is willing to read long-form content

### Structure

```
# [Topic]: The Complete Guide for [Audience]
OR
# Everything You Need to Know About [Topic]

[Introduction: why this topic matters, what this guide covers, who it's for]

## Table of Contents (recommended for posts over 2,000 words)
[Linked list of H2 sections]

## What Is [Topic]? / [Topic] Explained
[Definition and context]
[Why it matters]
[Brief history or evolution if relevant]

## Why [Topic] Matters for [Audience]
[Business impact]
[Concrete benefits]
[Data or statistics]

## How [Topic] Works
[Detailed explanation]
[Process or mechanics]
[Diagrams or visual aids]

## [Topic] Best Practices
[Actionable recommendations]
[Examples from real companies/practitioners]

## Common [Topic] Mistakes
[What goes wrong and why]
[How to avoid each mistake]

## [Topic] Tools and Resources (optional)
[Relevant tools, frameworks, or further reading]

## Conclusion / Key Takeaways
[Bullet-point summary of the most important points]
[CTA]
```

### Execution Tips
- **Include a table of contents** for posts over 2,000 words. Use anchor links so readers can jump to sections.
- **Front-load the definition.** "What is [topic]?" should be the first H2. Google uses this for featured snippets.
- **Use more visual aids.** Long-form content needs diagrams, tables, comparison charts, and images to maintain attention.
- **Include expert quotes or data points.** Comprehensive guides need credibility signals beyond your own experience.
- **Update regularly.** Deep-dive content should be treated as evergreen. Add a "Last updated: [date]" note.

### Length: 1,500-3,000 words
Can go longer for truly comprehensive topics, but break into multiple posts (pillar + cluster) if exceeding 3,000 words.

## Pattern 4: Comparison Post

### When to Use
- The reader is evaluating two or more options
- The content helps make a decision
- Keywords include "vs.," "compared," "which is better," "comparison," "[X] or [Y]"

### Structure

```
# [Option A] vs. [Option B]: [Key Decision Question]

[Introduction: why this comparison matters, what criteria you'll use]

## Quick Comparison

| Feature | [Option A] | [Option B] |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| [Criterion 1] | [Rating/detail] | [Rating/detail] |
| [Criterion 2] | | |
| [Criterion 3] | | |
| Best for | [Use case] | [Use case] |

## [Option A]: Overview
[What it is, key strengths, key weaknesses]

## [Option B]: Overview
[What it is, key strengths, key weaknesses]

## [Criterion 1]: [Option A] vs. [Option B]
[Detailed comparison on this specific dimension]
[Winner and why]

## [Criterion 2]: [Option A] vs. [Option B]
[Detailed comparison]
[Winner and why]

## [Criterion 3]: [Option A] vs. [Option B]
[Detailed comparison]
[Winner and why]

## When to Choose [Option A]
[Specific scenarios and user profiles]

## When to Choose [Option B]
[Specific scenarios and user profiles]

## The Verdict
[Clear recommendation with reasoning]
[CTA — often to try one of the options]
```

### Execution Tips
- **Put the comparison table near the top.** Many readers just want the summary. Give it to them immediately, then provide depth below.
- **Be genuinely balanced.** One-sided comparisons feel like sponsored content. Even if you prefer one option, acknowledge the other's strengths.
- **Use specific criteria, not vague ones.** "Ease of use" is subjective. "Time to complete [specific task]" is measurable.
- **Declare a winner per criterion AND an overall winner.** Readers want your recommendation, not "it depends."
- **Include a "When to choose" section.** Not everyone has the same needs. Help them self-select.

### Length: 1,000-2,000 words
Longer for complex, multi-criteria comparisons. Shorter for simple A vs. B decisions.

## Pattern 5: Opinion / Thought Leadership Post

### When to Use
- You (or the brand) have a perspective worth sharing
- The topic is debatable, trending, or contrarian
- The goal is to position the author as an authority
- Keywords may be less important; social sharing is the primary distribution

### Structure

```
# [Bold Claim or Perspective]
OR
# Why [Common Belief] Is Wrong
OR
# [Topic]: What Nobody Is Talking About

[Introduction: the conventional wisdom, then your counterpoint or unique angle]

## The Common Belief
[What most people think or do]
[Why it seems logical]

## Why I Disagree / What I've Seen Instead
[Your perspective with supporting evidence]
[Personal experience, client stories, or data]

## What This Means for [Audience]
[Practical implications of your perspective]
[What should the reader do differently?]

## The Nuance / When the Conventional Wisdom IS Right (optional but builds credibility)
[Acknowledge where the opposing view has merit]
[This makes your argument stronger, not weaker]

## What I'd Recommend Instead
[Your alternative approach]
[Concrete, actionable advice]

## Conclusion
[Restate your position confidently]
[Invite discussion or feedback]
[CTA]
```

### Execution Tips
- **Have a genuine perspective.** "I think quality is important" is not an opinion. "I think most freelancers should charge hourly, not project-based" is an opinion. Be specific and willing to be disagreed with.
- **Support opinions with evidence.** Personal experience, client results, industry data. An unsupported opinion is just a rant.
- **Acknowledge complexity.** The best opinion pieces say "Here's what I believe AND here's where I could be wrong." This builds trust.
- **Keep it concise.** Opinion posts lose their punch when they're too long. 600-1,200 words is the sweet spot.
- **Write in first person.** "I" and "we" are appropriate here. This is your voice, not an encyclopedia.
- **End with a question or invitation.** "What's been your experience? I'd love to hear if this resonates or if you disagree."

### Length: 600-1,200 words
Short and punchy. Every sentence should earn its place.

## Choosing the Right Pattern

Ask these questions to select the best structure:

1. **Is the reader trying to DO something?** → How-To
2. **Is the reader looking for a COLLECTION?** → Listicle
3. **Is the reader trying to UNDERSTAND something?** → Deep-Dive
4. **Is the reader trying to DECIDE between options?** → Comparison
5. **Is the author trying to PERSUADE or PROVOKE?** → Opinion

If the answer is "some of the above," default to the primary intent and borrow elements from secondary patterns. A how-to with a comparison table inside is perfectly fine.
