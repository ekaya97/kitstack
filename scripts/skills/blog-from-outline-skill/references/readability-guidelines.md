# Readability Guidelines for Web Content

This document provides detailed guidelines for writing readable blog content. Web reading is fundamentally different from print reading — people scan, skip, and abandon. These guidelines account for that behavior.

---

## How People Read on the Web

They don't. Research consistently shows:

- **79% of web users scan** rather than read word-by-word (Nielsen Norman Group)
- **Average time on a blog post** is 37-52 seconds — regardless of length
- **Reading follows an F-pattern:** readers scan the first line fully, then the first few words of subsequent lines, then jump to headers
- **Above the fold matters most.** The first 100-200 words determine whether someone stays or leaves
- **Mobile readers are even more aggressive scanners.** 60%+ of blog traffic is mobile

### What This Means for Writing
1. Front-load value — the most important information goes first
2. Make scanning productive — headers, bold text, and lists should tell the story independently
3. Short everything — sentences, paragraphs, sections
4. Visual variety — no wall of text should exceed 4 lines on a mobile screen

## Sentence Length

### Targets

| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| Average sentence length | 14-18 words | Optimal comprehension for web reading |
| Maximum sentence length | 30 words | Longer sentences cause re-reading |
| Minimum sentence length | 3-5 words | Short sentences create punch and rhythm |
| Sentence length variety | Mix short and long | Monotonous length is boring; vary for rhythm |

### The Rhythm Principle
Good writing alternates between sentence lengths. Read this aloud:

**Bad (monotonous):**
> "Value-based pricing is a model for service providers. It prices work based on client outcomes. This model works well for experienced practitioners. It requires a strong sales conversation. The best consultants use this approach regularly."

Every sentence is 7-10 words. It reads like a robot.

**Good (varied):**
> "Value-based pricing changes the game. Instead of billing by the hour, you price based on what the work is actually worth to the client — the revenue it generates, the costs it saves, the problems it eliminates. Not every freelancer can pull it off. But for those with a track record of measurable results, it's the most profitable model available."

Sentence lengths: 5, 30, 7, 19. The short sentences punch. The long sentence flows. The reader stays engaged.

### How to Shorten Sentences
| Before | After | Words Saved |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| "In order to achieve the best results" | "For best results" | 4 |
| "The reason that this is important is because" | "This matters because" | 5 |
| "It is important to note that" | (delete entirely, just state the thing) | 6 |
| "There are several factors that contribute to" | "Several factors contribute to" | 2 |
| "At this point in time" | "Now" | 4 |
| "Due to the fact that" | "Because" | 4 |
| "In the event that" | "If" | 3 |

## Paragraph Length

### Targets

| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| Sentences per paragraph | 2-4 | Longer paragraphs look like walls of text on mobile |
| Maximum paragraph length | 5 sentences / 75 words | Beyond this, readers start skipping |
| One-sentence paragraphs | Use sparingly for emphasis | Overuse makes writing feel choppy |
| Ideas per paragraph | 1 | Multiple ideas in one paragraph confuse scanning readers |

### The Mobile Test
Before publishing, view the post on a mobile device (or mobile preview). If any paragraph takes up more than half the screen, split it.

### Paragraph Structure
Each paragraph should follow one of these patterns:

**Topic-Support:** Start with the key point, then explain or support it.
> "Short paragraphs are essential for web readability. Research from the Nielsen Norman Group shows that online readers skip paragraphs longer than 3-4 sentences. On mobile devices, even shorter paragraphs perform better."

**Example-Insight:** Start with a concrete example, then extract the lesson.
> "A client once told me their proposal was rejected because it was 'too long to read.' The proposal was 12 pages. Their competitor's was 3. Conciseness isn't about having less to say — it's about respecting the reader's time."

**Question-Answer:** Start with a question, then answer it.
> "Why do most blog posts fail to generate traffic? Because they're written for the writer, not the reader. Start with what the audience is searching for, not what you want to tell them."

## Readability Scores

### Flesch-Kincaid Reading Ease

| Score | Interpretation | Suitable For |
|-------|---------------|-------------|
| 80-100 | Very easy (5th grade) | Consumer content, general audience |
| 60-80 | Standard (8th-9th grade) | Most blog content, B2B, professional |
| 40-60 | Somewhat difficult (college level) | Technical, academic, expert audience |
| Below 40 | Very difficult | Academic papers, legal documents |

**Target for most blog posts:** 60-75 (standard readability)

**How to improve your Flesch-Kincaid score:**
- Use shorter sentences (biggest impact)
- Use shorter words (2 syllables beats 4)
- Replace jargon with plain language
- Use contractions ("don't" instead of "do not")
- Break complex ideas into multiple simple sentences

### Hemingway Test
Run blog drafts through the Hemingway principle (even without the tool):
- Highlight sentences that are hard to read (>25 words with complex structure)
- Highlight adverbs that weaken verbs ("very quickly" → "fast")
- Highlight passive voice ("was created by" → "created")
- Highlight complex phrases ("utilize" → "use")

## Formatting for Scannability

### Bold Text
- Bold the 1-2 most important phrases per section
- Bold the first occurrence of key terms
- Bold key takeaways and recommendations
- **Never bold entire paragraphs** — if everything is bold, nothing is
- **Never bold more than 10% of the text** — it loses impact

### Bullet Points and Numbered Lists
- Use numbered lists for sequential steps or ranked items
- Use bullet points for collections of equal-weight items
- **Keep list items parallel in structure** — all start with a verb, or all are noun phrases
- **3-7 items per list is ideal.** Under 3 feels thin; over 7 feels overwhelming
- **Don't nest lists more than 2 levels deep** — it's confusing on screen

### Tables
Use tables when comparing multiple items across multiple dimensions. Tables are:
- Faster to scan than paragraphs
- Excellent for triggering Google featured snippets
- More accessible than trying to express comparisons in prose

### Blockquotes
Use for:
- Expert quotes
- Customer testimonials
- Important callouts or warnings
- Data points or statistics you want to highlight

### Whitespace
Whitespace is a formatting tool. Use it intentionally:
- Between sections (H2 headings)
- After complex explanations (give the reader a breath)
- Around images and tables
- Before and after blockquotes

## Vocabulary Guidelines

### Write for an International Audience
Even if your primary audience is in one country, clear writing is universal:

| Instead of | Use |
|-----------|-----|
| Leverage (as a verb) | Use, apply, take advantage of |
| Utilize | Use |
| Synergize | Combine, collaborate |
| Ideate | Brainstorm, develop ideas |
| Cadence | Rhythm, schedule, frequency |
| Paradigm shift | Major change, new approach |
| Best-in-class | Leading, top-performing |
| Cutting-edge | Advanced, modern, new |
| Deep dive | Detailed look, thorough analysis |
| Holistic | Complete, comprehensive, full |
| Move the needle | Make a difference, have impact |
| Low-hanging fruit | Quick wins, easy improvements |

### When Jargon Is OK
Jargon is appropriate when:
1. The audience uses it daily (developers understand "API," "deployment," "refactoring")
2. The jargon IS the keyword (don't avoid "CRO" if that's what people search for)
3. You define it on first use ("CRO — conversion rate optimization — is the practice of...")

### Active vs. Passive Voice

| Passive (avoid) | Active (prefer) |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| The proposal was written by the team | The team wrote the proposal |
| Costs were reduced by 15% | We reduced costs by 15% |
| The results were measured after 30 days | We measured results after 30 days |
| A decision needs to be made | You need to decide |

Passive voice is acceptable when:
- The actor is unknown: "The report was leaked" (who leaked it is unknown)
- The object matters more than the subject: "The Mona Lisa was painted in the 16th century"
- You're deliberately softening responsibility: "Mistakes were made" (though this is usually criticized)

## Proofreading Checklist

Before declaring a post "publish-ready," verify:

- [ ] No paragraph exceeds 4 sentences
- [ ] No sentence exceeds 30 words
- [ ] Every H2 is scannable and descriptive
- [ ] The first 100 words contain the target keyword
- [ ] The CTA is clear and specific
- [ ] No placeholder text remains ("[insert]," "TBD," "[example here]")
- [ ] All links work (internal and external)
- [ ] Bold is used for emphasis, not decoration
- [ ] The meta description is 150-160 characters
- [ ] The post reads well on mobile (short paragraphs, visible headers)
