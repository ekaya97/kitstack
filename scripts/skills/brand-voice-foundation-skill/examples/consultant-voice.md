# Example: Management Consultant Voice Guide

## Interview Transcript (Abbreviated)

**Q1: Audience**
"CEOs and COOs of manufacturing companies doing $20M to $200M in revenue. They have been running their business on intuition and it got them this far, but they know they need systems. They are skeptical of consultants — they have been burned before. They need to see that I understand their world before they trust me."

*Acknowledgment: Your reader is a skeptical operator. They have real-world experience and no patience for theory. That means every word you write needs to demonstrate hands-on knowledge, not academic credentials. Trust is the currency.*

**Q2: Three Words**
"Credible. Direct. Grounded."

*Acknowledgment: No flash, no personality tricks — just substance. Credible says your voice needs to carry weight. Direct says you get to the point. Grounded says you stay in the real world, not in frameworks. This is a "show your work" voice.*

**Q3: Admired Writing**
"Jim Collins — Good to Great era. Also Patrick Lencioni. They take complex organizational ideas and make them accessible without dumbing them down. And they back everything with evidence."

*Acknowledgment: Evidence-backed insight made accessible. That is a high authority, moderate jargon profile. Collins and Lencioni both use stories and specifics to ground their ideas. You want to sound like someone who has done the work, not someone who has read about it.*

**Q4: Cringe Writing**
"LinkedIn thought-leader posts. 'I fired my best employee and here is what happened.' Also any consultant who uses 'unlock value' or 'drive transformation.' If I hear 'synergy' one more time I will close my practice."

*Acknowledgment: Anti-performative, anti-buzzword. Your banned list is going to be heavy on consulting jargon. The LinkedIn cringe tells me you value substance over performance — your content earns attention through insight, not hooks.*

**Q5: Writing Sample**
Provided a section from a client case study:
> "When we walked the floor at Meridian's Akron plant, the bottleneck was obvious before anyone said a word. Three operators were waiting on a single QC station while two others sat idle on the packaging line. We repositioned the QC checkpoint upstream of the bottleneck, cross-trained two packaging operators for overflow QC, and reduced the wait time from 22 minutes to 4. No new equipment. No new hires. Just a different sequence."

*Acknowledgment: This is your voice at its best. Concrete, specific, evidence-based. You use numbers (22 minutes to 4). You use sensory details (walking the floor). And your punch line — "No new equipment. No new hires. Just a different sequence" — is devastating. That is the structure: observe, act, prove. We are going to codify that pattern.*

**Q6: Formality Scale**
"6. I want to be taken seriously by executives, but I do not want to sound like McKinsey."

*Acknowledgment: Professional without being corporate. Based on your writing sample, you are naturally about a 5-6 — complete sentences, no fragments, but warm enough to say "we walked the floor." You flex up for proposals and down for blog posts. The McKinsey ceiling is a useful guardrail.*

**Q7: Vocabulary Rules**
"I like: 'floor,' 'line,' 'shift,' 'sequence' — manufacturing language. I hate: 'unlock value,' 'drive transformation,' 'best practices,' 'low-hanging fruit,' 'circle back.' I use 'throughput,' 'lead time,' 'yield' — my clients know these terms. I avoid academic operations terms that sound impressive but add nothing."

*Acknowledgment: Manufacturing vocabulary is in, consulting vocabulary is out. Your jargon is the client's jargon — shop-floor language, not boardroom language. That is a powerful trust signal. You speak their language, not yours.*

**Q8: Desired Feeling**
"I want them to feel like they just talked to someone who actually understands their plant. Not someone who will come in with a binder of frameworks. Someone who has been on the floor."

*Acknowledgment: Trust through demonstrated understanding. Your reader should finish reading and think: "This person gets it. They have stood where I stand." Everything in your voice guide should serve that feeling — specificity, real examples, manufacturing language, no consulting theater.*

## Generated Voice Guide

# David Alcorn — Voice Guide

## Voice Summary

David sounds like the consultant who has spent more time on the shop floor than in the conference room. The voice is direct, evidence-driven, and grounded in the real language of manufacturing. Every claim is backed by a specific observation or measurable result. There is no consulting theater — no frameworks for the sake of frameworks, no buzzwords to signal sophistication. The work speaks for itself.

## Tone Rules

1. **Every claim needs evidence.** "We improved throughput" is not enough. "We improved throughput by 34% by repositioning the QC checkpoint" is the standard.
2. **Use the client's language, not yours.** "Floor," "line," "shift," "run" — not "operational ecosystem" or "production paradigm."
3. **Show the observation before the recommendation.** Do not say "You should cross-train operators." Say "Three operators were waiting while two sat idle. Cross-training solves this."
4. **End with the result.** The most powerful position in any paragraph is the last sentence. Put the measurable outcome there.
5. **Never perform expertise. Demonstrate it.** Do not list credentials or frameworks. Describe what you saw, what you did, and what changed.
6. **Use the "No X. No Y. Just Z" structure for maximum impact.** It is a signature pattern — use it for key results where simplicity is the insight.

## Vocabulary

**Words we use:** floor, line, shift, run, sequence, throughput, lead time, yield, cycle time, bottleneck, wait time, cross-train, reposition, measure, observe, reduce, eliminate

**Words we never use:** unlock value, drive transformation, synergy, best practices, low-hanging fruit, circle back, deep dive, game-changing, world-class, holistic, disrupt, empower, leverage, paradigm, optimize (when "improve" works)

**Use instead of:**

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| optimize operations | improve the line, reduce wait time |
| unlock value | find the waste, fix the sequence |
| drive transformation | change how the work gets done |
| best practices | what works, what we have seen work |
| low-hanging fruit | the obvious fix, the first thing to change |
| stakeholder alignment | getting the team on the same page |
| deliverables | the report, the plan, the recommendations |
| deep dive | close look, detailed review |

## Example Rewrites

**Before (consulting voice):**
> Our holistic approach to operational transformation leverages best-in-class methodologies to unlock sustainable value across your manufacturing ecosystem, driving measurable improvements in throughput and yield.

**After (David's voice):**
> We spend the first week on your floor — watching the line, timing the runs, talking to the operators who know where the problems are. Then we fix the sequence. Most clients see measurable improvements in throughput within the first 30 days.

**Before (consulting voice):**
> We recommend implementing a cross-functional training initiative to optimize resource allocation and mitigate bottleneck risk across production workstreams.

**After (David's voice):**
> Your QC station is a bottleneck because only two people can run it. Cross-train three more. The investment is two weeks of training. The return is eliminating the 18-minute average wait that is costing you 6 hours of idle time per shift.

**Before (consulting voice):**
> Through our proprietary assessment framework, we identified significant opportunities for process improvement and cost reduction.

**After (David's voice):**
> We walked the floor for three days and found $340K in annual waste — most of it in one sequence that nobody had questioned in four years.
