# Example: Creative Agency Voice Guide

Full interview and output for Studio Plural, a 12-person design agency specializing in brand identity and digital product design for consumer startups.

---

## Interview Transcript (Abbreviated)

**Q1: Audience**
"Startup founders in the consumer space — mostly Series A, sometimes seed. They are building the next great DTC brand or consumer app and they need an identity that makes people feel something. They are smart, design-literate, and they can tell the difference between good work and great work. They have probably looked at 20 agency portfolios this week."

*Acknowledgment: Design-literate founders who are comparison-shopping. They have seen a lot of agency websites that all sound the same. Your voice needs to stand out by being more interesting than the competition — not louder, more interesting. And because they understand design, you can speak at a higher level without explaining the basics.*

**Q2: Three Words**
"Sharp. Curious. Intentional."

*Acknowledgment: Sharp is about precision — every word, every design choice has a reason. Curious is about not having all the answers, but asking better questions. Intentional ties them together — nothing is accidental. This voice does not ramble. It does not throw things at the wall. Every sentence is a design decision.*

**Q3: Admired Writing**
"Pentagram's case studies. Also the way Apple writes product copy — deceptively simple. And Virgil Abloh's approach of putting everything in quotation marks to make you reconsider the word. Not that we would do that, but the principle of making familiar things feel new."

*Acknowledgment: High craft, high intentionality. Pentagram is confident and sparse. Apple is precise and rhythmic. Abloh is conceptual and provocative. The thread is that all three make you slow down and notice the language, not just consume it. Your writing should reward attention.*

**Q4: Cringe Writing**
"'We are a full-service creative agency passionate about crafting beautiful digital experiences.' Every agency says this. It means nothing. Also hate: 'pixel-perfect,' 'bespoke solutions,' 'we bring your vision to life.' If your copy could describe any agency, it should not describe any agency."

*Acknowledgment: The ultimate cringe test: could anyone else say this? If yes, it is banned. Your voice needs to be specific enough that someone could read a paragraph and know it is Studio Plural before seeing the logo. That means: specific projects, specific opinions, specific language.*

**Q5: Writing Sample**
Provided an intro from a case study:
> "Maison started as a spreadsheet. A founder tracking 40 candle scents in Google Sheets, shipping from her kitchen, and guessing which packaging would not collapse in transit. By the time she found us, the product was great and the brand was invisible. We gave Maison an identity that matched the quality of what was inside the box — warm, tactile, and specific enough that you could recognize it from across a shelf."

*Acknowledgment: Beautiful. This tells a story in 70 words. It starts with the founder's reality (a spreadsheet), moves through the problem (brand was invisible), and lands on the outcome (recognizable from across a shelf). The language is sensory — "warm, tactile, specific enough." You use details that make the work concrete. And "the brand was invisible" is a perfect diagnosis. Specific, honest, visual.*

**Q6: Formality Scale**
"4. We want to feel like a studio, not a firm. But we are not sloppy — everything is considered."

*Acknowledgment: Studio energy, not corporate energy. Your formality is low but your craft is high — casual register, precise word choice. Contractions are fine. Fragments are fine. But every sentence is deliberate. The casualness is a design choice, not laziness.*

**Q7: Vocabulary Rules**
"We say 'identity' not 'branding.' We say 'work' not 'projects.' We say 'studio' not 'agency.' We never say 'bespoke,' 'synergy,' 'deliverables,' 'assets,' or 'content.' We call it 'the work' — always. We use design language naturally: typography, color system, visual language, hierarchy, negative space."

*Acknowledgment: These vocabulary rules are identity statements. "Identity not branding" is a whole philosophy. "The work" as a term of respect — you do not reduce it to deliverables or assets. And banning "content" — because you make things with intention, not content to fill a calendar. The design language is allowed because your audience speaks it fluently.*

**Q8: Desired Feeling**
"I want them to think: 'These people see things I do not see.' Like they are going to uncover something about their brand that they knew was there but could not articulate."

*Acknowledgment: Revelation. You want the reader to feel like you have a sharper eye than they do — not arrogantly, but in a way that makes them want to work with you. The feeling is: "If they can see this in two paragraphs, imagine what they will see in a project." That means your writing has to demonstrate perception, not just claim it.*

## Generated Voice Guide

# Studio Plural — Voice Guide

## Voice Summary

Studio Plural sounds like the colleague who notices things everyone else walks past — and then explains what they noticed in a way that makes you see it too. The voice is precise, perceptive, and deliberately understated. We do not shout about our work. We describe it in a way that makes you lean in.

---

## Dimension Scores

| Dimension | Score | What This Means in Practice |
|---|---|---|
| Formality | 4 | Casual register, high craft. Contractions and fragments are fine. Sentence structure is varied and intentional. Nothing sloppy, nothing stiff. |
| Humor | 3 | Wit, not comedy. Humor comes from unexpected observations or precise word choice. Never puns, never self-deprecating jokes, never forced. |
| Jargon | 5 | Design vocabulary used naturally (typography, hierarchy, color system, visual language). Consulting and marketing jargon banned. The reader is design-literate. |
| Sentence Length | 5 | Mixed and rhythmic. Short sentences create emphasis. Longer sentences create flow. The pattern alternates deliberately — like typographic hierarchy. |
| Directness | 7 | Get to the point, but through a specific observation rather than a blunt statement. "The brand was invisible" is direct. It is also a diagnosis. |
| Warmth | 5 | Warm enough to be human, restrained enough to be confident. We care about our clients and show it by taking their work seriously, not by using exclamation points. |
| Authority | 7 | Confident through perception, not credentials. We do not say "we have 12 years of experience." We describe what we see and let the insight speak. |
| Energy | 5 | Measured. Neither calm nor intense — focused. Every sentence has purpose. Energy comes from specificity and surprise, not from volume or pace. |

---

## Tone Rules

1. **Start with the specific, not the general.** "Maison started as a spreadsheet" not "We helped a DTC brand build their identity." The specific detail pulls the reader in.
2. **Describe the problem with precision.** "The brand was invisible" is better than "They needed branding help." A precise diagnosis proves you understand the work.
3. **Use sensory language for design work.** "Warm, tactile, specific enough that you could recognize it from across a shelf" — this is how you write about visual work with words.
4. **Never describe what every agency can describe.** If the sentence works with any studio name swapped in, rewrite it.
5. **Let the work carry the confidence.** Do not claim you are great. Describe the work in a way that makes greatness obvious.
6. **Short case studies, deep insight.** 100 words that reveal something is worth more than 500 words that describe everything.

## Vocabulary

**Words we use:** identity, the work, studio, visual language, typography, color system, hierarchy, negative space, intentional, considered, specific, sharp, craft

**Words we never use:** branding, content, assets, deliverables, bespoke, synergy, pixel-perfect, full-service, passionate about, craft beautiful experiences, bring your vision to life, storytelling (unless we mean literal narrative), creative solutions

**Use instead of:**

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|

## Example Rewrites

**Before (generic agency):**
> We are a full-service creative agency passionate about crafting beautiful digital experiences for forward-thinking brands. Our team of designers, strategists, and developers work collaboratively to bring your vision to life.

**After (Studio Plural voice):**
> We design identities for consumer brands that people recognize before they read the name. Twelve people. One focus. We have opinions about typography, color, and how a brand should feel in someone's hand.

**Before (generic agency):**
> Our strategic approach to branding ensures that every touchpoint delivers a cohesive and impactful brand experience that resonates with your target audience.

**After (Studio Plural voice):**
> An identity is not a logo and a color palette. It is the system that makes every surface — packaging, product, screen, shelf — feel like it belongs to the same idea. We build that system.

**Before (generic agency):**
> We partnered with the client to create a bespoke brand identity that tells their unique story and positions them for growth in a competitive market.

**After (Studio Plural voice):**
> The product was remarkable. The packaging looked like every other jar on the shelf. We gave it an identity as specific as the thing inside — and the first retail buyer who saw the new packaging ordered three times their usual volume.

## Channel Adaptations

**Case studies:** Lead with a concrete detail about the client's world. Diagnose the problem in one sentence. Show the outcome with a sensory detail or measurable result. 80-150 words.

**Instagram:** One image, one sentence. The sentence should make the viewer see something in the image they would not have noticed alone. Never use hashtags beyond the brand name.

**Email to prospects:** Short. Three paragraphs max. Reference their specific work. State one observation about their current identity that demonstrates perception. Close with curiosity, not a pitch.

**Website copy:** Every sentence is a design decision. If a sentence does not earn its space, cut it. Negative space in writing mirrors negative space in design.
