# Example: Tech Founder Voice Guide

Full interview and output for Maya Chen, founder of TaskPilot (a project management SaaS for small agencies).

---

## Interview Transcript (Abbreviated)

**Q1: Audience**
"Small agency owners — 5 to 30 people. They have tried Asana, Monday, Basecamp, and they are drowning in tools. They just want one place to manage projects without a PhD in project management software. Most of them are creative people who hate process but know they need it."

*Acknowledgment: Your reader is a creative agency owner who resents complexity. They need process but do not want to feel like they are "doing project management." That means your voice has to feel effortless — no feature-heavy language, no productivity jargon.*

**Q2: Three Words**
"Clear. Opinionated. Helpful."

*Acknowledgment: Clear says you value simplicity over cleverness. Opinionated says you are not afraid to have a point of view about how work should get done — you are not trying to be everything to everyone. Helpful keeps it grounded. This is not a brand that lectures. It is a brand that solves.*

**Q3: Admired Writing**
"Basecamp's blog. They are not afraid to say 'this is the right way to do it.' Also Superhuman's product copy — every sentence earns its place."

*Acknowledgment: High directness from Basecamp, high craft from Superhuman. You want to be opinionated and precise. No filler.*

**Q4: Cringe Writing**
"Anything that says 'powerful yet intuitive' or 'enterprise-grade solution.' Also hate when tools say 'delight your customers.' Nobody is delighted by software. They are relieved it works."

*Acknowledgment: Strong anti-corporate, anti-hype instinct. Your banned-words list is going to be robust. And that line about 'relieved it works' — that is your voice right there. Honest, grounded, a little dry.*

**Q5: Writing Sample**
Provided a product update email:
> "We added recurring tasks this week. Not because it was on our roadmap — because three of you emailed us about it in the same week. So we built it. It works like you would expect: set a frequency, pick a day, and forget about it. No 17-step wizard. If you have feedback, reply to this email. We read every one."

*Acknowledgment: This is excellent. Short sentences — average about 10 words. Direct address. "We read every one" is the brand in four words: accessible, honest, personal. You use concrete details (17-step wizard) to make abstract claims (simplicity) tangible. That is a signature move.*

**Q6: Formality Scale**
"About a 3. Maybe 4 for investor updates."

*Acknowledgment: Your writing sample confirms the 3. Contractions, fragments, starting sentences with conjunctions. You flex to 4-5 when the stakes are higher, but your baseline is casual and direct.*

**Q7: Vocabulary Rules**
"Love: 'works,' 'built,' 'simple,' 'just.' Hate: 'solution,' 'platform,' 'leverage,' 'ecosystem,' 'stakeholders.' We are a tool, not a platform."

*Acknowledgment: "We are a tool, not a platform" — that is a founding principle disguised as a vocabulary rule. Your word list is going to be very concrete: build words, not business words.*

**Q8: Desired Feeling**
"I want them to feel like they just found the thing they have been looking for. Relief. Like 'finally, someone gets it.'"

*Acknowledgment: Relief is your north star. Not excitement, not inspiration — relief. That means every piece of content should reduce anxiety, not create it. Simple beats clever. Clear beats comprehensive.*

## Generated Voice Guide

# TaskPilot Voice Guide

## Voice Summary

TaskPilot sounds like the smartest person on your team who also happens to be the most straightforward. We are opinionated about how work should get done, but we never lecture — we show. Our voice is direct, grounded, and quietly confident. We earn trust by being useful, not by being loud.

---

## Dimension Scores

| Dimension | Score | What This Means in Practice |
|---|---|---|
| Formality | 3 | Contractions always. Fragments welcome. Start sentences with "And" or "But" freely. |
| Humor | 4 | Dry wit, not jokes. Humor comes from honesty and specificity, never from trying to be funny. |
| Jargon | 3 | Plain language. If an agency owner who studied graphic design would not know the term, do not use it. |
| Sentence Length | 3 | Short. Average 8-12 words. One idea per sentence. Let whitespace do the work. |
| Directness | 8 | Lead with the point. No "we are excited to announce." Just say what changed and why it matters. |
| Warmth | 6 | Warm but not gushy. We care about our users and show it through action (building what they ask for), not language (exclamation points). |
| Authority | 7 | We have opinions about how work should get done. We state them confidently. We do not hedge with "might" or "could." |
| Energy | 5 | Enthusiastic when it is earned. A big launch gets energy. A bug fix does not. Default is calm and steady. |

---

## Tone Rules

1. **Lead with what changed, not how you feel about it.** "We added recurring tasks" not "We are thrilled to announce recurring tasks."
2. **Use concrete details instead of adjectives.** "Set a frequency, pick a day, forget about it" not "our intuitive recurring task feature."
3. **One idea per sentence. One topic per paragraph.** If a paragraph has more than 4 sentences, split it.
4. **Write like you are emailing one person, not broadcasting to a list.** "You" not "our users." "Reply to this email" not "reach out to our support team."
5. **Have an opinion.** "This is the right way to do it" is more useful than "here are five options."
6. **Show, do not claim.** Never say "simple" — demonstrate simplicity. Never say "powerful" — show what it can do.

---

## Vocabulary

**Words we use:** works, built, simple, just, tool, fix, ship, clear, fast, try, real, honest

**Words we never use:** solution, platform, leverage, ecosystem, stakeholders, enterprise-grade, powerful yet intuitive, delight, empower, holistic, robust, cutting-edge, seamless

**Use instead of:**

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| solution | tool |
| platform | app, product |
| stakeholders | your team, the people involved |
| leverage | use |
| utilize | use |
| facilitate | run, help with |
| seamless | smooth, easy |
| robust | solid, reliable |
| we are excited to announce | we built, we shipped, we added |

## Example Rewrites

**Before (generic):**
> We are thrilled to announce our powerful new integration with Slack, designed to streamline your team's communication and enhance productivity across your organization.

**After (TaskPilot voice):**
> TaskPilot now works with Slack. When a task is due, your team gets a ping in the channel where they already work. No tab-switching. No missed deadlines.

**Before (generic):**
> Our intuitive onboarding experience helps new users get up and running quickly with our comprehensive feature set.

**After (TaskPilot voice):**
> Sign up, create a project, invite your team. Takes about 4 minutes. No training videos required.

**Before (generic):**
> We leverage cutting-edge technology to deliver an enterprise-grade solution that scales with your business needs.

**After (TaskPilot voice):**
> It is fast, it is reliable, and it works the same whether you have 5 projects or 500.
