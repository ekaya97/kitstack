# Tone Spectrum Examples

Five voice archetypes that cover the most common positions on the tone spectrum. These are not templates to copy — they are reference points to help calibrate where a brand sits relative to familiar examples.

---

## Archetype 1: The Friendly Expert

**Who uses this:** SaaS companies, online educators, B2B tools that want to feel accessible. Think Notion, Stripe documentation, ConvertKit.

**Dimension scores:**
| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Formality | 4 |
| Humor | 4 |
| Jargon | 5 |
| Sentence Length | 5 |
| Directness | 7 |
| Warmth | 7 |
| Authority | 6 |
| Energy | 5 |

**Sample paragraph:**
> Your landing page is not converting because you are asking visitors to make a decision before you have given them a reason to care. Lead with the outcome they want, not the features you built. Show them the "after" state. Then — and only then — explain how you get them there. We have seen this single change lift conversion rates by 15-30% across dozens of client projects.

**Why it works:** This voice teaches without condescending. It names the problem directly (high directness), backs it up with experience (moderate authority), and uses "you" constantly (high warmth). The sentence rhythm alternates between short and medium. Jargon is present but accessible.

## Archetype 2: The Straight Shooter

**Who uses this:** Consultants, agencies that sell on expertise, founders with strong opinions. Think 37signals/Basecamp, Seth Godin, Sahil Lavingia.

**Dimension scores:**
| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Formality | 3 |
| Humor | 3 |
| Jargon | 3 |
| Sentence Length | 3 |
| Directness | 9 |
| Warmth | 4 |
| Authority | 8 |
| Energy | 7 |

**Sample paragraph:**
> Stop building features nobody asked for. Talk to five customers this week. Not surveys. Conversations. Ask them what they did right before they opened your app and what they did right after. That sequence is your product. Everything else is inventory.

**Why it works:** Short sentences. Commands. No hedging. This voice has extremely high directness and authority — it tells you what to do without apologizing. The low warmth is deliberate: respect is shown through bluntness, not softness. Humor is dry and rare. Energy is high but controlled — urgency without hype.

## Archetype 3: The Thought Leader

**Who uses this:** Management consultancies, strategy firms, senior executives, research-driven brands. Think McKinsey Quarterly, Harvard Business Review, Lenny Rachitsky.

**Dimension scores:**
| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Formality | 7 |
| Humor | 2 |
| Jargon | 6 |
| Sentence Length | 7 |
| Directness | 5 |
| Warmth | 3 |
| Authority | 9 |
| Energy | 4 |

**Sample paragraph:**
> The organizations that will thrive in the next decade are not those with the best technology or the most capital. They are the ones that have developed a systematic approach to learning from failure at the institutional level. Our research across 340 mid-market companies reveals a consistent pattern: the top quartile in growth had failure-review processes embedded in their operating rhythm, while bottom-quartile performers treated post-mortems as occasional exercises triggered by crisis.

**Why it works:** Longer sentences with layered ideas. Data-backed claims (340 companies). Third person and institutional voice. The authority is near-maximum — this voice does not invite debate, it presents findings. Warmth is low by design; the reader is here for insight, not connection. Formality is high but avoids being stiff through concrete examples.

## Archetype 4: The Conversationalist

**Who uses this:** Personal brands, newsletters, coaches, community-driven businesses. Think Ann Handley, Paul Jarvis, creators on Substack.

**Dimension scores:**
| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Formality | 2 |
| Humor | 6 |
| Jargon | 2 |
| Sentence Length | 4 |
| Directness | 6 |
| Warmth | 9 |
| Authority | 4 |
| Energy | 6 |

**Sample paragraph:**
> I rewrote my About page last Tuesday. For the fourth time this year. And you know what? The version I landed on is basically what I would say if you asked me at a coffee shop what I do. Turns out the "professional" version I kept trying to write was the problem. Not the solution. If your About page makes you cringe when you read it out loud, start over and just... talk.

**Why it works:** This reads like someone talking to you. First person. Fragments. Self-deprecation. The warmth is near-maximum — you feel like you know this person. Authority is low by choice; the writer positions as a peer going through the same struggle. The humor is natural and self-directed. Formality is very low — contractions, ellipses, casual conjunctions.

## Archetype 5: The Technical Authority

**Who uses this:** Developer tools, engineering blogs, technical documentation. Think Vercel, Cloudflare blog, Julia Evans.

**Dimension scores:**
| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Formality | 5 |
| Humor | 3 |
| Jargon | 8 |
| Sentence Length | 5 |
| Directness | 8 |
| Warmth | 4 |
| Authority | 7 |
| Energy | 5 |

**Sample paragraph:**
> Edge functions execute at the CDN layer, which means your API response is generated within 50ms of the user, not 200ms from a centralized server. The tradeoff is a constrained runtime — no filesystem access, limited memory, cold starts on first invocation per region. For read-heavy endpoints that serve personalized content, the latency improvement outweighs the constraints. For write operations that need transaction guarantees, stick with your origin server.

**Why it works:** High jargon used with precision, not for show. Each technical term earns its place. Directness is high — the tradeoff is stated plainly, with a clear recommendation. Authority comes from specificity (50ms, 200ms) rather than credentials. Warmth is low but not cold — the "stick with" at the end is colloquial and human. The voice trusts the reader to be technical and respects that by not over-explaining.

## How to Use These Archetypes

1. **During the interview:** If the user is struggling to articulate their voice, read them two contrasting samples and ask which feels closer.
2. **After scoring:** Compare the user's dimension profile to these five archetypes. Name the closest match and note where they diverge. "Your voice is closest to the Friendly Expert, but with more humor and less authority — more like a Friendly Expert who is also a Conversationalist."
3. **For calibration:** When a dimension score feels ambiguous, refer to these samples. If the user's formality is a 4, their writing should sound closer to the Conversationalist samples than the Thought Leader samples.
4. **For team alignment:** Share the relevant archetype with team members as a starting point. "We sound like The Straight Shooter with 20% more warmth" is faster to internalize than a list of abstract rules.
