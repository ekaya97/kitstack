# Vocabulary Frameworks

Word choice is where voice becomes tangible. Writers can internalize tone and formality gradually, but vocabulary rules are immediately actionable — a writer can open the word list on day one and produce on-brand content.

---

## The Core Framework: Use / Never Use / Use Instead

Every voice guide includes three vocabulary lists:

### Words We Use
Words that carry the brand's values and personality. These are not mandatory in every piece — they are the vocabulary that feels "right" when it appears.

**How to build this list:**
- Pull recurring words from the user's writing samples
- Include words that map to their desired feeling (Question 8)
- Add words from admired brands (Question 3) that fit
- Aim for 10-20 words, organized by category

**Example categories:**
- **Action words:** build, ship, launch, test, try, explore
- **Values words:** clear, honest, straightforward, simple, real
- **Relationship words:** together, team, partner, you, we
- **Outcome words:** results, growth, impact, progress, momentum

### Words We Never Use
Words that violate the brand's voice. These are hard rules — if a writer uses one, it should be flagged in review.

**Common categories of banned words:**
- **Corporate filler:** leverage, synergy, utilize, streamline, optimize, best-in-class, cutting-edge, holistic, robust
- **Empty superlatives:** incredible, amazing, game-changing, revolutionary, world-class (unless the brand is deliberately high-energy)
- **Passive hedging:** it should be noted that, in order to, at the end of the day, when all is said and done
- **Condescension:** simply, just, obviously, as everyone knows, of course
- **Hype language:** disrupt, transform, reimagine, empower (unless the brand genuinely operates in these spaces)

### Use Instead Of (Substitution Table)
The most practical part of the vocabulary guide. Shows writers exactly what to swap.

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| utilize | use |
| leverage | use, build on |
| facilitate | help, run, lead |
| implement | build, set up, start |
| optimize | improve, fix, tune |
| stakeholders | team, people involved, decision-makers |
| deliverables | what we will make, the work |
| circle back | follow up, revisit |
| deep dive | close look, detailed review |
| best-in-class | top-performing, the best we have seen |

## Jargon Policy

Jargon is not inherently bad. It is a tool. The policy defines when and how to use it.

### Three Jargon Postures

**Posture 1: Jargon-Free (Dimension score 1-3)**
- No term that requires industry knowledge to understand
- If a technical term is unavoidable, define it immediately: "DNS records (the settings that tell the internet where your website lives)"
- Analogies replace technical concepts wherever possible
- Test: would your non-technical friend understand this sentence?

**Posture 2: Jargon-Aware (Dimension score 4-6)**
- Common industry terms are used freely (e.g., "conversion rate," "SEO," "API")
- Specialized terms are defined on first use
- Acronyms are expanded on first use per piece, then abbreviated after
- Test: would someone 1-2 years into the industry understand this?

**Posture 3: Jargon-Native (Dimension score 7-10)**
- Technical terminology is expected and valued
- Defining common terms would feel condescending
- Precision matters more than accessibility
- Test: would this pass peer review in the industry?

## Emoji Policy

Emoji use is a voice decision, not a personal preference. Define it explicitly.

### Four Emoji Postures

**No Emoji (Formality 7+):**
Emoji never appear in any channel. Period. Not even in internal Slack messages that become public.

**Functional Emoji Only (Formality 5-6):**
Emoji used as visual markers in lists, status updates, and headers. Never in running prose.
- Acceptable: checkmarks, arrows, warning signs in documentation
- Never: smiley faces, reactions, expressive emoji in body text

**Light Emoji (Formality 3-4):**
Emoji appear occasionally in casual channels (social media, chat, internal comms). Never more than 1-2 per piece. Never in headlines or first sentences.

**Emoji-Forward (Formality 1-2):**
Emoji are part of the voice. They appear in subject lines, social posts, and even product copy. They add personality and break up text. Still: never more than 3 per paragraph, and always with purpose.

## Formatting Conventions

Voice is expressed through formatting choices too. Define these:

### Capitalization
- **Sentence case everywhere** (most modern brands): "How to write better emails"
- **Title case for headings** (more traditional): "How to Write Better Emails"
- **All caps for emphasis** (high energy): only if the brand is above 7 on energy. Use sparingly.

### Punctuation Personality
- **Oxford comma:** Yes or no. Pick one. Be consistent.
- **Em dashes:** Casual brands love them. Formal brands prefer semicolons or parentheses.
- **Ellipses:** Very casual or very dramatic. Most brands should avoid them.
- **Exclamation points:** Define a maximum per piece. One per email is a common rule. Zero is also valid.

### Paragraph and Sentence Rules
- **Maximum paragraph length:** 3-4 sentences for most digital content. 1-2 for high-energy brands.
- **One-sentence paragraphs:** Powerful for emphasis. Overuse dilutes the effect.
- **Lists vs. prose:** High-directness brands favor lists. High-warmth brands favor prose. Most brands use both.

### Person and Perspective
- **First person singular ("I"):** Personal brands, solo founders, thought leaders
- **First person plural ("we"):** Companies, teams, agencies
- **Second person ("you"):** Almost always. The reader should feel addressed directly.
- **Third person ("the company," "users"):** Formal documentation, legal, enterprise

## Building the Vocabulary Section

During the interview, vocabulary clues emerge from every answer:

| Interview Question | Vocabulary Signals |
|---|---|
| Audience | Jargon tolerance, complexity ceiling |
| Three Words | Core "words we use" candidates |
| Admired Writing | Style words to borrow or adapt |
| Cringe Writing | "Words we never use" list |
| Writing Sample | Natural vocabulary baseline |
| Formality Scale | Register and punctuation rules |
| Vocabulary Rules | Direct input for all three lists |
| Desired Feeling | Emotional vocabulary targets |

Compile signals from all 8 questions. The explicit vocabulary rules from Question 7 are the foundation, but every question contributes.
