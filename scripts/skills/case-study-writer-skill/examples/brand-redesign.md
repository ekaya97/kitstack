## The Challenge

The brand was three years old, profitable, and stuck. Their products were premium — formulated with high-quality natural ingredients, priced between €35-€85 per item, and earning strong customer reviews. But their visual identity told a different story.

The logo was a generic serif font with a leaf icon. The packaging was white with green accents — clean, but indistinguishable from dozens of mass-market natural skincare brands. The Shopify store was functional but forgettable: stock photography, crowded product pages, and a color palette that screamed "health food store" rather than "luxury self-care."

The mismatch between product quality and brand perception was costing them in three ways:

1. **Pricing resistance.** Customers questioned why the products cost 3-4x more than what the brand looked like. The visual identity undermined the premium positioning.
2. **Media invisibility.** Beauty editors and publications consistently passed on featuring them. As the founder put it: "They'd look at our website, see a brand that looked like a startup, and move on."
3. **Gift market exclusion.** Skincare is a major gift category. But nobody wraps a product that looks like a pharmacy brand. They were absent from the gift guides and holiday roundups that drive Q4 revenue.

The founder knew the brand needed to look like what it already was: a premium, carefully formulated skincare line worth the investment.

## The Approach

We started by reframing the project. This wasn't about making things "look nicer." It was about aligning the brand's visual identity with its market position — and using design as a strategic lever for revenue.

### Discovery (Weeks 1-2)

We conducted a brand audit that included:

- **Customer perception survey** (200+ responses): Customers loved the products but described the brand as "clinical," "basic," and "nothing special visually." When asked what premium skincare brands they admired, they named Aesop, Byredo, and Drunk Elephant — brands where the packaging is part of the experience.
- **Competitive visual analysis:** We mapped 15 competitors on a spectrum from mass-market to luxury, identifying the visual gap the brand needed to fill. Their current identity sat firmly in the "natural/mass" quadrant. Their price point and product quality belonged in the "modern luxury" quadrant.
- **Shelf test:** We printed the existing packaging at scale and placed it next to competitor products. The results were immediate and uncomfortable — the brand visually disappeared.

### Brand Identity Design (Weeks 3-6)

Based on the discovery findings, we developed a new visual identity anchored in three principles: **restrained luxury, textural richness, and quiet confidence.**

- **Logo:** Replaced the generic serif + leaf with a custom wordmark — a refined, slightly condensed sans-serif with subtle ligatures that feel modern and premium without being cold. No icon. The name is the mark.
- **Color palette:** Moved from white-and-green to a palette of warm ivory, deep terracotta, and matte black with gold accents. The palette communicates warmth and sophistication — natural without being "granola."
- **Typography:** A paired system: the custom wordmark for the brand name, a clean geometric sans for body text, and an elegant serif for accent headlines. Three typefaces, strict hierarchy.
- **Packaging:** Complete redesign of all product packaging. Heavy-stock matte boxes, embossed logo, terracotta and ivory color blocking that varies by product line. The packaging now feels like something you'd display on a bathroom shelf, not hide in a drawer.

### Shopify Redesign (Weeks 6-9)

The store was rebuilt from the ground up with a focus on communicating premium positioning:

- **Photography direction:** We art-directed a new product shoot. No more stock images. Products photographed on natural stone surfaces with warm lighting. Lifestyle shots that showed the products in curated spaces — marble countertops, linen towels, natural light.
- **Product pages:** Simplified from cluttered information dumps to focused, breathing layouts. Ingredient stories told visually. Large product photography with zoom functionality. Trust signals (ingredient sourcing, certifications) presented as elegant icons rather than text blocks.
- **Homepage:** A full-width hero with a single product, minimal text, and a strong editorial feel. The homepage reads like a magazine spread, not a catalog page.
- **Checkout:** Streamlined from 4 steps to 2. Added gift wrapping and personalized note options — directly addressing the gift market gap.

### Brand Guidelines (Week 10)

We delivered a 40-page brand guidelines document covering logo usage, typography, color specifications, photography style, tone of voice, social media templates, and packaging specifications. This ensures consistency as the brand grows and works with other agencies or freelancers.

## The Results

The rebrand launched simultaneously across packaging, website, and social channels. Within the first four months, the business impact was measurable.

### Results at a Glance

| Metric | Before | After (4 months) | Change |
|--------|--------|-------------------|--------|
| Average order value | €62 | €76 | +22% (+€14) |
| Product return rate | 8.4% | 5.1% | -39% |
| Time on site | 2:10 | 3:45 | +74% |
| Social media engagement rate | 1.8% | 4.2% | +133% |
| Press features | 0 (12-month period) | 2 major publications | — |

**The 22% AOV increase** was the headline, but the driver was revealing. Customers weren't buying more expensive products — they were buying more products per order. The new packaging and unboxing experience made multi-product gifting (and self-gifting) feel premium and intentional. The addition of gift wrapping at checkout contributed to a 35% increase in orders with 2+ items.

**The return rate drop** reflected a shift in customer expectations. With the old brand, customers sometimes felt the product didn't justify the premium price — the packaging set the wrong expectation. The new identity sets the right one. When you open a box that feels like €75, you're less likely to question the product inside.

**The press features** were perhaps the most strategically significant result. Two major German beauty publications featured the brand within three months of relaunch — both citing the "stunning new packaging" and "premium identity" as reasons for coverage. This earned media exposure would have cost an estimated €15,000-€25,000 in equivalent advertising.

> "The moment I saw the new packaging, I knew it was right. For the first time, our brand looks like it costs what it costs. The editorial features alone have been worth the entire investment — but what really changed is how customers interact with us. They feel like they're buying something special. Because they are."
> — **Founder, DTC Skincare Brand (Hamburg)**
