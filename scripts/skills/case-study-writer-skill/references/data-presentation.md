# Data Presentation in Case Studies

This document covers how to present metrics, results, and quantitative data in case studies for maximum impact and credibility.

---

## The Dual Number Rule

Never present a single format. Always pair:
- **Percentage + Absolute:** "Revenue increased by 23% ($180,000)"
- **Rate + Volume:** "Conversion rate improved from 2.1% to 3.4%, adding 450 new customers per month"
- **Metric + Context:** "Page load time dropped to 1.9 seconds (industry average: 3.2 seconds)"

Percentages alone can mislead (a 200% increase from 1 to 3 isn't impressive). Absolutes alone lack proportionality ($10,000 is different at a $100K company vs. a $10M company). Use both.

---

## Before/After Comparisons

The most powerful way to present results is a direct before/after comparison. The reader's brain calculates the improvement automatically.

### Table Format (Best for 3-5 Metrics)

| Metric | Before | After | Improvement |
|--------|--------|-------|-------------|
| Monthly revenue | €340,000 | €520,000 | +53% (+€180,000) |
| Conversion rate | 1.8% | 3.4% | +89% |
| Customer acquisition cost | €145 | €87 | -40% (-€58) |
| Average order value | €89 | €112 | +26% (+€23) |
| Support tickets/week | 340 | 95 | -72% |

### Inline Format (Best for 1-2 Key Metrics)

> Monthly revenue went from €340,000 to €520,000 — a 53% increase representing €180,000 in additional monthly revenue.

### Visual Suggestions

When suggesting visuals for case studies:
- **Bar charts:** Best for comparing before/after on 2-4 metrics
- **Line charts:** Best for showing improvement over time (month-by-month progression)
- **Metric cards:** Large numbers with labels, great for landing pages ("53% Revenue Increase")
- **Progress bars:** Visual before/after, especially for percentage improvements

## Metric Hierarchy

Not all results are equal. Present them in order of impact:

### Tier 1: Business Outcomes (Lead with these)
- Revenue increase/decrease
- Cost reduction
- Profit margin improvement
- Customer lifetime value
- Market share change

These are the metrics that executives care about. Lead your results section with Tier 1 metrics.

### Tier 2: Performance Metrics
- Conversion rate improvement
- Traffic/engagement increase
- Customer acquisition cost reduction
- Time-to-market reduction
- Operational efficiency gains

These explain HOW the business outcome was achieved. Present after Tier 1.

### Tier 3: Activity Metrics
- Number of pages redesigned
- Tests run
- Features shipped
- Reports delivered
- Training sessions conducted

These are outputs, not outcomes. Include only if Tier 1 and Tier 2 data is thin. Never lead with activity metrics — "We redesigned 47 pages" tells the reader nothing about impact.

### Tier 4: Qualitative Outcomes
- Team satisfaction improvement
- Client feedback and quotes
- Process improvements
- Knowledge transfer
- Cultural changes

Include one or two qualitative outcomes to add a human dimension. "The marketing team reported spending 60% less time on manual reporting, freeing them to focus on strategy" adds warmth to the numbers.

## Making Numbers Compelling

### Contextualization Techniques

**Compare to industry benchmarks:**
> "Their new conversion rate of 3.4% now exceeds the e-commerce industry average of 2.5% — putting them in the top 25% of performers."

**Compare to the client's own history:**
> "This is the first quarter in three years where customer acquisition cost has been below €100."

**Project forward:**
> "At this trajectory, the improvements will generate an additional €2.1M in revenue over the next 12 months."

**Compare to the investment:**
> "The €45,000 engagement has already generated €180,000 in incremental revenue within 90 days — a 4x return."

**Use time anchors:**
> "Within 30 days of launch..." or "By the end of the first quarter..." — timeframes make results feel real and achievable.

### Rounding Rules

| Metric Type | Rounding Guidance | Example |
|-------------|-------------------|---------|
| Percentages | One decimal for small changes, whole numbers for large | 2.3% → 3.4% or "improved by 47%" |
| Revenue | Round to thousands or nearest significant figure | €523,400 → "over €520,000" |
| Costs | Be specific for smaller amounts, round for larger | €87/customer, "over €200,000 saved" |
| Time | Be specific for short durations, approximate for long | "8 minutes" (not "about 8 min"), "approximately 3 months" |
| Counts | Exact for small numbers, round for large | "12 new features," "over 500 new customers" |

### The "So What" Test

Every metric must pass the "so what" test. Present the metric, then immediately connect it to what it means for the business.

| Metric Alone (Weak) | Metric + So What (Strong) |
|---------------------|--------------------------|
| "Page load time decreased by 3.2 seconds" | "Page load time decreased by 3.2 seconds, which reduced bounce rate by 28% and directly contributed to a 15% increase in conversions" |
| "We shipped 12 new features" | "We shipped 12 new features in 8 weeks, including the payment integration that unlocked their European market — a segment now generating €45,000/month" |
| "NPS score improved by 15 points" | "NPS score improved from 32 to 47, crossing the threshold from 'passive' to 'promoter' territory for the first time in the company's history" |
| "Reduced meeting time by 40%" | "Reduced meeting time by 40%, giving the team back approximately 12 hours per week — time they redirected to customer-facing activities" |

## Handling Missing or Weak Data

Not every engagement produces dramatic metrics. Here's how to handle common data challenges:

### When You Don't Have Exact Numbers

**Use directional language with qualifiers:**
- "Approximately 30% improvement" (based on your best estimate)
- "The client reported a significant reduction in support tickets — from several hundred per week to under 100"
- "Revenue grew by an estimated €150,000-200,000 in the first year"

**Use proxy metrics:**
- Can't measure revenue impact directly? Use conversion rate, traffic, engagement, or leads as proxies
- Can't measure time saved? Estimate based on process changes: "Automating the report reduced a 4-hour weekly task to 15 minutes"

### When Results Are Modest

**Frame honestly but strategically:**
- A 12% improvement is still a real result. Don't apologize for it. "A 12% improvement in conversion rate represents approximately €36,000 in additional annual revenue."
- Focus on what the result enables: "The 12% improvement validated the approach, giving the client confidence to invest in a full-funnel optimization program."
- Emphasize the efficiency: "The 12% improvement was achieved within 4 weeks, with minimal disruption to the existing customer experience."

### When the Client Won't Share Numbers

**Use anonymized ranges:**
- "Revenue increased by 20-30%"
- "Cost savings in the six-figure range"
- Focus on qualitative outcomes and process improvements instead

**Offer an anonymized version:**
- "A leading B2B SaaS company" instead of the company name
- Disguise specific metrics while preserving the story arc
- See `references/client-approval-guide.md` for anonymization best practices

## Results Section Templates

### Template 1: Metrics-Led (Best for data-rich engagements)

```
## Results

Within [timeframe], the engagement delivered measurable improvements across [number] key metrics:

| Metric | Before | After | Change |
|--------|--------|-------|--------|
| [Primary metric] | [X] | [Y] | [+/-Z%] |
| [Secondary metric] | [X] | [Y] | [+/-Z%] |
| [Tertiary metric] | [X] | [Y] | [+/-Z%] |

[One paragraph connecting the metrics to business impact]
```

### Template 2: Narrative-Led (Best for consulting or strategy engagements)

```
## Results

[Opening sentence with the headline metric]

[Paragraph detailing the primary outcome and its business significance]

[Paragraph detailing secondary outcomes]

**Key outcomes:**
- [Metric 1 with context]
- [Metric 2 with context]
- [Metric 3 with context]

[Closing sentence about ongoing or future impact]
```

### Template 3: Before/After-Led (Best for visual transformations)

```
## The Transformation

### Before
[2-3 bullet points describing the before state with metrics]

### After
[2-3 bullet points describing the after state with metrics]

### The Impact
[1-2 paragraphs translating the transformation into business value]
```
