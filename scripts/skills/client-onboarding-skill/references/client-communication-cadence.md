# Client Communication Cadence Reference

This document provides detailed guidance on when, how, and why to communicate with clients during onboarding and beyond. Use it to populate communication-related tasks in the onboarding checklist.

---

## Communication Principles

1. **Proactive beats reactive.** Clients should never have to ask "What's happening?" If they do, you've already failed.
2. **Frequency decreases over time.** Heavy communication in Phase 1-3, then dial back as trust is established.
3. **Match the client's preferred channel.** Ask in the intake questionnaire. Some clients live in email; others in Slack; others only pick up the phone.
4. **Every message should have a purpose.** Status update, question, delivery, or decision request. Never "just checking in" with no substance.
5. **Assume your message is being forwarded.** Clients forward your emails to their bosses, partners, and teams. Write accordingly.

## Communication by Phase

### Phase 1: Welcome (Day 0-1)

| Communication | Channel | Timing | Purpose |
|--------------|---------|--------|---------|
| Welcome email | Email | Within 2 hours of signing | Set tone, share next steps, attach intake questionnaire |
| Intake questionnaire reminder | Email | 24 hours after first email (only if not completed) | Gentle nudge |
| Kickoff scheduling | Email or scheduling link | Within 24 hours | Lock in the meeting |
| Invoice / payment confirmation | Email | Within 24 hours | Administrative, keep it brief |

**Tone in Phase 1:** Warm, confident, organized. You're demonstrating that they hired someone who has their act together.

**Template — Welcome Email:**
```
Subject: [Project Name] — Welcome + Next Steps

Hi [Client Name],

Thrilled to officially kick things off on [project name]. Here's what happens next:

1. **Intake questionnaire** (attached / linked) — takes about 10 minutes. 
   Please complete by [date, 2-3 days out].
2. **Kickoff meeting** — I'd like to schedule this for [date range]. 
   [Scheduling link] or let me know what works.
3. **First deliverable** — you'll see [description] by [date].

If you have any questions before the kickoff, reply here or reach me at [phone/Slack].

Looking forward to this one.

[Your name]
```

### Phase 2: Setup (Day 1-3)

| Communication | Channel | Timing | Purpose |
|--------------|---------|--------|---------|
| Intake receipt confirmation | Email | Within 4 hours of receiving | Acknowledge, note any follow-up questions |
| Kickoff agenda | Email | 24 hours before the meeting | Help client prepare, show professionalism |
| Access/asset follow-up | Email | Day 2-3 (only if items are missing) | Polite reminder for outstanding items |

**Tone in Phase 2:** Efficient, professional, focused. You're in preparation mode — keep communication purposeful and minimal.

**Template — Kickoff Agenda Email:**
```
Subject: Kickoff Agenda — [Date, Time]

Hi [Client Name],

Looking forward to our kickoff on [day]. Here's what we'll cover:

1. Introductions and roles (5 min)
2. Project goals and success metrics (10 min)
3. Scope and timeline walkthrough (15 min)
4. Communication plan (5 min)
5. Next steps and first deliverable (10 min)
6. Your questions (10 min)

Total: ~45-60 minutes

If there's anything specific you'd like to discuss that's not listed, 
let me know and I'll add it.

See you [day].

[Your name]
```

### Phase 3: Kickoff (Day 3-5)

| Communication | Channel | Timing | Purpose |
|--------------|---------|--------|---------|
| Kickoff meeting | Video call (preferred) or phone | Scheduled | Align on everything |
| Kickoff summary | Email | Within 24 hours of meeting | Document decisions, confirm action items |
| Project access invitations | Email / tool notifications | Within 24 hours | Share PM board, folder access |

**Tone in Phase 3:** Collaborative, clear, action-oriented. The kickoff is about listening and confirming, not presenting.

**Template — Kickoff Summary:**
```
Subject: Kickoff Summary — [Project Name]

Hi [Client Name],

Great kickoff today. Here's a summary of what we aligned on:

**Project Goals:**
- [Goal 1]
- [Goal 2]
- [Goal 3]

**Key Decisions:**
- [Decision 1]
- [Decision 2]

**Action Items:**
- [ ] [Your name]: [Task] by [date]
- [ ] [Client name]: [Task] by [date]
- [ ] [Your name]: [Task] by [date]

**Next Touchpoint:** [Date, format]

**Timeline:** [Link to PM board or attached timeline]

If anything above doesn't match your understanding, let me know 
by end of day tomorrow. Otherwise, we're off to the races.

[Your name]
```

### Phase 4: First Delivery (Day 5-14)

| Communication | Channel | Timing | Purpose |
|--------------|---------|--------|---------|
| Progress update (optional) | Email or Slack | Midway (day 7-8) | Reduce silence anxiety for longer projects |
| First delivery | Email with link/attachment | Day 7-12 | The work product with context |
| Feedback nudge | Email | 48 hours after delivery (only if no response) | Don't let it sit in their inbox |
| Revision delivery | Email | Per agreed timeline | Address feedback with notes |

**Tone in Phase 4:** Confident, detailed, specific. Accompany every delivery with reasoning and clear asks.

**Template — First Delivery:**
```
Subject: [Deliverable Name] — Ready for Review

Hi [Client Name],

[Deliverable] is ready for your review: [link]

**What you're looking at:**
[Brief description of what was done and key decisions made]

**A few things to note:**
- [Design/strategic choice and why]
- [Anything that's placeholder or in-progress]
- [Any deviation from the original plan and why]

**I'd love your feedback on:**
1. [Specific question about direction/tone/approach]
2. [Specific question about content/accuracy]
3. [Specific question about anything that's missing]

Please share feedback by [date] and I'll have revisions back to you 
by [date].

[Your name]
```

### Phase 5: Check-In (Day 14-30)

| Communication | Channel | Timing | Purpose |
|--------------|---------|--------|---------|
| Check-in scheduling | Email | Day 14-20 | Propose the check-in meeting |
| Check-in meeting or async survey | Call or email | Day 20-30 | Process review |
| Check-in follow-up | Email | Within 48 hours | Confirm changes you'll make |

**Tone in Phase 5:** Reflective, appreciative, forward-looking. This is about the relationship, not just the project.

## Ongoing Communication (Post-Onboarding)

### By Engagement Type

**Project-Based (fixed scope):**
- Weekly status email or brief call (15-30 min)
- Delivery emails at each milestone
- Final delivery with handoff documentation
- Post-project review (optional but recommended)

**Retainer (ongoing monthly):**
- Monthly planning call (30-60 min) at the start of each month
- Weekly async update (email or PM tool comment)
- Monthly performance/progress report
- Quarterly strategy review (deeper, 60 min)

**Advisory/Coaching:**
- Session-based (weekly or biweekly calls)
- Brief async check-ins between sessions
- Monthly or quarterly goal review
- Annual engagement review

## When to Use Each Channel

### Email
- Formal deliveries and summaries
- Anything that needs to be referenced later
- Communication that may be forwarded to others
- Invoices, contracts, and administrative items
- When you need a paper trail

### Slack / Chat
- Quick questions that need fast answers
- Informal status updates
- Sharing links or quick assets
- Day-to-day collaboration
- When the question is simple and doesn't need a paper trail

### Video Call
- Kickoff meetings and check-ins
- Presenting deliverables that need explanation
- Difficult conversations or misalignments
- Relationship building (especially early in the engagement)
- When text-based communication is creating confusion

### Phone Call
- Urgent issues that can't wait for email
- Sensitive topics better handled with voice tone
- Quick alignment when Slack/chat isn't cutting it
- When the client prefers phone (some do)

## Response Time Expectations

Set these explicitly during the kickoff. Recommended defaults:

| Channel | Your Response Time | Client Response Time (suggested) |
|---------|-------------------|----------------------------------|
| Email | Within 24 hours (business days) | Within 48 hours |
| Slack / Chat | Within 2-4 hours (business hours) | Same day |
| Urgent (phone/text) | Within 1 hour | Within 1 hour |
| Deliverable feedback | N/A | Within 3-5 business days |

**Important:** Define "urgent" during the kickoff. Without a shared definition, everything becomes urgent (or nothing does).

---

## Escalation Framework

When something goes wrong, the communication cadence changes:

| Situation | Action | Timing |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Minor delay (1-2 days) | Email update with new timeline | As soon as you know |
| Major delay (1+ week) | Phone call, then email confirmation | Immediately |
| Scope issue discovered | Email with options and recommendations | Within 24 hours of discovery |
| Quality concern from client | Video call to discuss | Within 24 hours of receiving feedback |
| Budget overrun risk | Phone call with transparent numbers | Before the overrun happens |
| Relationship tension | Video call, focus on listening | Within 48 hours of sensing the issue |

**The golden rule of escalation:** Bad news does not get better with age. Communicate problems early, with a proposed solution. Clients can handle problems; they cannot handle surprises.
