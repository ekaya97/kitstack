# Onboarding Phases — Deep Reference

This document provides exhaustive detail on each of the 5 onboarding phases. Use it as a lookup when generating phase-specific tasks and when the user's situation requires nuanced guidance.

---

## Phase 1: Welcome (Day 0-1)

### Purpose
The welcome phase exists to reduce buyer's remorse and create forward momentum. The moment a client signs a contract, a psychological countdown begins. Every hour of silence after signing increases anxiety. The welcome phase interrupts that anxiety with confidence-building communication.

### Task Breakdown

| Task | Owner | Timing | Priority |
|------|-------|--------|----------|
| Send welcome email | You | Within 2 hours of signing | Critical |
| Send intake questionnaire | You | Attached to welcome email or within 4 hours | Critical |
| Request access credentials | You | Within welcome email or separate follow-up | High |
| Schedule kickoff meeting | You | Within 24 hours (propose 2-3 time slots) | Critical |
| Add client to billing system | You (internal) | Within 24 hours | High |
| Send first invoice (if upfront payment) | You | Within 24 hours | High |

### Welcome Email Components
1. **Opening line** — Reference the specific project, not a generic greeting
2. **What happens next** — 3-4 bullet points describing the next 5 days
3. **Intake link or questionnaire** — One clear ask, not five
4. **Kickoff meeting scheduling** — Propose specific times or include a scheduling link
5. **Your direct contact info** — Name, email, phone/Slack (whichever you prefer)
6. **Closing** — Express genuine enthusiasm for the specific project

### Intake Questionnaire Design
- Keep to 10-15 questions maximum for the initial intake
- Mix of closed (multiple choice) and open (short text) questions
- Group into sections: Business Context, Project Specifics, Preferences, Access/Logistics
- Include an estimated completion time ("This takes about 10 minutes")
- Mandatory fields: business goals, target audience, existing assets, key stakeholders
- Optional fields: competitor examples, inspiration, brand guidelines, historical data

### Common Mistakes
- Sending a welcome email 2 days later ("Sorry for the delay, things have been busy...")
- Asking for too much information upfront — the client just made a big decision, don't overwhelm them
- Using a template that still has another client's name in it
- Not providing a clear timeline of what happens next
- Sending the contract, invoice, questionnaire, and NDA all in one email

## Phase 2: Setup (Day 1-3)

### Purpose
Internal preparation that the client never sees. This is where you build the scaffolding that makes everything else look effortless. A well-executed setup phase means the kickoff runs smoothly and the first delivery arrives on time.

### Task Breakdown

| Task | Owner | Timing | Priority |
|------|-------|--------|----------|
| Create project folder/workspace | You | Day 1 | Critical |
| Set up PM board with milestones | You | Day 1-2 | Critical |
| Review intake questionnaire responses | You | Within 4 hours of receiving | Critical |
| Draft kickoff agenda | You | Day 2 | High |
| Send kickoff agenda to client | You | Day 2-3 (at least 24hrs before meeting) | High |
| Set up communication channel (Slack, etc.) | You | Day 1-2 | Medium |
| Configure billing/time tracking | You (internal) | Day 1 | Medium |
| Brief team members (if applicable) | You | Day 2 | High |
| Gather and organize client assets | You | Day 1-3 | Medium |
| Prepare first deliverable outline | You | Day 2-3 | Medium |

### Project Space Structure
Create a consistent structure for every engagement:
```
[Client Name] - [Project Name]
├── 00-Admin (contracts, invoices, proposals)
├── 01-Discovery (intake responses, research, briefs)
├── 02-Work-in-Progress (active deliverables)
├── 03-Delivered (final approved files)
├── 04-Assets (brand files, logos, photos from client)
└── 05-Communication (meeting notes, important emails)
```

### Kickoff Agenda Preparation
- Review every intake response and note follow-up questions
- Prepare a 1-page project brief summarizing your understanding
- Pre-populate the timeline in your PM tool
- Identify 2-3 "quick wins" you can demonstrate in the first week
- Prepare screen shares or visuals if the kickoff is remote

### Common Mistakes
- Not reviewing intake responses before the kickoff — the client can tell
- Creating the project space during the kickoff meeting
- Not sending the agenda in advance — clients want to prepare too
- Over-engineering the internal setup for small projects
- Forgetting to set up billing, then awkwardly invoicing weeks later

## Phase 3: Kickoff (Day 3-5)

### Purpose
The kickoff is the formal beginning of the working relationship. It's where alignment happens, expectations are set, and the human connection is made. A great kickoff creates momentum that carries through the first month. A bad one creates doubt that lingers forever.

### Task Breakdown

| Task | Owner | Timing | Priority |
|------|-------|--------|----------|
| Confirm kickoff time and attendees | You | Day before | Critical |
| Send agenda and any pre-read materials | You | 24 hours before | High |
| Run kickoff meeting | You (facilitate) | Scheduled time | Critical |
| Take meeting notes | You or team member | During meeting | Critical |
| Send kickoff summary within 24 hours | You | Day of or next day | Critical |
| Update timeline based on kickoff decisions | You | Within 48 hours | High |
| Create contact/stakeholder sheet | You | After kickoff | Medium |
| Grant client access to project space | You | After kickoff | Medium |

### Kickoff Meeting Facilitation Tips
- Start with a human moment — ask about their week, their team, something non-work
- Present your understanding of the project, then ask "Did I get that right?"
- Walk through the timeline and ask where they see risks
- Explicitly confirm: "Who on your side approves deliverables? How long do approvals typically take?"
- Agree on communication norms: preferred channel, expected response times, meeting days
- End with: "What's the one thing that would make this project a failure in your eyes?"

### Kickoff Summary Template
The summary sent after the meeting should include:
1. **Project goals** (as confirmed in the meeting)
2. **Key decisions made** (not a transcript — just decisions)
3. **Timeline and milestones** (updated)
4. **Action items** (who, what, by when)
5. **Next touchpoint** (date and format)

### Common Mistakes
- Treating the kickoff as a presentation instead of a conversation
- Not confirming stakeholders — you present to the project manager, but the CEO has final approval
- Skipping the "How do you prefer to communicate?" question
- Not sending the summary — decisions made verbally are forgotten within 48 hours
- Running the kickoff longer than 60 minutes — attention dies after one hour
- Not recording the meeting (with permission) for team members who couldn't attend

## Phase 4: First Delivery (Day 5-14)

### Purpose
The first delivery is the proof point. Everything before this was promises and process. Now the client sees your actual work for the first time. The quality, timing, and presentation of this delivery determines whether the client relaxes or starts worrying.

### Task Breakdown

| Task | Owner | Timing | Priority |
|------|-------|--------|----------|
| Complete first deliverable | You/team | Day 5-10 (ahead of schedule if possible) | Critical |
| Internal review of deliverable | You/team | Before sending to client | High |
| Write delivery context message | You | With the delivery | Critical |
| Send deliverable with feedback questions | You | Day 7-12 | Critical |
| Follow up if no response in 48 hours | You | 48 hours after delivery | High |
| Process feedback and plan revisions | You | Within 24 hours of receiving feedback | High |
| Deliver revision | You | Per agreed timeline | High |

### Delivery Context Message
Every deliverable should be accompanied by:
1. **What you delivered** — brief description
2. **Your approach** — why you made the choices you made
3. **What you need from them** — specific feedback questions (3-5 max)
4. **Timeline for revisions** — when you'll turn around changes
5. **Next milestone** — what comes after this

### Feedback Question Examples
Instead of "Let me know what you think," ask:
- "Does the tone feel right for your audience?"
- "Are there any sections that feel unclear or incomplete?"
- "On a scale of 1-5, how close is this to what you envisioned?"
- "Is there anything you expected to see that's missing?"
- "Who else needs to review this before we move forward?"

### Common Mistakes
- Delivering late without prior communication
- Sending a deliverable with no context ("Here's the draft, let me know")
- Asking for feedback without specific questions
- Not following up when the client goes silent after delivery
- Delivering a polished final when the client expected a rough draft (or vice versa)
- Ignoring small errors because "it's just a first draft" — first impressions stick

## Phase 5: Check-In (Day 14-30)

### Purpose
The check-in phase catches issues before they become resentments. Most clients won't tell you something is wrong until they're actively angry. A proactive check-in gives them a safe, structured moment to surface concerns. It also signals that you care about the relationship, not just the deliverables.

### Task Breakdown

| Task | Owner | Timing | Priority |
|------|-------|--------|----------|
| Schedule 30-day check-in | You | During kickoff or after first delivery | High |
| Prepare check-in questions | You | Day before | Medium |
| Conduct check-in (call or async) | You + client | Day 14-30 | High |
| Document feedback and action items | You | Same day | High |
| Implement process changes | You | Within 1 week | Medium |
| Send confirmation of changes made | You | After implementing | Medium |
| Transition to ongoing cadence | You | After check-in | Medium |

### Check-In Questions
- "How is the communication working for you? Too much, too little, about right?"
- "Is there anything about our process you'd change?"
- "Do you feel informed about where the project stands?"
- "Is the pace of work meeting your expectations?"
- "Is there anything you haven't brought up that's been on your mind?"
- "On a scale of 1-10, how confident are you that we'll hit the project goals?"

### When to Ask for Referrals/Testimonials
- Only after the check-in confirms satisfaction (score 8+ on the confidence question)
- Frame as: "If you're happy with how things are going, we'd love a short testimonial" — not "Can you refer us to people?"
- Provide a template or specific questions to make it easy
- For retainer clients, wait until month 2-3

### Common Mistakes
- Skipping the check-in entirely ("Everything seems fine")
- Asking about process when the client is stressed about deliverables — fix the work first
- Getting defensive about feedback instead of thanking them for it
- Not acting on feedback — if you ask and don't change anything, you've made it worse
- Asking for a referral when the client is clearly unhappy with something
