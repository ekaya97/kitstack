# Pricing Frameworks

## 1. Project-Based Pricing (Default)

**When to use:** Defined scope, clear deliverables, predictable timeline.

**How it works:**
- Break the project into phases
- Assign a fixed price to each phase based on effort + value
- Total is the sum of phases

**Advantages:**
- Client knows exactly what they'll pay
- You're rewarded for efficiency
- Easy to compare across vendors

**How to calculate:**
1. Estimate hours per phase (internal only — never share with client)
2. Apply your internal hourly rate
3. Add 15-25% buffer for scope creep, revisions, and communication overhead
4. Round to a clean but not suspicious number (e.g., $9,750 not $10,000)
5. Sanity-check against market rates for similar work

**Pricing table format:**
| Phase | Duration | Deliverables | Investment |
|-------|----------|-------------|------------|
| Discovery & Audit | 2 weeks | Stakeholder interviews, competitive audit, findings report | $4,500 |
| Strategy & Design | 3 weeks | Brand strategy doc, visual identity system, guidelines | $8,250 |
| Implementation | 2 weeks | Website templates, social assets, launch support | $5,500 |
| **Total** | **7 weeks** | | **$18,250** |

**Payment schedule options:**
- **50/50:** 50% on signing, 50% on delivery. Simple. Best for small projects.
- **Thirds:** 33% on signing, 33% at midpoint milestone, 34% on delivery. Best for 4-8 week projects.
- **Milestone-based:** Payment tied to phase completion. Best for large or phased projects.
- **Monthly:** For projects spanning 3+ months. Invoice at end of each month for completed work.

## 2. Value-Based Pricing

**When to use:** The project directly impacts revenue, reduces cost, or creates measurable business value.

**When NOT to use:** Commodity work (e.g., basic website maintenance), projects where the client has many alternative vendors at known prices.

**How it works:**
- Quantify the business impact: revenue gained, cost saved, risk reduced
- Price at 10-20% of the expected value
- Present the ROI explicitly in the proposal

**Example calculation:**
- Client's problem: Website converts at 1.2%, industry average is 2.8%
- Client's monthly traffic: 50,000 visitors
- Client's average order value: $85
- Current monthly revenue from web: 50,000 x 1.2% x $85 = $51,000
- Projected revenue at 2.5%: 50,000 x 2.5% x $85 = $106,250
- Monthly uplift: $55,250
- Annual uplift: $663,000
- Your fee at 10% of year-one uplift: $66,300
- Presented as: "Your investment of $66,300 targets a $663,000 annual revenue increase — a 10x return."

**How to present in the proposal:**
> Based on your current conversion rate of 1.2% and monthly traffic of 50,000 visitors, a redesign targeting the industry benchmark of 2.5% would generate an additional $55,000 in monthly revenue. Your investment of $66,300 represents approximately 10% of the projected first-year uplift.

**Framing tips:**
- Always present the client's numbers, not yours
- Use conservative projections (say "targeting 2.5%" when industry is 2.8%)
- Frame your fee as a fraction of their gain
- Include a "breakeven point" — "You'll recoup this investment within 5 weeks of the new site going live."

## 3. Retainer Pricing

**When to use:** Ongoing advisory, maintenance, fractional roles, or work that doesn't have a defined end date.

**How it works:**
- Monthly fee for a defined scope of work or hour allocation
- Typically 3-6 month minimum commitment
- Unused hours do NOT roll over (unless negotiated)

**Retainer structures:**

### Hours-Based Retainer
- Client buys a block of hours: e.g., 20 hours/month at $150/hr = $3,000/month
- You track and report hours monthly
- Overages billed at standard rate or at a premium (1.25x)
- Best for: support, maintenance, ad-hoc consulting

### Scope-Based Retainer
- Fixed monthly fee for a defined set of deliverables
- Example: 4 blog posts + 2 social campaigns + monthly analytics report = $4,500/month
- No hour tracking — you're selling output, not time
- Best for: content, marketing, design work with recurring needs

### Advisory Retainer
- Monthly fee for access + strategic guidance
- Typically includes: X hours of calls/meetings + async support via email/Slack
- Example: Fractional CMO, 10 hours/month, $5,000/month
- Best for: senior expertise that doesn't require full-time presence

**Retainer pricing tips:**
- Price retainers 10-15% below the equivalent project rate — the client is committing to volume
- Always include a minimum term (3 months standard, 6 for executive advisory)
- Define what happens to unused hours (expire, roll over up to X hours)
- Include a quarterly review clause — adjust scope or pricing every 3 months

## 4. Hourly Pricing

**When to use:** Only when the client specifically requests it, or when scope is genuinely unpredictable (e.g., ongoing support, bug fixes, legal review).

**Why to avoid as default:** Hourly pricing punishes efficiency, creates trust friction (client watches the clock), and commoditizes your expertise. A senior consultant who solves a problem in 2 hours delivers more value than a junior who takes 20 hours — but hourly pricing says otherwise.

**If you must use hourly:**
- Always provide an estimate range: "We estimate 40-60 hours at $150/hr ($6,000-$9,000)"
- Include a cap: "Not to exceed $9,000 without written approval"
- Bill in meaningful increments (30 minutes minimum, not 6-minute lawyer increments)
- Report hours with descriptions — client should see what they're paying for

**Rate positioning by experience level:**
| Level | Range (USD) | Range (EUR) |
|-------|------------|------------|
| Junior (1-3 years) | $50-100/hr | 45-90/hr |
| Mid (3-7 years) | $100-175/hr | 90-160/hr |
| Senior (7-15 years) | $150-300/hr | 135-275/hr |
| Expert/Principal (15+ years) | $250-500/hr | 225-450/hr |
| Boutique agency (blended) | $125-250/hr | 110-225/hr |

## 5. Hybrid Pricing

**When to use:** Complex engagements with both defined deliverables and ongoing support.

**Common hybrid structures:**
- **Project + Retainer:** Fixed fee for the build phase, then monthly retainer for ongoing optimization
- **Project + Success Fee:** Base fee for the work + bonus tied to outcomes (e.g., 5% of revenue increase above baseline)
- **Retainer + Per-Project:** Monthly base fee + quoted add-ons for specific projects

**Example:**
> **Phase 1 — Website Redesign (Project):** $32,000 over 10 weeks
> **Phase 2 — Ongoing Optimization (Retainer):** $3,500/month for 6 months
> **Total Year-One Investment:** $53,000

## Pricing Presentation Principles

1. **Always show the breakdown.** A single number without context feels arbitrary. A breakdown feels transparent.
2. **Round intentionally.** $9,750 feels calculated. $10,000 feels made up.
3. **Present options when appropriate.** A "Good / Better / Best" structure gives the client agency. The middle option is almost always chosen.
4. **Never apologize for your price.** Present it with confidence. If you feel the need to apologize, you've priced too high for the value you're delivering.
5. **Frame as investment.** "Your investment of $18,250" — not "The cost is $18,250" or "Our fee is $18,250."
6. **Include what's NOT included.** A clear out-of-scope section prevents scope creep and demonstrates thoroughness.
7. **State validity.** "This proposal is valid for 30 days from the date above." Creates urgency without pressure.
