# Personalization Framework

How to research a prospect and weave personalized elements into cold email sequences without sounding stalker-ish or forced.

---

## The 3-Layer Model

### Layer 1: Company Context (2 minutes of research)

**Where to find it:**
- Company website: About page, blog, press/news section, careers page
- LinkedIn company page: recent posts, employee count, growth signals
- Crunchbase: funding rounds, investors, company size
- Google News: "[Company name]" — recent press coverage

**What to look for:**
- Recent funding round ("Congrats on the Series B")
- New product launch or feature release
- Office expansion or new market entry
- Hiring surge in a specific area (indicator of priorities)
- Recent press coverage or award

**How to use it:**
- Reference it in the first line, not the subject line (subject line should be about them, not their news)
- Connect the news to your value proposition: "Saw you're expanding into DACH — that's exactly where we've helped [similar company] navigate the market."
- Keep it to one sentence. Don't summarize their entire press release.

**Example:**
> Noticed [Company] just opened a Berlin office — exciting move into DACH. When [Similar Company] made the same expansion last year, they ran into [specific challenge]. We helped them [result].

---

### Layer 2: Role Context (3 minutes of research)

**Where to find it:**
- LinkedIn profile: headline, about section, experience, featured section
- Personal website or blog (if they have one)
- Conference speaker profiles
- Podcast guest appearances (often listed on their LinkedIn)

**What to look for:**
- Their specific responsibilities and focus areas
- How long they've been in the role (new = different priorities than tenured)
- Career trajectory (where they came from tells you what they value)
- Skills and endorsements (shows what they want to be known for)
- Content they share or create

**How to use it:**
- Reference their specific role challenges, not generic industry problems
- If they're new in the role (< 6 months), they're likely making changes — frame your outreach around "new leader" priorities
- If they've been in the role 2+ years, they're optimizing — frame around improvement, not change

**Example:**
> Saw you moved from [Previous Company] to lead [function] at [Company] about 6 months ago. The first year in a new [role] usually means inheriting systems that weren't your choice. If [specific problem area] is on your list, I have a few observations that might save you a quarter of experimentation.

---

### Layer 3: Personal Context (5 minutes of research)

**Where to find it:**
- LinkedIn posts and articles they've written
- Twitter/X posts (if active)
- Podcast appearances (search "[name] podcast")
- Conference talks (search "[name] speaker" or check YouTube)
- Medium, Substack, or personal blog

**What to look for:**
- Opinions they've expressed publicly
- Challenges they've mentioned
- Frameworks or approaches they advocate
- Personal interests that overlap with professional (e.g., they post about leadership books)
- Shared experiences (same university, city, previous employer, conference)

**How to use it:**
- Reference a specific post, article, or talk — not vaguely ("I love your content") but specifically ("Your post about [topic] on [date/platform] resonated — particularly the point about [specific point].")
- Agree, build on, or respectfully challenge their stated position
- Connect your outreach to their publicly stated priorities

**Example:**
> Your LinkedIn post last week about the diminishing returns of more sales headcount vs. better sales tooling struck a chord. We're seeing the same pattern with our clients — [Company A] cut their SDR team by 30% and increased pipeline by 40% after [what you did]. Curious if that's a trade-off you're exploring at [their company].

## Personalization Intensity by Prospect Value

| Prospect Value | Research Time | Personalization Depth | Customization |
|---------------|--------------|----------------------|---------------|
| Low (mass outreach) | 30 seconds | Layer 1 only: company + role | Template with merge fields |
| Medium (targeted outreach) | 2-3 minutes | Layers 1 + 2: company + role context | Semi-custom first email, templated follow-ups |
| High (strategic accounts) | 5-10 minutes | All 3 layers: company + role + personal | Fully custom first 2 emails, semi-custom follow-ups |
| Critical (top 10 dream accounts) | 15+ minutes | All 3 layers + mutual connections + event timing | Entirely custom sequence, possibly multi-channel |

## Personalization Variables

These are the dynamic elements to customize per prospect. Mark them in templates with brackets:

```
[first_name] — Prospect's first name
[company] — Prospect's company name
[role] — Their job title or function
[company_context] — Recent news, growth signal, or company milestone
[role_context] — Challenge specific to their role
[personal_context] — Reference to their content, talk, or shared experience
[similar_company] — A company similar to theirs that you've helped
[specific_result] — Quantified result you achieved for the similar company
[mutual_connection] — Shared connection, if any
[trigger_event] — What prompted this outreach now (funding, hiring, launch)
```

## Personalization Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- Reference things that are publicly available (LinkedIn, company blog, press)
- Connect personalization to your value proposition (don't just show off your research)
- Keep personalization to 1-2 sentences max — it opens the door, it's not the whole email
- Use personalization in Email 1 and Email 3 primarily — Emails 2 and 4 can be more templated
- Reference recent content (last 30 days) — it shows you're paying attention now, not Googling old articles

### Don't:
- Reference private information (family, health, personal social media)
- Overdo it — 3+ personalized references in one email feels like surveillance
- Fake personalization ("I love what you're doing at [Company]" without specifics is worse than no personalization)
- Reference competitor drama or negative press — keep it positive
- Assume you know their problems — frame as questions ("Is [challenge] something you're dealing with?") not statements ("I know you're struggling with [challenge]")
- Stale personalization — referencing a funding round from 18 months ago shows you just Googled them

## Generating Personalization Hooks

When you have prospect information, generate 3-5 hooks to choose from:

**Hook types:**
1. **Trigger hook** — Reference a recent event: "Saw your Series A announcement..."
2. **Content hook** — Reference their content: "Your post about [topic] resonated..."
3. **Connection hook** — Reference a shared connection: "Sarah at [Company] suggested I reach out..."
4. **Observation hook** — Share a genuine observation: "Noticed your team has grown 3x in the last year — the challenge of maintaining culture at that growth rate is real..."
5. **Challenge hook** — Reference a known industry challenge: "Most [role]s I talk to are dealing with [specific challenge] right now..."

**Selecting the best hook:**
- Trigger and content hooks have the highest response rates (they're the most relevant and recent)
- Connection hooks have the highest open rates (people open emails referencing people they know)
- Challenge hooks are the most scalable (one research session covers many prospects in the same role)
- Observation hooks demonstrate the most depth (shows you've thought about their situation, not just Googled them)

Use the best hook for Email 1. Save the second-best for Email 3.
