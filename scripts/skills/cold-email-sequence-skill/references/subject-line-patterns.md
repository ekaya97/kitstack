# Subject Line Patterns

15+ tested patterns for cold email subject lines. Each includes the pattern, when to use it, and examples.

---

## Rules (Review Before Selecting)

1. Under 6 words. Mobile preview cuts off at 30-40 characters.
2. Lowercase is acceptable and often outperforms title case in cold email.
3. No exclamation marks. Ever.
4. No spam words: free, guarantee, limited time, act now, exclusive, urgent.
5. No fake RE: or FWD: prefixes.
6. One subject per thread. Follow-ups in the same sequence should use NEW subject lines, not replies to the original.

---

## Pattern 1: The Question

**Pattern:** A genuine question about their situation.
**When to use:** Email 1 in most sequences. Works across all industries.
**Why it works:** Questions create a cognitive loop — the brain wants to answer them.

**Examples:**
- `[company]'s approach to [topic]?`
- `quick question about [company]`
- `how does [company] handle [challenge]?`
- `[first_name], thoughts on [topic]?`

---

## Pattern 2: The Mutual Connection

**Pattern:** Reference a shared person or context.
**When to use:** When you have a genuine mutual connection or shared experience.
**Why it works:** Social proof + familiarity. Highest open rate pattern.

**Examples:**
- `[mutual_name] suggested I reach out`
- `fellow [conference/group] member`
- `[mutual_name] mentioned you`

---

## Pattern 3: The Observation

**Pattern:** Reference something specific you noticed about their company.
**When to use:** When you've done research and found a specific trigger.
**Why it works:** Demonstrates effort and relevance.

**Examples:**
- `noticed [company]'s expansion into [market]`
- `saw [company] is hiring [X roles]`
- `[company]'s new [product/feature]`
- `your [conference] talk on [topic]`

---

## Pattern 4: The Result

**Pattern:** Lead with a specific, quantified result.
**When to use:** Email 3 (social proof email). Works best with impressive, specific numbers.
**Why it works:** Numbers stand out in an inbox. Specific results build credibility.

**Examples:**
- `23% churn reduction for [similar company]`
- `how [company] cut CAC by 40%`
- `[similar company]'s pipeline grew 3x`
- `from 2 to 14 qualified leads/week`

---

## Pattern 5: The Resource

**Pattern:** Offer something useful without asking for anything.
**When to use:** Email 2 (value-add email).
**Why it works:** Reciprocity. Giving before asking builds goodwill.

**Examples:**
- `[topic] benchmark report`
- `3 ideas for [company]'s [challenge]`
- `thought you'd find this useful`
- `something for your [specific initiative]`

---

## Pattern 6: The [Company] + [Topic]

**Pattern:** Their company name + the topic you want to discuss.
**When to use:** Universal opener. Safe and effective.
**Why it works:** Company name grabs their attention. Topic signals relevance.

**Examples:**
- `[company] + [your offering category]`
- `[company]'s [department] workflow`
- `[company] and outbound`
- `[company] growth experiment`

---

## Pattern 7: The Contrarian

**Pattern:** Challenge a common assumption in their industry.
**When to use:** When your value proposition goes against conventional wisdom.
**Why it works:** Curiosity gap. People click on things that challenge their beliefs.

**Examples:**
- `the problem with [common approach]`
- `why [standard practice] doesn't work anymore`
- `unpopular opinion on [topic]`
- `what [industry] gets wrong about [topic]`

---

## Pattern 8: The Permission-Based

**Pattern:** Ask if a topic is even worth their time.
**When to use:** Email 1 for high-level prospects (C-suite, founders).
**Why it works:** Respects their time. Reduces the perceived ask.

**Examples:**
- `worth a conversation?`
- `relevant for [company]?`
- `is this on your radar?`
- `quick question — 2 min`

---

## Pattern 9: The Name-Drop

**Pattern:** Reference a recognizable company or person (that you've actually worked with).
**When to use:** When you have genuine, impressive social proof.
**Why it works:** Association with recognized brands transfers credibility.

**Examples:**
- `how we helped [recognized company]`
- `what [recognized company] did differently`
- `[recognized name]'s approach to [topic]`

---

## Pattern 10: The Time-Bound

**Pattern:** Reference a specific, relevant time pressure.
**When to use:** When there's a genuine deadline or seasonal relevance (NOT fake urgency).
**Why it works:** Real relevance to their planning cycle.

**Examples:**
- `Q4 planning and [topic]`
- `before your [known event/launch]`
- `[industry event] next month`
- `end-of-year [challenge]`

---

## Pattern 11: The Breakup

**Pattern:** Signal that this is your last email.
**When to use:** Final email in the sequence.
**Why it works:** Loss aversion. People pay more attention to things going away.

**Examples:**
- `closing the loop`
- `should I stop reaching out?`
- `last one from me`
- `not the right time?`

---

## Pattern 12: The Casual

**Pattern:** One or two words, extremely casual.
**When to use:** Follow-ups, especially Email 2 or 3.
**Why it works:** Stands out in an inbox full of formal subject lines. Feels personal.

**Examples:**
- `hey`
- `quick thought`
- `idea for you`
- `this might help`

---

## Pattern 13: The Specific Number

**Pattern:** Lead with a specific, interesting number.
**When to use:** When you have a compelling data point.
**Why it works:** Numbers are processed faster than words. Specificity builds credibility.

**Examples:**
- `47 days to close → 12`
- `3 things about [company]'s [topic]`
- `1 question for you`
- `the 15-minute experiment`

---

## Pattern 14: The Challenge

**Pattern:** Frame as addressing a known challenge they have.
**When to use:** When you know their specific pain point.
**Why it works:** Immediately relevant. Signals you understand their world.

**Examples:**
- `solving [specific challenge] at [company]`
- `the [role] hiring challenge`
- `[company]'s [known bottleneck]`

---

## Pattern 15: The Referral

**Pattern:** Imply (truthfully) that someone pointed you in their direction.
**When to use:** When you were genuinely referred, or when you found them through their content/speaking.
**Why it works:** Referrals have 3-5x higher response rates than cold outreach.

**Examples:**
- `your talk at [event] led me here`
- `saw you on [podcast name]`
- `your post on [topic] — had to reach out`

---

## Pattern 16: The Intrigue

**Pattern:** Hint at something valuable without revealing everything.
**When to use:** When you have a genuinely interesting insight to share.
**Why it works:** Curiosity gap drives opens. But the email MUST deliver on the promise.

**Examples:**
- `noticed something about [company]`
- `an idea for [first_name]`
- `something [similar company] tried`

## Subject Lines by Sequence Position

| Position | Best Patterns | Avoid |
|----------|--------------|-------|
| Email 1 | Question, Observation, Permission-Based, [Company] + [Topic] | Result (too salesy for first touch), Breakup |
| Email 2 | Resource, Casual, Specific Number | Breakup, Mutual Connection (should be in Email 1) |
| Email 3 | Result, Name-Drop, Challenge, Contrarian | Permission-Based (too soft for Email 3) |
| Email 4 | Breakup, Casual | Everything else (keep it simple) |
| Email 5 | Breakup | Everything else |

---

## A/B Testing Guidelines

For each sequence, generate 2 subject line options for Email 1 (highest-impact position):

- **Version A:** The most personalized option (company name, specific reference)
- **Version B:** The most curiosity-driven option (question, intrigue, contrarian)

Test with a 50/50 split on a sample of at least 50 recipients before selecting a winner for the full list.
