# Example: Agency Competitor Teardown

## User Input

> Compare my agency against BigDesign Studio. I run a 6-person branding agency in Berlin focused on tech startups. BigDesign is our biggest competitor — they're 20 people, have a flashier portfolio, and keep showing up in pitches against us. I need to understand how to position against them.

---

## Generated Teardown

# Competitive Positioning: Your Agency vs. BigDesign Studio
## May 2026

---

## Market Context

The Berlin startup branding market has a specific dynamic: a handful of premium agencies (15+ people, EUR 30K+ projects) serve funded startups, while dozens of freelancers and micro-agencies serve bootstrapped founders. The mid-tier — 4-10 person agencies that deliver premium quality at accessible price points — is less crowded. That's where you sit.

BigDesign occupies the premium end. Understanding exactly what they do well and where they're vulnerable lets you stop competing on their terms and start competing on yours.

---

## Head-to-Head Profile

| Dimension | Your Agency | BigDesign Studio |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Team size** | 6 | 20 |
| **Focus** | Branding for tech startups | Branding + digital design for tech companies (startup through growth) |
| **Price range** | EUR 8,000-25,000 per project | EUR 25,000-80,000+ per project |
| **Typical client** | Seed to Series A, 5-30 employees | Series A to Series C, 30-200 employees |
| **Timeline** | 4-8 weeks | 8-16 weeks |
| **Portfolio style** | Clean, strategic, founder-friendly | Bold, award-winning, design-forward |
| **Positioning** | "Brand strategy + identity for tech startups" | "Design studio for ambitious tech companies" |
| **Sales process** | Founder-to-founder conversation | Formal pitch, account manager, proposal deck |
| **Strengths** | Speed, direct access to senior talent, strategic depth, startup empathy | Visual execution, brand recognition, case study depth, design awards |
| **Weaknesses** | Smaller portfolio, less name recognition, limited capacity (can only take 3-4 projects simultaneously) | Slow, expensive, junior designers do the work after senior pitch it, process-heavy |

---

## Where BigDesign Is Strong (Be Honest)

1. **Visual execution is exceptional.** Their portfolio is genuinely impressive. The work is polished, bold, and consistent. If a prospect judges purely on visual portfolio quality, BigDesign wins.

2. **Social proof.** They've worked with recognizable startups. Prospects see logos they know. This creates trust, especially for founders who've never hired a branding agency before.

3. **Awards and press.** They've won design awards and been featured in design publications. This matters more than it should — but it does matter for a certain type of buyer.

4. **Capacity for larger scopes.** A 20-person team can handle a full brand identity + website + marketing assets + packaging in one engagement. You'd need to subcontract parts of that scope.

---

## Where BigDesign Is Vulnerable

1. **The bait-and-switch problem.** A partner or creative director pitches the work. A mid-level designer executes it. This is the most common agency complaint in the market and it applies to BigDesign based on Glassdoor reviews and founder feedback. Your counter: "The person who pitches you is the person who does the work."

2. **Timeline.** 8-16 weeks for a brand identity is standard for a large agency but painful for a startup that needs to launch. Your 4-8 week timeline is a genuine competitive advantage for speed-sensitive founders.

3. **Price-to-outcome mismatch for early-stage startups.** A EUR 50K brand identity for a pre-revenue startup is hard to justify regardless of quality. The brand will evolve as the product evolves. BigDesign's process is optimized for companies that need a "final" brand, not a "version 1" brand.

4. **Process overhead.** At 20 people, BigDesign has account managers, project coordinators, and approval workflows. For a 10-person startup, this feels like unnecessary bureaucracy. Founders want to talk to the designer, not schedule a call with the account manager to schedule a call with the designer.

5. **Strategy depth.** BigDesign leads with visual design. Their strategy phase is shorter and less rigorous. For founders who want brand strategy (positioning, messaging, audience definition) before visual identity, your deeper strategic approach is an advantage.

## Positioning Analysis

**Axes:** Speed-to-Delivery vs. Project Budget

```
Fast Delivery
     |
     |   YOUR AGENCY
     |
     |
     |                   [Freelancers]
     |
     |                            BigDesign
     |
     |
     |_________________________________________ High Budget
```

You and BigDesign don't actually compete on the same axis. They're high-budget, slower delivery. You're accessible budget, fast delivery. The problem isn't that you're worse — it's that you're appearing in the same pitches because founders don't yet understand the difference.

---

## When You Win vs. When They Win

| Scenario | Who wins | Why |
|----------|----------|-----|
| Seed-stage startup needs a brand before demo day in 6 weeks | You | Speed, price, founder empathy |
| Series B company rebranding after 3 years of growth | BigDesign | Portfolio depth, capacity for full scope, perceived "safety" of a larger team |
| Technical founder who wants strategy before aesthetics | You | Deeper strategy phase, direct access to senior thinking |
| Non-technical founder who wants to "look like a Series B company" | BigDesign | Visual portfolio creates aspiration |
| Startup with EUR 12K brand budget | You | BigDesign won't take this project |
| Startup with EUR 40K brand budget and time pressure | You (if you position right) | Your speed + direct senior access offsets the smaller team |
| Startup with EUR 40K budget and no time pressure | BigDesign | Portfolio and awards create perceived safety |

**Key insight:** You lose to BigDesign primarily on perceived risk, not actual quality. A founder choosing between you thinks: "BigDesign is safer — they're bigger, they have more case studies." The fix isn't to become bigger. It's to make your specific advantages so clear that "bigger" stops being the deciding factor.

## Positioning Strategy

### Your "Only" Statement

> "We are the only Berlin branding agency that delivers a complete brand identity — strategy, visual system, and guidelines — in under 6 weeks, led by a senior strategist from day one, for tech startups at EUR 8-25K."

This statement is true and BigDesign cannot make it without fundamentally changing their model.

### The Three Messages That Win

**Message 1: "Senior talent from pitch to pixels."**
Position against the bait-and-switch. Make it explicit: "The person you meet in the first call is the person designing your brand. No handoffs, no juniors, no surprises." This directly attacks BigDesign's vulnerability without naming them.

**Message 2: "Brand in 6 weeks, not 6 months."**
Position around speed as a feature for startups, not a compromise. "Startups don't need a 12-week process. You need a brand that works today and evolves with you. We deliver in 4-6 weeks because we start with strategy, not with a 3-hour kickoff workshop."

**Message 3: "Strategy-first, not portfolio-first."**
Position around the depth of thinking, not the boldness of the visuals. "Your brand isn't a logo. It's your positioning, your messaging, and your visual identity — in that order. We spend the first 2 weeks on strategy before designing a single pixel." This appeals to technical founders who are skeptical of pure visual agencies.

---

## Tactical Recommendations

### 1. Create a "Startup Brand" Package

**What:** A productized brand offering specifically for seed-to-Series A companies. Fixed price (EUR 12,500), fixed timeline (5 weeks), fixed deliverables (positioning, visual identity, brand guidelines, 3 application mockups).

**Why:** BigDesign does custom scoping for every project. A productized offering reduces the prospect's decision anxiety ("I know exactly what I'm getting and what it costs"). It also positions you as the specialist — you've done this so many times you've productized it.

**How:** Create a landing page for the package. List exactly what's included. Show the 5-week timeline. Include 3 case studies of startups you've done this for.

### 2. Invest in Startup-Specific Case Studies

**What:** Produce 3-5 detailed case studies that show the before, the process, and the after. Focus on outcomes: "They raised their Series A 3 months after the rebrand" or "Conversion increased 40% after the brand refresh."

**Why:** BigDesign has more case studies, but they're portfolio pieces (look at the design). Yours should be strategy pieces (look at the outcome). Different type of proof for a different type of buyer.

**How:** Contact 3-5 past clients. Interview them. Write the case study as a story: problem, approach, result.

### 3. Build a "BigDesign Comparison" Talking Point (Not a Page)

**What:** Prepare a response for when prospects say "We're also talking to BigDesign." Not a comparison page — a conversation-ready talking point.

**Talk track:** "They do great work. If you're looking for a 15-week premium brand process with award-winning visual execution, they're a strong choice. What we do differently is: we lead with strategy, we deliver in 5 weeks, and the senior strategist who sits in this room today is the same person who designs your brand. For a startup that needs to move fast and wants strategic depth over process, that's a meaningful difference."

**Why:** This is confident, not competitive. You acknowledge BigDesign's strengths (which builds trust) and then reframe the decision on your terms.

### 4. Target the "BigDesign Is Too Expensive" Segment

**What:** There are founders who want BigDesign-quality work but can't afford EUR 40K+. These are your ideal prospects.

**How:** Monitor startup communities (Berlin Startup Slack, LinkedIn, founder events) for conversations about branding. When someone mentions needing a brand agency, your message is: "Premium branding doesn't require a premium agency price tag. We deliver senior-level brand strategy and identity for EUR 8-25K in under 6 weeks."

### 5. Develop a Post-BigDesign Service

**What:** Some BigDesign clients will need ongoing brand work — social templates, pitch deck updates, collateral — that BigDesign won't do (too small for them). Position yourself as the "brand maintenance" partner.

**How:** Network with BigDesign clients after their engagement ends. Offer a brand retainer: EUR 2,000-3,000/month for ongoing design support, applying the brand system BigDesign created.

**Why:** This is non-competitive (you're not taking work from BigDesign) and builds relationships with the exact type of funded startup that might choose you for their next venture.
