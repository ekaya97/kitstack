# Pricing Intelligence

How to analyze competitor pricing — not just what they charge, but what their pricing strategy tells you about their positioning and where opportunities exist.

---

## 1. Gathering Pricing Data

### Public Sources

| Source | What you find | Reliability |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| Pricing page | Listed prices, tiers, feature breakdown | High — this is what they want you to see |
| G2 / Capterra reviews | Mentions of price, value perception, hidden costs | Medium — reviews are self-selecting |
| Job postings | Technology stack (indicates build vs. buy costs), team size (indicates cost structure) | Medium — indirect signal |
| Crunchbase / LinkedIn | Funding stage (indicates pricing pressure), team size | Medium — context for pricing decisions |
| Reddit / Twitter / HN | Complaints about pricing, comparisons to alternatives | Medium — vocal minority |
| Case studies / testimonials | Sometimes mention specific pricing or ROI ("saved $X/month") | Low-medium — marketing-selected |
| Sales conversations | Competitor pricing mentioned by prospects ("your competitor quoted us $X") | High — real market signal |

### Hidden Pricing Signals

**What they don't list is as important as what they do:**
- "Contact us for pricing" = Enterprise pricing, likely $500+/month or custom quotes. They're price-discriminating by buyer.
- "Starting at $X" = The real price is higher. The starting price is for a feature-limited tier designed to anchor perception.
- Free tier with strict limits = Land-and-expand strategy. The real revenue comes from upgrades.
- Annual-only pricing = They need cash flow and want to reduce churn. The monthly equivalent is their real target price.
- Per-seat pricing = They're optimizing for team adoption. Expect discounts at volume.

### Reverse Engineering from Proposals

When a prospect shares a competitor's quote or proposal:
- Note the price point AND the scope — a lower price for less scope is not cheaper
- Identify what's included vs. what's an add-on (many competitors unbundle to look cheaper)
- Check if the pricing is introductory (first year discount, then renews at full rate)
- Ask about payment terms — net 30 vs. annual prepay changes the effective cost

## 2. Analyzing Pricing Strategy

Pricing is not just a number — it's a positioning statement. Here's how to decode what a competitor's pricing tells you.

### The Value-Price Matrix

Plot competitors on two axes: the value they deliver vs. the price they charge.

|                    | **Low Price**         | **High Price**          |
|--------------------|-----------------------|------------------------|
| **High Value**     | Disruptor / Growth play — acquiring market share by underpricing. Funded, or planning to raise prices later. | Premium / Enterprise — confident in their value, targeting buyers who aren't price-sensitive. |
| **Low Value**      | Commodity / Race to the bottom — competing on price because they can't compete on value. Dangerous to compete with here. | Overpriced / Legacy — riding brand recognition or switching costs. Vulnerable to disruption. |

**Where to position yourself:**
- **If you're small:** Don't compete in the low-price quadrants. You can't out-cheap a funded competitor or a commodity provider. Compete on value.
- **If you're a freelancer/agency:** Price in the high-value, high-price quadrant. Justify it with specificity (niche expertise, outcomes, case studies).
- **If you're a SaaS:** Price in the high-value quadrant. Whether high or low price depends on your growth strategy (market share vs. revenue per customer).

### Pricing Model Analysis

Each pricing model sends a signal. Analyze what your competitor's model tells you:

| Model | Signal | Implication for you |
|-------|--------|-------------------|
| Per-seat / per-user | They monetize adoption. More users = more revenue. | If your product works for the whole team but charges flat, position as "no per-seat tax" |
| Usage-based | They monetize growth. As the customer grows, they pay more. | If your product offers predictable pricing, position as "no surprise bills" |
| Flat monthly | They want simplicity. Easy to sell, easy to understand. | Hard to compete on simplicity. Compete on value per dollar instead. |
| Tiered (Good/Better/Best) | They're segmenting by buyer willingness to pay. The middle tier is the target. | Study their tier boundaries. What features gate the upgrade? Is there a natural tier they're missing that you could own? |
| Free + paid | They monetize conversion from free to paid. The free product is marketing. | You can't beat free on price. Compete on the value of the paid tier, or target the segment that won't start with a free tool (enterprise, regulated). |
| Custom / "Contact us" | They price-discriminate by buyer. Enterprise strategy. | If you have transparent pricing, position it as a differentiator: "See our prices now. No sales calls required." |
| Project-based (services) | They're selling outcomes, not hours. | If you're on hourly, consider switching to project-based. If they're on project-based, understand their scope definition to compare accurately. |

### Price Anchoring Analysis

How does the competitor use anchoring?

- **Decoy pricing:** The expensive tier makes the middle tier look reasonable. Identify the decoy.
- **Comparison anchoring:** Do they compare to more expensive alternatives on their website? ("10x cheaper than Salesforce")
- **ROI anchoring:** Do they frame their price against the cost of the problem? ("Your team wastes 10 hours/week — at $50/hour, that's $2,000/month. Our tool is $99/month.")
- **Annual discount anchoring:** "Save 20% with annual billing" — the annual price is the real price. The monthly price is the anchor.

## 3. Pricing Gap Analysis

After gathering and analyzing competitor pricing, identify gaps:

### Price Point Gaps
Is there a price range with no good options? If competitors cluster at $29/month and $299/month, the $79-149 range might be underserved.

### Model Gaps
If every competitor charges per-seat, a flat-rate model is differentiation. If everyone charges monthly, annual with a significant discount is differentiation.

### Segment Gaps
Are there customer segments being overcharged or underserved?
- Solopreneurs paying for team features they don't use
- Enterprises forced onto a plan designed for SMBs
- Seasonal businesses paying monthly for something they use 4 months/year

### Transparency Gaps
If competitors hide pricing, transparency is a weapon: "We publish our prices. No sales calls, no negotiations, no 'it depends.'"

### Value Delivery Gaps
If competitors charge for features but don't deliver outcomes, position around outcomes: "We don't charge for features. We charge for [result]."

## 4. Pricing Intelligence for Services (Freelancers and Agencies)

Service pricing is less transparent than product pricing, but signals exist.

### How to Estimate Competitor Rates

| Signal | What it tells you |
|--------|------------------|
| Proposals shared by prospects | Direct pricing data. Gold standard. |
| Glassdoor / PayScale salary data for their employees | If their designer earns $80K, their agency likely charges $150-250/hr for that person's time |
| Case studies mentioning project scope | If they built a website in 8 weeks with a team of 3, estimate: 8 x 40 x 3 = 960 hours. Apply a rate range. |
| Their positioning language | "Premium" or "boutique" = higher rates. "Affordable" or "efficient" = lower rates. |
| Client list | Enterprise clients = higher rates. Startups and small businesses = lower rates. |
| Team size vs. revenue (if estimable) | Revenue per head indicates average billing rate and margin |

### Service Pricing Positioning Strategies

**The "Not Cheapest, Not Most Expensive" Position:**
Most service buyers distrust the cheapest option (quality risk) and can't afford the most expensive. Pricing in the 60th-80th percentile of the market often captures the most clients.

**The Niche Premium:**
A generalist web designer charges $100/hr. A web designer specializing in e-commerce for DTC beauty brands charges $175/hr. The niche justifies the premium because the buyer perceives reduced risk.

**The Package vs. Hourly Shift:**
If competitors charge hourly, offer project-based pricing. This reframes the conversation from "how much does your time cost?" to "what's the result worth?"

## 5. Using Pricing Intelligence in Teardowns

### In the Report

Present pricing intelligence as a comparison table:

| Competitor | Model | Starting Price | Enterprise Price | Notable |
|-----------|-------|---------------|-----------------|---------|
| Competitor A | Per-seat, monthly | $12/user/month | Custom | Free tier, aggressive land-and-expand |
| Competitor B | Flat, tiered | $49/month | $299/month | No free tier, annual discount 25% |
| Competitor C | Usage-based | $0 (pay per use) | Volume discounts | Hard to predict costs at scale |
| **You** | **[Model]** | **[Price]** | **[Price]** | **[Differentiation]** |

Then interpret: "Competitor A's per-seat model means a 50-person team pays $600/month. Your flat $199/month for unlimited users is a clear cost advantage for teams above 17 users. Position around this."

### In Recommendations

Pricing recommendations should be specific:
- "Price $10-20 above Competitor A's mid-tier to signal higher quality, but include features they gate behind their enterprise tier"
- "Match Competitor B's annual pricing but offer monthly with no commitment — their annual-only model frustrates buyers in your target segment"
- "Position your hourly rate at $150 (25% above local market average) and justify with niche expertise in [area]"
