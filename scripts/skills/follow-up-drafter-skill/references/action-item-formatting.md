# Action Item Formatting Guide

How to present action items in follow-up emails so they're clear, trackable, and actually get done. The format you choose matters — buried action items don't get completed.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Every action item has an owner.** "We need to update the proposal" is not an action item. "Maria will update the proposal" is.
2. **Every action item has a deadline.** "Soon" and "ASAP" are not deadlines. "By Friday, May 9" is.
3. **Action items are verifiable.** The reader should be able to tell when it's done. "Think about pricing" is not verifiable. "Send revised pricing table" is.
4. **Use the person's name, not their role.** "Sarah" is better than "the project manager." Names create accountability. Roles create ambiguity.
5. **Separate your actions from theirs.** Make it obvious what you're committing to and what you're asking of them.

## Format Options

### Option 1: Table Format (Best for 4+ Action Items)

Use a table when there are many action items or when the email goes to multiple people with different responsibilities.

| Action | Owner | Deadline |
|--------|-------|----------|
| Send revised proposal with updated scope | Me | Friday, May 9 |
| Share API documentation and staging credentials | Thomas (Acme) | Wednesday, May 7 |
| Schedule legal review call | Both — I'll send invite | Week of May 12 |
| Confirm budget approval from CFO | Lisa (Acme) | By next meeting (May 14) |
| Deliver wireframes for review | Me | Monday, May 12 |

**When to use:** Complex meetings with multiple owners, project kickoffs, planning sessions.

**Formatting rules:**
- Owner column uses first names, with company/team in parentheses if cross-organization
- "Me" for your own items (not your full name — the email is from you, they know who "Me" is)
- "Both" when joint ownership is required, with clarification on who initiates
- Deadlines as specific dates, not relative ("Friday, May 9" not "end of this week")

### Option 2: Grouped Lists (Best for 2-Party Emails)

When the email is between two parties, group action items by owner. This makes it immediately clear who owes what.

**What I'll do:**
- Send the revised proposal with Phase 2 pricing by Friday, May 9
- Set up the shared project channel in Slack by tomorrow
- Draft the project timeline and share for your review by Monday

**What we need from your side:**
- Brand guidelines and logo files (any format) by Wednesday
- Confirm the internal stakeholder list for the kickoff call
- Provide read-only access to your analytics dashboard

**Formatting rules:**
- Your items first (demonstrates initiative)
- Their items second (clear ask, not buried)
- Each item is a complete sentence with a deadline
- Use "we need from your side" not "you need to" (collaborative, not demanding)

### Option 3: Inline Action Items (Best for 2-3 Items in Short Emails)

When there are only a couple of action items, embedding them in the email body is cleaner than a table.

> I'll send the revised timeline by Thursday. Could you share the updated stakeholder list before our call on Friday?

**When to use:** Brief follow-ups, check-ins, simple meetings with few outcomes.

**Formatting rules:**
- Bold the key action if the email has other content: "**I'll send the revised timeline by Thursday.**"
- Keep it conversational — inline items should read as natural prose
- Don't mix inline with tables — pick one format per email

## Owner Assignment Conventions

### Assigning to Yourself
- Use "I'll" or "Me" — not your full name
- Phrase as a commitment: "I'll send..." / "I'll have this ready by..."
- Include a specific date, even when the deadline is obvious

### Assigning to Others (External)
- Use their first name: "Sarah will share..." / "Could you [first name] confirm..."
- Phrase as a confirmation of what was discussed, not a new assignment: "As we discussed, Thomas will provide the API docs by Wednesday."
- If assigning something that wasn't explicitly discussed, frame as a suggestion: "It would be helpful to have the analytics access by Wednesday — is that doable?"

### Assigning to Others (Internal / Team)
- Direct and specific: "[Name] — please have the updated deck ready by EOD Thursday."
- Include the standard or scope: "Update the deck with the revised Q3 numbers and the new timeline slide."
- Tag or CC only the people with action items

### Joint or Shared Ownership
- Always clarify who initiates: "Both — I'll send the calendar invite" or "Joint — Sarah will draft, I'll review"
- Shared ownership without a point person means nobody owns it. Pick someone to lead.

## Deadline Formatting

### Specific Dates (Preferred)
- "By Friday, May 9"
- "Before our next call on Tuesday, May 13"
- "End of day Wednesday"

### Relative Dates (Acceptable When Date is Obvious)
- "By end of week" (only when sent Monday-Wednesday)
- "Before our next meeting" (only when the meeting is already scheduled)
- "Within 48 hours"

### Never Use
- "ASAP" — this means different things to everyone. Replace with a date.
- "When you get a chance" — this means never. Replace with a date.
- "Soon" — meaningless. Replace with a date.
- "At your earliest convenience" — corporate filler for "I don't have the confidence to set a deadline." Replace with a date.

## Priority Indicators

Use priority markers sparingly. If everything is high priority, nothing is.

### In Tables
Add a priority column only when items have genuinely different urgency levels:

| Action | Owner | Deadline | Priority |
|--------|-------|----------|----------|
| Fix production login bug | Dev team | Today | Urgent |
| Send revised contract | Me | Friday | High |
| Research competitor pricing | Ana | Next week | Normal |

### In Lists
Use inline markers:
- **[Urgent]** Fix the production login bug — Dev team, today
- **[Before next meeting]** Send revised contract — Me, Friday
- Research competitor pricing — Ana, next week

### When NOT to Use Priority Markers
- When all items have the same priority (just list them in order of urgency)
- When the email has 3 or fewer items (the ordering implies priority)
- In client-facing emails where "Urgent" might create unnecessary anxiety

## Follow-Up Tracking Language

Phrases that create accountability without being aggressive:

**Committing to your own follow-up:**
- "I'll send this by Friday. If you don't see it by end of day, ping me."
- "I'll confirm with legal and circle back by Wednesday."
- "Expect the revised proposal in your inbox by Thursday morning."

**Requesting others' follow-up:**
- "Could you share the brand files by Wednesday so we can stay on track for the Friday deadline?"
- "Let me know if Thursday works for the stakeholder list — happy to adjust if you need more time."
- "We'll need the staging access before we can start the integration work. Would Monday be feasible?"

**Setting check-in points:**
- "I'll check in on Thursday to see where things stand."
- "If anything changes on your end, just flag it and we'll adjust."
- "Let's plan to review progress at our Tuesday call."

**Phrases that escalate without confrontation:**
- "I know your calendar is packed — want me to loop in [colleague] to help coordinate?"
- "This item is blocking the next phase. Would it help to schedule a 15-minute call to sort it out?"
- "I want to make sure we hit the May 20 deadline — is there anything on your side that might push the timeline?"
