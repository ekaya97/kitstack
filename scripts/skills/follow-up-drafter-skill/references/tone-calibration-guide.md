# Tone Calibration Guide

How to match email tone to the specific context of the meeting and relationship. Tone is not a binary choice between "formal" and "casual" — it's a matrix of relationship, outcome, hierarchy, and culture.

---

## 1. Tone by Relationship Stage

### New Prospect / First Meeting
- **Register:** Professional, slightly formal
- **Assumptions:** Zero — restate context, don't assume shared knowledge
- **Language:** "As you mentioned during our call..." / "Based on what you shared about..."
- **Sign-off:** "Looking forward to continuing the conversation." / "Please don't hesitate to reach out with any questions."
- **Avoid:** Nicknames, abbreviations, humor, overly casual closings ("Cheers!")

### Existing Client (Active Engagement)
- **Register:** Warm, direct, efficient
- **Assumptions:** Shared context exists — no need to restate everything
- **Language:** "Great progress today." / "Here's where we landed." / "Quick recap:"
- **Sign-off:** "Talk soon." / "Let me know if anything looks off."
- **Avoid:** Over-formality (it signals distance when closeness exists), restating things they already know

### Long-Term Client / Close Colleague
- **Register:** Conversational, collaborative
- **Assumptions:** High — you share vocabulary, history, and trust
- **Language:** "Quick one from today's call:" / "Three things to lock down:"
- **Sign-off:** "Thanks, [first name]" / no sign-off needed if it's rapid-fire
- **Avoid:** Stiff structure when a 5-sentence email would do

### Internal Team Member
- **Register:** Clear, no fluff, task-oriented
- **Assumptions:** Full shared context — skip the pleasantries
- **Language:** "Decisions from today:" / "Action items below." / "Need by Friday:"
- **Sign-off:** Initials or nothing
- **Avoid:** Corporate phrasing that no one uses in person. If you wouldn't say "please find below" out loud, don't write it.

## 2. Tone by Meeting Outcome

### Positive / Productive Meeting
- **Energy:** Forward-looking, optimistic, action-oriented
- **Opening:** Reference a highlight or breakthrough moment: "The approach you outlined for the Q4 launch is sharp — here's how we'll build on it."
- **Body:** Emphasize momentum: "With today's decisions locked in, here's what happens next."
- **Close:** Confident next step: "I'll have the revised plan to you by Thursday."
- **Avoid:** Over-enthusiasm that sounds performative. "What an amazing meeting!" feels hollow. Specific praise lands better: "Your insight about the onboarding funnel reframed the whole approach."

### Neutral / Exploratory Meeting
- **Energy:** Professional, open, non-committal (in a good way)
- **Opening:** Reference the purpose: "Good to dig into the options for the data migration today."
- **Body:** Summarize what was explored without forcing conclusions: "We discussed three approaches — here's a summary of each with trade-offs."
- **Close:** Keep doors open: "Let us know which direction you'd like to explore further, and we'll put together a detailed plan."
- **Avoid:** Pushing toward a decision that wasn't made. If the meeting was exploratory, the follow-up should be too.

### Difficult / Tense Meeting
- **Energy:** Measured, factual, solution-oriented
- **Opening:** Acknowledge the difficulty without dwelling: "I appreciate the candid conversation today about the timeline concerns."
- **Body:** Focus on facts and agreed actions, not emotions: "We identified three areas where the timeline is at risk. Here's the revised approach we discussed."
- **Close:** Reaffirm commitment: "We're committed to getting this back on track. I'll send the updated plan by end of day tomorrow."
- **Avoid:** Editorializing ("The meeting was tough but productive"), assigning blame ("As you acknowledged, the delays were on your side"), or being defensive. Let the facts and actions speak.

### Bad News Delivery
- **Energy:** Direct, empathetic, forward-looking
- **Opening:** State the news clearly in the first two sentences. Don't bury it.
- **Body:** Explain the cause briefly (one paragraph max), then pivot to the path forward: "Here's what we're doing to address this."
- **Close:** Specific next step and timeline: "I'll share the revised timeline by tomorrow afternoon. Happy to jump on a call if you'd like to discuss."
- **Avoid:** Over-apologizing (one clear apology is enough), long explanations before the bad news (readers sense the stalling), and vague remediation ("We'll do better going forward").

## 3. Tone by Hierarchy

### Writing to Senior Executives (CEO, VP, Board)
- **Structure:** Inverted pyramid — most important information first
- **Length:** As short as possible. If the email is more than 200 words, it's too long for a C-level reader.
- **Content priority:** Decisions, outcomes, risks. Not process or methodology.
- **Format:** Short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max), bullet points for decisions, one action item at the end
- **Language:** "The decision is..." / "The risk is..." / "We recommend..." / "Next step:"
- **Avoid:** Background context they already have, methodological details, hedge words ("perhaps," "potentially," "we think maybe")

### Writing to Lateral Peers
- **Structure:** Standard follow-up structure
- **Length:** Match the complexity of the meeting (150-400 words)
- **Content priority:** Balanced — context, decisions, and actions
- **Format:** Conversational but structured
- **Language:** "Here's what we covered..." / "The main takeaways:" / "I'll own X, you're on Y."
- **Avoid:** Talking down (over-explaining what they were present for) or talking up (being overly deferential to an equal)

### Writing to Direct Reports / Junior Team Members
- **Structure:** Clear instructions, then context (why, not just what)
- **Length:** Enough to be unambiguous — err on the side of more detail, not less
- **Content priority:** What needs to happen, by when, to what standard
- **Format:** Numbered action items with deadlines, then a paragraph of context
- **Language:** "Here's what I need from you:" / "Please have X ready by [date]." / "Happy to hop on a call if any of this is unclear."
- **Avoid:** Vagueness (they need clarity, not ambiguity), micromanagement disguised as "context," CC'ing their manager on routine follow-ups

## 4. Tone by Cultural Context

### German Formal (DACH Region)
- **Greeting:** "Sehr geehrte/r [Herr/Frau Lastname]" for first contact; "Liebe/r [Herr/Frau Lastname]" once relationship is established. In English: use "Dear Mr./Ms. [Lastname]" until invited to use first names.
- **Structure:** Precise and thorough. German business culture values completeness — include all relevant details.
- **Action items:** Be explicit and specific. Ambiguity is not valued.
- **Closing:** "Mit freundlichen Gruessen" / "Best regards" — don't get casual until they do.
- **Key principle:** Formality is respect. Premature informality is read as unprofessional.

### US Professional
- **Greeting:** First names from the first meeting. "Hi [Firstname],"
- **Structure:** Concise, action-oriented, positive framing
- **Action items:** Clear but can be woven into prose rather than rigid tables
- **Closing:** "Best," / "Thanks," / "Looking forward to it."
- **Key principle:** Friendly efficiency. Warm but not slow. Get to the point, but don't be cold.

### UK Professional
- **Greeting:** First names after introduction, but tone slightly more reserved than US
- **Structure:** Measured, slightly understated. Less exclamation marks than US style.
- **Action items:** Clear, often phrased as suggestions even when mandatory ("It would be helpful if..." = "Please do this")
- **Closing:** "Kind regards," / "Best wishes,"
- **Key principle:** Politeness signals competence. Read between the lines — British understatement is real.

### Startup / Tech Casual
- **Greeting:** First name, no greeting needed in thread replies
- **Structure:** Short, scannable, emojis acceptable in moderation
- **Action items:** Bold or tagged (@person) in collaborative tools, brief in email
- **Closing:** "Thanks!" / "LMK" / "Talk soon" / just the name
- **Key principle:** Speed and clarity over formality. Nobody reads long emails.

### Nordic Professional
- **Greeting:** First names immediately, minimal titles
- **Structure:** Direct and egalitarian. Avoid hierarchical language.
- **Action items:** Consensus-oriented: "We agreed that..." rather than "I'm assigning you..."
- **Closing:** "Best," / "Regards," — straightforward
- **Key principle:** Flat hierarchy even in communication. Don't over-differentiate by rank.
