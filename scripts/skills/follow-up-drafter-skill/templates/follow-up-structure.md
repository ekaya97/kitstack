# Follow-Up Email Structure Template

Use this skeleton for every follow-up email. Adjust section weight based on the meeting type. Delete inline instructions before outputting.

---

**Subject:** [Specific topic] — [content type: recap, action items, next steps] [+ optional date or context]

<!-- Subject line rules:
- Never generic ("Meeting follow-up" or "Great chat today")
- Include the topic and what the email contains
- Under 60 characters if possible
- Examples:
  - "Follow-up: Q3 launch plan — decisions + action items"
  - "Greenline scheduling project — initial thoughts + next steps"
  - "Project Atlas — Week 3 update: on track, one blocker"
-->

---

Hi [Name],

<!-- Opening line: 1-2 sentences. Rules:
- Reference something SPECIFIC from the meeting — a quote, a data point, a moment
- This proves the email is personal, not a template
- Never start with: "I hope this email finds you well" / "Thanks for your time" / "Per our conversation"

Good examples:
- "Your point about the 40% drop-off at onboarding step 3 reframed the whole approach."
- "The question Priya raised about offline mode was exactly the right concern to surface."
- "Good to align on the self-serve direction today — here's where we landed."

Bad examples:
- "Thank you for taking the time to meet with us today."
- "It was great to connect and learn more about your company."
- "Following up on our productive meeting earlier."
-->

[Opening line]

<!-- Context recap: 2-3 sentences. Orientation for the reader.
- What was the meeting about?
- What was the goal?
- This is for skimmers and for people CC'd who weren't in the meeting
- NOT a transcript — just enough to frame what follows

Example: "We met to review the Phase 2 timeline and address the two open blockers from last week. Here's what we decided and what happens next."
-->

[Context recap]

**Key takeaways:**

<!-- Decisions and takeaways: 3-7 bullets.
- Use past tense for decisions: "We agreed to..." / "The team confirmed..."
- Use present tense for facts: "The current timeline is..." / "Budget stands at..."
- Each bullet should be self-contained — readable without the others
- Order by importance, not chronology
- If a decision was NOT made, say so explicitly: "Pricing is not finalized — we'll test two options and decide after the pilot."
-->

- [Decision or takeaway 1]
- [Decision or takeaway 2]
- [Decision or takeaway 3]

**Action items:**

<!-- Action items: Table for 4+ items, grouped list for 2-3 items, inline for 1-2 items.

TABLE FORMAT:
| Action | Owner | Deadline |
|--------|-------|----------|
| [Specific, verifiable action] | [First name] | [Specific date] |

GROUPED FORMAT:
**What I'll do:**
- [Action + deadline]

**What we need from your side:**
- [Action + deadline]

Rules:
- Every item has a named owner (not a team)
- Every item has a specific deadline (not "soon" or "ASAP")
- Your items first, then theirs
- If deadline wasn't set in the meeting, propose one
- "Me" for your own items
-->

| Action | Owner | Deadline |
|--------|-------|----------|
| [Action 1] | [Owner] | [Date] |
| [Action 2] | [Owner] | [Date] |
| [Action 3] | [Owner] | [Date] |

<!-- Open questions / parking lot: OPTIONAL. Only include if there were unresolved items.
- Frame as "we'll address these next" — not as forgotten items
- Assign prep work where possible so they get resolved at the next meeting
- Skip this section entirely if everything was resolved
-->

**Open questions:**
- [Unresolved item + suggested path to resolution]

<!-- Next steps: 1-3 sentences. Specific.
- What happens next?
- When does it happen?
- Who initiates it?

Good: "I'll send the revised timeline by Thursday. Let's plan to review it on our Friday call."
Bad: "Let's stay in touch." / "Looking forward to next steps."
-->

[Next steps]

<!-- Closing: Match the relationship and formality level.

FORMAL / NEW RELATIONSHIP:
"Please don't hesitate to reach out if any questions come up before then."
"Looking forward to continuing the conversation."

PROFESSIONAL / ACTIVE ENGAGEMENT:
"Let me know if anything looks off."
"Talk soon."

CASUAL / CLOSE COLLEAGUE:
"Thanks, [name]"
[or no closing at all for internal threads]

SIGN-OFF OPTIONS by context:
- "Best," — universal safe choice
- "Thanks," — when they did something helpful
- "Talk soon," — when a follow-up call is expected
- "Best regards," — slightly more formal, good for DACH/UK
- [Name only] — internal, casual

NEVER: "Warmly," (too personal for business) / "Cheers," (unless you're British or Australian) / "Respectfully," (too formal unless military/government)
-->

[Closing],
[Your Name]

## Section Weight by Meeting Type

Not every section needs equal weight. Adjust based on what matters:

| Meeting Type | Heavy Sections | Light / Skip |
|-------------|---------------|-------------|
| Client discovery | Opening, key takeaways, next steps | Action items (usually few at this stage) |
| Internal planning | Decisions, action items | Opening (skip the pleasantries), closing |
| Sales demo | Opening (anchor to positive moment), answers to questions, CTA | Formal action table (weave actions into prose) |
| Vendor/partner | Agreed terms, outstanding items | Context recap (both parties know) |
| Status check-in | Progress update, blockers, next check-in | Decisions (usually none to report) |
| Kickoff | Roles, communication plan, first milestones | Key takeaways (it's a kickoff, not a strategy meeting) |

## Length Guidelines

| Email Type | Target Word Count | Rule of Thumb |
|-----------|------------------|---------------|
| Quick check-in follow-up | 75-150 words | Fits in one phone screen |
| Standard meeting follow-up | 150-300 words | 1-2 minute read |
| Complex meeting with many action items | 300-500 words | Maximum for most contexts |
| Multi-party meeting with parking lot items | 400-600 words | Only when necessary |

If the email exceeds 500 words, consider: (a) splitting into two emails (external + internal), (b) moving detailed items to an attachment, or (c) cutting the context recap shorter — the people who were there don't need it.
