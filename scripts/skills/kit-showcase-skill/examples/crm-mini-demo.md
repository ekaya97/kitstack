# Example: CRM Mini-Demo

## Context

User asks to try a kit and chooses CRM. They provide their own contact data.

---

## Conversation

**Assistant:** Let's try the CRM kit. Give me 2-3 people you work with — name, company, and how you know them.

**User:** Okay. There's Anna Richter at Wellspring Digital — she's a potential client I met last month at a networking event. David Liu at Kora Labs — he's an active client, I'm doing a website for him. And Priya Patel — she's a freelance copywriter I sometimes collaborate with.

**Assistant:** Got it. When did you last talk to each of them?

**User:** Anna was maybe 3 weeks ago, right after the event. David was yesterday. And Priya... probably two months? We finished a project together and haven't connected since.

**Assistant:** Here's your contact board:

| Name | Company | Relationship | Last Contact |
|------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Anna Richter | Wellspring Digital | Potential client, met at networking event | 3 weeks ago |
| David Liu | Kora Labs | Active client, website project | Yesterday |
| Priya Patel | Freelance copywriter | Collaboration partner | ~2 months ago |

Three contacts. Now let me work with this.

**Follow-up priorities for this week:**

1. **Priya Patel** — Two months is a long gap for a collaboration partner. She's the kind of relationship that brings you work when it's warm and disappears when it's cold. Suggestion: reach out with something low-effort. "Hey Priya, haven't caught up in a while — anything interesting on your plate?" keeps the door open without being forced.

2. **Anna Richter** — Three weeks post-event is the outer edge of the follow-up window. If you wait another week, the connection goes cold. She's a potential client, so the follow-up should be value-forward — not "just checking in" but "I came across this [relevant thing] and thought of your conversation about [topic from the event]."

3. **David Liu** — No action needed. You're in active project mode. Keep the cadence.

**User:** That's actually really useful. I totally forgot about Priya.

**Assistant:** That's the exact scenario this is built for. Now here's the thing.

Everything we just did — Anna, David, Priya, the follow-up logic — lives in this conversation. When this conversation ends, I won't remember any of them. If you start a new chat tomorrow and ask "who should I follow up with?" I'll have no idea.

Imagine instead you'd been logging contacts like this for 3 months. You'd have 30, 50, maybe 80 contacts. You could ask:
- "Who haven't I talked to in more than a month?"
- "Show me everyone tagged as a lead"
- "Who referred me the most work this year?"
- "I'm going to the Web Summit — who do I know who'll be there?"

That's what the CRM kit does. It stores your contacts in a real database. Every conversation builds on the last. After a few weeks, it becomes a tool that actually knows your network.

**User:** How does it look? Is it just text or is there a visual?

**Assistant:** You get both. When you ask questions, you get text answers like what I just gave you. But the kit also has views — a dashboard showing all your contacts with recent interactions, and a detail page for each person with their full history. Those render right here in the chat as visual interfaces.

If you're interested, you can find the CRM kit at KitStack. But the core idea is what you just experienced: add contacts naturally, get smart follow-up suggestions, and have it all persist.

**User:** Cool. I'll check it out.

**Assistant:** Sounds good. In the meantime — don't forget to message Priya this week.
