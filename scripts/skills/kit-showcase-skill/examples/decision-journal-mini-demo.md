# Example: Decision Journal Mini-Demo

## Context

User is curious about kits and picks the decision journal. They share a real decision they're currently facing.

---

## Conversation

**Assistant:** Let's try the Decision Journal. Think of a decision you're facing or one you recently made. What was it, what options are you considering, and what's your current leaning?

**User:** I'm trying to decide whether to take on a big new client. They're a well-known brand — great for my portfolio — but the project would take 80% of my capacity for 3 months. I'd have to pause or drop my other 2 smaller clients.

**Assistant:** Good one. Let me structure this.

**Decision:** Accept large client project (well-known brand, 3-month engagement)

**Context:** The project would consume 80% of capacity, requiring you to pause or drop 2 existing smaller clients.

Let me make sure I have the options. What are you weighing?

**User:** Option A is take the project and pause the smaller clients — tell them I'm at capacity and will come back. Option B is decline the big project and keep my current setup. Option C, which I haven't fully thought through, is try to do all of it somehow.

**Assistant:** Let me log this:

---

**Decision:** Accept large brand client project
**Date:** May 7, 2026

**Options:**
| Option | Description | Pros | Cons |
|--------|-------------|------|------|
| A: Take it, pause small clients | Accept the brand project. Inform existing clients you're unavailable for 3 months. | Portfolio prestige. Higher revenue (likely). Career signal. | Risk losing 2 existing clients permanently. All eggs in one basket. |
| B: Decline | Keep current client mix. | Stability. Maintained relationships. Diversified income. | Miss the portfolio opportunity. Potential regret. |
| C: Do everything | Accept the brand project while maintaining existing clients. | Maximum revenue. No relationships lost. | 80% + 40% = 120% capacity. Quality risk on all fronts. Burnout. |

**Your leaning:** Sounds like you're drawn to Option A but worried about the existing clients.

---

Now let me analyze this.

**Option C should probably come off the table.** Running at 120% capacity for 3 months reliably degrades quality. If the big brand project gets subpar work because you're stretched, you've damaged the portfolio opportunity that was the whole reason to take it. And if your smaller clients get worse service, you lose them anyway — plus they leave with a bad impression instead of "she was honest about capacity."

That leaves A vs. B. Here's the real question: **What happens to the small clients if you pause them?**

- If they're the kind of clients who will wait 3 months — Option A is strong. You get the portfolio win and come back to a stable base.
- If they'll find another freelancer in your absence — Option A means trading 2 ongoing relationships for 1 time-limited project. The math only works if the brand project leads to more work at that level.

**What I'd push on:** Have you talked to the smaller clients? "I have a 3-month opportunity and want to be transparent — can we pause and resume?" might get a better answer than you expect. Most reasonable clients respect honesty about capacity.

**Confidence check:** On a scale of 1-10, how confident are you in your leaning?

**User:** Maybe 6. The portfolio upside is real but I'm anxious about losing the reliable income.

**Assistant:** 6/10 is honest. That's "leaning toward it but not convinced."

Let me record this:

**Current leaning:** Option A (take the project, pause small clients)
**Confidence:** 6/10
**Key uncertainty:** Whether small clients will be there when you return
**Decision deadline:** When do you need to decide by?

**User:** I told them I'd let them know by Friday.

**Assistant:** Okay. Before Friday, I'd suggest one thing: **have the conversation with your existing clients.** Their response will move your confidence from 6 to either 8 (they'll wait) or 4 (they won't). Right now you're deciding based on a fear that might not be real.

Also worth asking the brand client: **Is there flexibility on the 80% capacity requirement?** Could the project be scoped to 60% with a slightly longer timeline? That might let you keep one of the two smaller clients running. It doesn't hurt to ask.

**Suggested review date:** June 7 — one month into the project (whichever you choose), check in on how it's going and whether the decision feels right.

---

**User:** This is actually super helpful. I didn't think to ask about reducing the scope.

**Assistant:** That's what structured decision-making does. When you write down the options and constraints explicitly, gaps in your thinking become visible.

Now here's the thing about this decision log.

Right now, I know you're facing this choice. I know your options, your confidence level, your reasoning, and that Friday is your deadline. If you come back on June 7 for a review, I could ask: "How did the decision play out? Was your 6/10 confidence justified?"

But I won't remember any of this. New conversation, blank slate.

A decision journal kit stores every decision you log. After a few months, you'd have a record of how you think:

- "When I'm at 6/10 confidence, how often does the decision work out?" (Most people discover that 6/10 decisions succeed about 50% of the time — which means the uncertainty was real, not just anxiety.)
- "Do I tend to overvalue prestige opportunities?" (Looking at past decisions about brand-name vs. steady clients.)
- "What types of decisions do I second-guess most?" (Reversible ones? Financial ones? People decisions?)

One decision is a note. Fifty decisions is a map of how your mind works. But that only works if the data persists.

**User:** That's a really interesting idea. I've always wanted to keep a decision journal but never stuck with it.

**Assistant:** The sticking problem is real. Paper journals require discipline. Spreadsheets feel clinical. The advantage of a kit is that logging a decision is just a conversation — "I decided to take the project, here's why" — and the kit structures and stores it automatically. The barrier to entry is just talking.

The Decision Journal kit stores everything, gives you a timeline view of all your decisions, and surfaces patterns over time. It also sets review reminders — so your June 7 check-in would actually happen.

**User:** Will do. Thanks.

**Assistant:** Good luck with it. And whatever you decide — log it somewhere. Future you will want to know why.
