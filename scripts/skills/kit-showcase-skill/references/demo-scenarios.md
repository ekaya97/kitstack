## CRM Demo Script

### Phase 1: Quick Win

**Prompt:** "Give me 2-3 people you work with. Name, company, and how you know them."

**If they provide data:** Use it. Add a "Last Contact" column by asking "Roughly when did you last talk to each?"

**If they want sample data:**

| Name | Company | Relationship | Last Contact |
|------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Leah Park | Riviera Health | Warm lead, interested in Q3 launch | 2 weeks ago |
| Marcus Weber | TechBridge GmbH | Active client, monthly retainer | This week |
| Nina Schulz | Freelance designer | Collaboration partner, referred 2 clients | 6 weeks ago |
| James Okonkwo | Altitude Ventures | Investor contact from demo day | 3 months ago |

**After entering:** "Got it. 4 contacts, all at different stages. Let me do something useful with this."

### Phase 2: Smart Query

"Based on your contacts, here's what I'd prioritize this week:

**Immediate follow-ups:**

1. **Nina Schulz** — 6 weeks since last contact. She's referred you 2 clients, which makes her one of your most valuable relationships. A quick check-in now maintains the referral pipeline. Suggestion: send a brief message asking what she's working on, or share something relevant to her work.

2. **James Okonkwo** — 3 months is a long silence for an investor contact. If you're not actively raising, a quarterly touchpoint keeps you on his radar for when you are. Suggestion: share a milestone or interesting article, not an ask.

**No action needed:**
- **Marcus Weber** — Active client, spoke this week. Keep the cadence.
- **Leah Park** — 2 weeks is fine for a warm lead, but don't let it go past 3. If she mentioned a Q3 launch timeline, mid-May is a good moment to reconnect with something specific to her launch."

### Phase 3: Memory Moment

"Now imagine you've been using this for 3 months. You have 80 contacts, 200+ logged interactions. You could ask things like:

- 'Who referred me the most clients this year?' — and get Nina's name instantly
- 'Show me everyone I met at the Web Summit' — tagged and searchable
- 'I'm going to Munich next week — who do I know there?' — filtered by location
- 'Which leads went cold in the last 30 days?' — automatic follow-up list

Right now, this only lives here. When this conversation ends, I won't remember Leah, Marcus, Nina, or James. Your follow-up list disappears. A kit changes that — the data persists, and every interaction makes it smarter."

### Phase 4: Bridge

"The CRM kit does everything we just did, but it sticks. Plus you get a visual dashboard showing all your contacts and a detail view for each person — right here in the chat. No separate app, no tab switching."

## Expenses Demo Script

### Phase 1: Quick Win

**Prompt:** "Let's log a few work expenses. What did you spend money on this week for work? Or I can use sample data."

**If they want sample data:**

| Date | Description | Amount | Category |
|------|-------------|--------|----------|
| May 1 | Monthly coworking membership | EUR 250 | Office |
| May 2 | Figma Pro (annual, billed monthly) | EUR 12 | Software |
| May 3 | Coffee meeting with potential client | EUR 14 | Client entertainment |
| May 5 | Train ticket to Hamburg for workshop | EUR 67 | Travel |
| May 5 | Workshop lunch | EUR 18 | Meals |
| May 6 | Adobe Creative Cloud | EUR 60 | Software |
| May 7 | Domain renewal (portfolio site) | EUR 12 | Software |

**After entering:** "7 expenses logged. Let me crunch this."

### Phase 2: Smart Query

"Here's your week:

| Category | Total | Items |
|----------|-------|-------|
| Office | EUR 250 | 1 (coworking) |
| Software | EUR 84 | 3 (Figma, Adobe, domain) |
| Travel | EUR 67 | 1 (Hamburg train) |
| Meals | EUR 18 | 1 (workshop lunch) |
| Client entertainment | EUR 14 | 1 (coffee meeting) |
| **Total** | **EUR 433** | **7 expenses** |

**What stands out:**
- Coworking is 58% of your weekly spend. That's EUR 3,000/year just on workspace. Worth evaluating if you go every day or if a flexible plan would save money.
- You're running 3 software subscriptions. Together they're EUR 84/month = EUR 1,008/year. If you only use Figma daily and Adobe weekly, there might be an opportunity to switch to a cheaper plan or find an alternative for lighter-use tools.
- Travel and meals for the Hamburg workshop: EUR 85 combined. If this was a client workshop, it's a deductible business expense.

If you're tracking for taxes: everything here except possibly the coffee meeting is straightforwardly deductible as a freelance business expense in Germany."

### Phase 3: Memory Moment

"Now imagine this over a full month. Or a quarter. You'd see:

- Monthly spending trends ('Am I spending more this month than last?')
- Category breakdowns over time ('My software costs went from EUR 84 to EUR 120 — what changed?')
- Budget tracking ('I set EUR 400/month for tools and I'm at EUR 380')
- Tax-ready totals ('Show me all travel expenses for 2026' — instant answer for your Steuerberater)

Right now, these 7 expenses live in this conversation only. If you logged next week's expenses in a new chat, I wouldn't know about this week. The expense kit stores everything persistently. After a few months, it becomes your financial memory."

### Phase 4: Bridge

"The Expense kit gives you a monthly overview dashboard with charts — spending by category, daily trends, budget versus actual. Everything logged with natural language, no spreadsheet formatting required."

## Decision Journal Demo Script

### Phase 1: Quick Win

**Prompt:** "Think of a decision you made recently. What was it, what options did you consider, and why did you choose what you chose?"

**If they want sample data:**

"Let me log a sample decision:

**Decision:** Hire a part-time virtual assistant or keep doing admin myself
**Date:** May 3, 2026
**Context:** Spending ~8 hours/week on scheduling, invoicing, and email management. My hourly rate on client work is EUR 130. Admin hours are effectively costing EUR 1,040/week in opportunity cost.

**Options considered:**
1. **Hire a VA (15 hrs/week, EUR 20/hr = EUR 1,200/month)** — Frees up 8 hours for client work. Net gain: ~EUR 3,000/month after VA cost. Risk: onboarding time, quality of work.
2. **Automate with tools instead** — Invest in Calendly, accounting software, email templates. One-time cost ~EUR 500. Saves maybe 3-4 hours, not 8.
3. **Keep doing it myself** — No cost, no risk, but continues the opportunity cost drain.

**Choice:** Option 1 — Hire the VA
**Reasoning:** The math is clear. Even if the VA only frees up 5 hours (not 8), the ROI is positive. Automation handles some tasks but not scheduling coordination or client follow-up.
**Confidence:** 8/10
**Review date:** June 3 — check if the VA has actually freed up the expected hours"

### Phase 2: Smart Query

"Let me analyze this decision:

**Decision quality assessment:**
- **Reasoning structure:** Strong. You quantified the opportunity cost, compared options with specific numbers, and identified the risk (onboarding time).
- **Confidence calibration:** 8/10 is appropriate given the clear math and defined risk.
- **Blind spots to watch:** You didn't factor in management overhead — managing a VA takes time too. If onboarding plus ongoing management takes 3 hours/week, your net time savings drops from 8 to 5 hours. Still positive, but worth monitoring.

**The review date is smart.** One month is the right check-in window. When June 3 arrives, the key questions are:
1. How many hours did the VA actually free up?
2. What did you do with those hours? (Client work = ROI realized. Netflix = ROI theoretical.)
3. How much time are you spending managing the VA?

If the answers are favorable, increase VA hours. If not, diagnose whether it's a training issue or a fit issue before deciding to continue.

**Pattern note:** This decision shows a bias toward quantitative analysis — you led with the numbers. That's a strength for financial decisions but might be a blind spot for decisions where the key factor is qualitative (team dynamics, creative direction, personal energy)."

### Phase 3: Memory Moment

"Now imagine you've logged 20 decisions over 3 months. You'd be able to see:

- **Confidence calibration:** 'When I'm at 8/10 confidence, how often am I right? When I'm at 5/10?' Most people discover their confidence is poorly calibrated — they're overconfident on some decision types and underconfident on others.
- **Decision patterns:** 'Do I tend toward compromise? Do I avoid irreversible decisions? Do I decide faster under pressure or slower?'
- **Outcome tracking:** 'What happened with the VA decision? What about the pricing decision from March? The partnership decision from February?'
- **Regret analysis:** 'Which decisions do I revisit or second-guess most? Is there a pattern in what I regret?'

The journal gets more valuable with every entry. But right now, this one decision lives only in this conversation. A decision journal kit would store it, remind you to review it on June 3, and connect it to every future decision you log."

### Phase 4: Bridge

"The Decision Journal kit stores every decision persistently. It gives you a timeline view of all your decisions and a pattern analysis view that shows trends in your decision-making over time. The June 3 review date? The kit would surface it automatically."
