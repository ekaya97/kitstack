# Example: Story Post

## User Input

> I'm a management consultant. Last month I accidentally sent a client the wrong financial model — it was the model for a different client. The client called me, and instead of being angry, she pointed out that the model I accidentally sent was more sophisticated than the one I'd built for her, and asked why she was getting a simpler version. It turned into an awkward but really productive conversation about scope and pricing.

---

## Post Analysis

**Post type:** Story
**Hook strategy:** Confession (Pattern #3) — admitting a professional mistake is disarming
**Target audience:** Consultants, freelancers, professional services
**Goal:** Trust + engagement (showing vulnerability that leads to a useful insight)

---

## Generated Post

Last month I sent a client the wrong financial model.

Not a minor mix-up. I sent her the model I'd built for a completely different client. Different industry. Different assumptions. Much more sophisticated.

She called me within an hour.

I was bracing for anger. Instead, she asked: "Why does this other company get a Monte Carlo simulation and scenario analysis, and I get a basic P&L projection?"

Fair question. Painful answer.

She was getting a simpler model because she was paying for a simpler scope. But I'd never shown her what the more sophisticated version looked like. She didn't know it existed. In her mind, my basic model was my best work.

We spent the next 30 minutes redesigning her engagement. She upgraded to the full financial modeling package. Her project value went from EUR 8,000 to EUR 22,000.

The lesson I keep thinking about: most clients are buying based on what they know is possible. They can't ask for the Monte Carlo simulation if they don't know you do Monte Carlo simulations.

Your proposal doesn't just sell the project. It frames what's even available. And most of us default to offering less than we can do — because we're afraid of the price conversation.

Sometimes the best sales tool is an accidental email.

## Why This Post Works

1. **Hook is a confession** — admitting a mistake immediately builds trust and curiosity
2. **Real story with specific details** — Monte Carlo simulation, EUR 8K to 22K, the exact question the client asked
3. **The lesson is non-obvious** — it's not "proofread your emails" (the obvious lesson) but rather "clients buy based on what they know is available"
4. **The closing line is memorable** — "the best sales tool is an accidental email" is quotable
5. **Natural voice** — reads like someone telling a story, not writing a LinkedIn post
6. **Shows vulnerability without being performative** — the mistake is real, the lesson is practical, there's no "this is so scary to share"
