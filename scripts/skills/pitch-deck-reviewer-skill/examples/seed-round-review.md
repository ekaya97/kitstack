# Example: Seed Round Deck Review

## User Input

> I'm raising a $1.5M seed round for my startup, InvoiceBot. We automate invoicing for freelancers. Here's my 10-slide deck:
>
> Slide 1: InvoiceBot — "Invoicing on Autopilot" — Logo, name, tagline
> Slide 2: About Us — Founded in 2025, team of 3, based in Berlin
> Slide 3: The Problem — Freelancers spend 5+ hours/month on invoicing. Most use Word or Excel. It's tedious, error-prone, and they send invoices late, which means they get paid late.
> Slide 4: Our Solution — InvoiceBot connects to your time tracker and bank account. It generates invoices automatically, sends them on schedule, and follows up on overdue payments.
> Slide 5: How It Works — Screenshot of the dashboard. Time tracking integration → Invoice generation → Auto-send → Payment tracking → Auto-follow-up
> Slide 6: Market — The global invoicing software market is $12.2B
> Slide 7: Business Model — Freemium. Free for up to 5 invoices/month, $19/month for unlimited, $49/month for teams
> Slide 8: Traction — 800 users, 120 paying customers, $4,200 MRR, launched 4 months ago
> Slide 9: Competition — Table: InvoiceBot vs. FreshBooks vs. Xero vs. Wave. We win on automation and simplicity.
> Slide 10: The Ask — Raising $1.5M. Use of funds: 50% Engineering, 30% Marketing, 20% Operations

## Generated Review

# Deck Review: InvoiceBot Seed Pitch

## Overall Assessment

**Score: 5.5/10 — Needs Work**

The foundation is there — you have a real problem, a working product, and early traction. But the deck undersells your strengths, skips critical elements, and makes structural mistakes that weaken the pitch. The good news: the fixes are specific and achievable. The story is right; the execution needs tightening.

---

## Narrative Evaluation

### What works:
The problem is relatable. Anyone who has freelanced or worked with freelancers immediately gets it. The solution is clear and concrete — "connects to time tracker, generates invoices, follows up" is easy to understand.

### What's missing:
- **No "Why Now" slide.** Why is this the moment for automated invoicing? Without it, an investor wonders: freelancers have suffered with invoicing for decades — why will they adopt a new tool now?
- **No team slide.** The "About Us" slide on position 2 is a bio dump in the wrong location. The team should come after traction, and it should answer "why are you the right people to build this?"
- **No financial projections.** At seed stage, investors want to see that you've modeled the next 18-24 months. Even rough numbers show you've thought about the path.

### Narrative arc:
The deck goes: "who we are → problem → solution → product → market → model → traction → competition → ask." The correct arc is: "problem → solution → product → traction → market → model → competition → team → financials → ask." Leading with "About Us" breaks the narrative — the audience should care about you AFTER they care about the problem.

## Top 3 Issues

### Issue 1: Missing "Why Now" (Critical)

Freelancer invoicing pain has existed for 20 years. FreshBooks launched in 2003. Why is InvoiceBot the right product now? Without answering this, sophisticated investors will pass.

**Possible "Why Now" angles:**
- AI makes it possible to auto-categorize time entries and generate context-aware invoices (wasn't possible 3 years ago)
- Open banking APIs enable direct payment tracking and automated reconciliation
- The freelance workforce grew 30% since 2020 — more freelancers means more invoicing pain
- E-invoicing mandates (EU, Germany 2025+) create regulatory urgency for compliant invoicing

Add a "Why Now" slide after the solution.

### Issue 2: Market Sizing Is Meaningless (High Impact)

"$12.2B global invoicing market" is a top-down number that includes enterprise invoicing, AP/AR platforms, and full accounting suites. You're not competing in that market.

**Rewrite the market slide:**
- Number of freelancers in target markets (EU + US): ~60 million
- Freelancers who invoice clients (not platform workers): ~20 million
- Freelancers who would pay $19/month for automation: ~5% adoption = 1 million
- SAM: 1 million x $228/year = $228M
- Your initial market (English + German-speaking freelancers): ~400K potential customers = $91M

This is smaller than $12.2B but infinitely more credible. And $91M is still a venture-scale opportunity.

### Issue 3: "About Us" Should Be a "Team" Slide, Repositioned (Medium Impact)

The team slide should come after traction (slide 9 or 10 position) and answer: "Why are you the right people to build this?"

**What to include:**
- Each founder's name, photo, and one line of relevant experience
- If someone has freelanced: "Was a freelancer for 5 years — built this because I hated invoicing"
- If someone has technical expertise: "Built billing infrastructure at [Company]"
- If someone has relevant domain knowledge: any fintech, payments, or accounting background

---

## Missing Elements

1. **"Why Now" slide** — Critical. Add after solution.
2. **Team slide (proper)** — Replace "About Us." Move to position 9.
3. **Financial projections** — Even simple: monthly burn, runway at current rate, projected MRR growth for 18 months.
4. **Retention / engagement data** — How often do active users log in? What's monthly churn? This shows product-market fit.
5. **Customer quote or testimonial** — One sentence from a real user adds credibility. Consider adding to the traction slide.

## Rewrite Suggestions

### Slide 1 (Title) — Rewrite

**Current:** InvoiceBot — "Invoicing on Autopilot"

**Proposed:**
```
InvoiceBot

800 freelancers. $4,200 MRR. 4 months.
Automated invoicing for the people who'd rather do the work than bill for it.
```

### Slide 6 (Market) — Rewrite

**Current:** The global invoicing software market is $12.2B

**Proposed:**
```
60M freelancers in the US + EU.
20M invoice clients regularly.
1M would pay $19/month to never touch an invoice again.

Our addressable market: $228M
Starting with: English + German-speaking freelancers ($91M)
```

### Slide 9 (Competition) — Rewrite

**Current:** Feature table where InvoiceBot wins every column

**Proposed:**
```
FreshBooks and Xero are accounting platforms. They're excellent if you want full double-entry bookkeeping.

Most freelancers don't. They want to finish the work and get paid.

InvoiceBot is not an accounting tool. It's an invoicing autopilot:
- Connects to your time tracker (they don't)
- Generates and sends invoices automatically (they require manual creation)
- Follows up on overdue payments without you asking (they send a notification you ignore)

We compete on one axis: how little the freelancer has to think about invoicing. On that axis, we win.
```

### Slide 10 (Ask) — Rewrite

**Current:** Raising $1.5M. Use: 50% Engineering, 30% Marketing, 20% Operations.

**Proposed:**
```
Raising $1.5M

This buys:
- 18 months of runway
- Engineering team from 2 → 5 (build integrations for top 10 time trackers)
- First marketing hire (content + community — our best channel is word-of-mouth)
- Target: $50K MRR and 5,000 paying customers by month 18

Current burn: $22K/month
Post-raise burn: $65K/month
Path to profitability: Month 14 at projected growth rate
```

3. Solution
4. How It Works (product)
5. Why Now (NEW)
6. Traction
7. Market (bottom-up)
8. Business Model (with unit economics)
9. Competition (honest positioning)
10. Team (with relevant experience)
11. Financial Projections (NEW)
12. The Ask (with milestones)

## Next Steps

1. **Rewrite slides 6 (Market) and 9 (Competition) — these are the weakest.** Use the rewrites above as starting points.
2. **Add a "Why Now" slide.** Pick one or two of the angles listed above. This is the highest-impact addition.
3. **Rebuild the "About Us" into a proper Team slide.** Move it to position 10.
4. **Run the headline test.** Read your new slide headlines in order. Do they tell the InvoiceBot story from problem to ask? If yes, the structure is solid.
5. **Practice the 3-minute version.** If you can pitch InvoiceBot in 3 minutes using only the headlines, the deck works.
