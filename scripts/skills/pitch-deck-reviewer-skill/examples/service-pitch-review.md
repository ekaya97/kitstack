>
> Slide 1: "GrowthLab — Your Growth Partner" — logo, tagline
> Slide 2: About GrowthLab — Founded 2020, 8-person team, offices in Berlin and Hamburg, 40+ clients served
> Slide 3: Our Services — Paid ads (Meta, Google), SEO, email marketing, content strategy, social media management
> Slide 4: Our Process — Discovery → Strategy → Execution → Optimization → Reporting (with icons)
> Slide 5: Case Study 1 — Fashion brand, grew revenue 200% in 6 months through Meta ads
> Slide 6: Case Study 2 — SaaS company, reduced CAC by 40% with Google Ads optimization
> Slide 7: Case Study 3 — Food delivery app, built email list to 50K subscribers
> Slide 8: The Team — 8 photos, names, titles
> Slide 9: Pricing — EUR 8,000/month retainer. Includes: paid ad management, email campaigns, monthly reporting
> Slide 10: Let's Talk — Contact info, scheduling link

## Generated Review

# Deck Review: GrowthLab Agency Pitch to KitchenPro

## Overall Assessment

**Score: 4/10 — Major Issues**

This deck is about GrowthLab. It should be about KitchenPro. The most critical problem: at no point in 10 slides do you demonstrate that you understand the prospect's business, their challenges, or their market. You're pitching capabilities, not solutions. An e-commerce company selling premium kitchen equipment has specific problems — this deck could be sent unchanged to a SaaS company, a fashion brand, or a restaurant chain. That's the issue.

---

## Narrative Evaluation

### Current narrative:
"Here's who we are. Here's what we do. Here's who we've helped. Here's our team. Here's the price. Let's talk."

### Needed narrative:
"Here's what we understand about your business. Here's the specific challenge you're facing. Here's how we'd solve it. Here's proof we've done similar work. Here's who would work on your account. Here's the investment and what you'd get. Here's what happens next."

The difference: the first is a brochure. The second is a pitch.

### Missing emotional arc:
The deck has no tension. A good agency pitch creates a moment where the prospect thinks: "They really understand our problem." That moment doesn't exist here because there's no problem slide.

## Top 3 Issues

### Issue 1: No Understanding of the Prospect's Business (Critical)

The deck has zero content about KitchenPro — their market, their customers, their challenges. Before the pitch meeting, you should research:
- Their current ad campaigns (are they running Meta/Google ads? What do they look like?)
- Their email marketing (sign up for their newsletter — what do they send?)
- Their competitive landscape (who else sells premium kitchen equipment online in Germany?)
- Their website (conversion rate, product range, average order value if estimable)

Add a slide after the title: "What We Understand About KitchenPro." Include 3-4 observations that show you've done your homework. This single slide will differentiate you from every other agency that sends a template deck.

### Issue 2: Case Studies Don't Match the Prospect (High Impact)

None of the three case studies are from e-commerce or kitchen/home/lifestyle brands. The prospect is thinking: "They've never worked with a business like mine."

**Fix options:**
- If you have e-commerce case studies: swap them in.
- If you don't: reframe the existing cases around the transferable challenge, not the industry. "This fashion brand had the same challenge you do: high average order value, long consideration period, and repeat purchase potential. Here's how we approached it."
- Best option: include a mini audit of KitchenPro's current marketing as a case study. "We analyzed your current Meta campaigns and found [3 specific opportunities]." This is more persuasive than any past case study.

### Issue 3: No Specific Strategy for the Prospect (High Impact)

The pitch shows your process but not your thinking. The prospect wants to know: "What would you actually DO for our business?"

Add a "Proposed Approach" slide:
- **Paid Ads:** "We'd focus on [Google Shopping for high-intent buyers] and [Meta retargeting for cart abandoners]. Your product price point (EUR 200-500) suggests a longer consideration period — we'd build a funnel, not a single-touch campaign."
- **Email Marketing:** "We'd build 3 automated flows: welcome sequence (convert first-time visitors), abandoned cart (recover lost revenue), post-purchase (recipe content → repeat purchase). These 3 flows alone should generate 15-25% of your email revenue."

This shows strategic thinking. It's what the prospect is buying — not "ad management" but "someone who understands my business and has a plan."

---

## Missing Elements

1. **Prospect-specific slide** — "What we understand about KitchenPro" (after title)
2. **Their challenge** — What problem are they trying to solve? Revenue growth? Customer acquisition cost? Repeat purchase rate? Show you know.
3. **Proposed strategy** — What would you actually do in months 1-3? Be specific.
4. **Expected outcomes** — "Based on similar engagements, we'd target: [X% ROAS improvement], [Y% email revenue contribution], [Z% reduction in CAC]." Ranges are fine. Specificity shows competence.
5. **Ad spend clarity** — Is the EUR 8,000 retainer on top of ad spend? What ad budget do you recommend? This must be addressed in the deck.

## Rewrite Suggestions

### Slide 1 (Title) — Rewrite

**Current:** "GrowthLab — Your Growth Partner"

**Proposed:**
```
Scaling KitchenPro's E-Commerce
Paid Advertising + Email Marketing Strategy

Prepared by GrowthLab | [Date]
```

### New Slide 2 — "What We See"

```
What We See in KitchenPro's Marketing

1. Your Google Shopping campaigns are driving traffic, but your Meta presence 
   is underutilizing retargeting — visitors leave and don't come back.

2. Your email signup offers 10% off, but there's no automated sequence after 
   the welcome email. You're leaving repeat revenue on the table.

3. Your average order value (EUR 280+) means a longer buyer journey. 
   Your current campaigns optimize for single-touch conversion — 
   a multi-touch funnel would perform significantly better.

These are fixable. Here's how we'd approach it.
```

This slide would have more impact than any case study in the deck. It proves you've done the work.

### Slide 8 (Team) — Rewrite

**Current:** 8 photos and names

**Proposed:**
```
Your Team at GrowthLab

Anna Becker — Paid Ads Lead
6 years managing Meta and Google campaigns for e-commerce brands. 
EUR 2M+ in managed ad spend.

Max Richter — Email & Automation
Built automated email flows for 15+ e-commerce companies. 
Specializes in post-purchase retention sequences.

Lisa Schmidt — Account Director (your day-to-day contact)
Manages 5 e-commerce accounts at GrowthLab. 
Weekly check-ins, monthly strategy reviews, always reachable.
```

Three people, not eight. Named, with relevant credentials. The prospect knows exactly who they're working with.

### Slide 9 (Pricing) — Rewrite

**Current:** EUR 8,000/month retainer

**Proposed:**
```
Your Investment: EUR 8,000/month

What's included:
- Paid ad management: Meta + Google campaigns, creative testing (4-6 variants/month), 
  bid optimization, audience building
- Email marketing: 3 automated flows (welcome, abandoned cart, post-purchase) + 
  4 campaign emails/month
- Reporting: Weekly Slack updates, monthly strategy call (60 min), 
  live dashboard with real-time metrics

Not included (on top):
- Ad spend (we recommend EUR 5,000-8,000/month to start, scaling based on ROAS)
- Creative production (photography, video — we provide briefs, you produce or we 
  connect you with our production partners)

Minimum commitment: 3 months (results in paid ads take 6-8 weeks to stabilize)
```

## Next Steps

1. **Research KitchenPro before the pitch.** Sign up for their newsletter. Review their ads (Meta Ad Library). Browse their site. Note 3-5 specific observations.
2. **Rewrite slides 1, 2, and 3** to center the prospect, not your agency.
3. **Replace or reframe case studies** for e-commerce relevance.
4. **Add the "Proposed Approach" slide.** This is the single highest-impact addition. It shows you have a plan, not just capabilities.
5. **Clarify pricing.** Separate retainer from ad spend. List what's included explicitly.
