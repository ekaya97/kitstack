# Deck Structure Patterns

Standard structures for four types of presentations. Use these as diagnostic tools — compare the user's deck against the pattern to identify missing elements, wrong ordering, and unnecessary slides.

---

## 1. Sequoia Format (Investor Pitch — Gold Standard)

The Sequoia format is the most widely recognized investor deck structure. Named after Sequoia Capital's recommended pitch structure. Works for seed through Series B.

### Slide Order

| Slide # | Title | Purpose | Key Content |
|---------|-------|---------|-------------|
| 1 | **Title** | Hook, establish credibility | Company name, one-line description, your name. Optional: a single impressive metric. |
| 2 | **Problem** | Create tension | Who has this problem? How painful is it? Quantify the cost of the problem. |
| 3 | **Solution** | Release tension | How you solve it. One sentence, clear and simple. Not features — outcome. |
| 4 | **Why Now** | Establish urgency | What changed in the market, technology, or regulation that makes this possible/necessary NOW? |
| 5 | **Market Size** | Show the opportunity | TAM/SAM/SOM with clear methodology. Bottom-up preferred over top-down. |
| 6 | **Product** | Demonstrate value | Screenshots, demo, or product walkthrough. Show, don't tell. |
| 7 | **Business Model** | Show how you make money | Revenue model, pricing, unit economics. LTV/CAC if available. |
| 8 | **Traction** | Prove it works | Key metrics: MRR, growth rate, user count, retention, notable customers. |
| 9 | **Competition** | Show market awareness | Honest competitive landscape. Your differentiation. NOT a 2x2 where you're alone in the top right. |
| 10 | **Team** | Reduce execution risk | Why this team? Relevant experience, domain expertise, previous exits. |
| 11 | **Financials** | Show the path | 3-year projections. Key assumptions. Path to profitability or next round. |
| 12 | **The Ask** | Close the loop | How much you're raising, what it buys (18 months of runway, key milestones), expected use of funds. |

### Sequoia Format Rules

1. **One idea per slide.** If you need two ideas on a slide, you need two slides.
2. **Every headline should be a claim, not a label.** "Market" is a label. "$4.2B market growing 30% annually" is a claim.
3. **Problem before solution. Always.** If the audience doesn't feel the problem, they won't care about the solution.
4. **"Why Now" is the most underrated slide.** It answers the implicit question: "If this is such a good idea, why hasn't someone done it already?" Good answers: new technology, regulatory change, behavioral shift, infrastructure maturity.
5. **Traction is the credibility slide.** Everything else is a story. Traction is proof. If you have it, put it early (after product). If you don't have traditional traction, show validation: waitlist, LOIs, pilot results, expert endorsements.

### Common Mistakes in Sequoia Format

| Mistake | Why it's a problem | Fix |
|---------|-------------------|-----|
| Putting the team slide first | Investors want to know the problem before they care about the team | Move team to slide 10 |
| TAM-only market sizing | "$500B market" without SAM/SOM is meaningless — every startup can claim a huge TAM | Show bottom-up: how many potential customers x average contract value |
| Competition slide with no competitors | Investors know there are always competitors. "No competitors" signals naivety. | Include direct, indirect, and status quo competitors |
| Financial projections that are hockey sticks | Every projection goes up and to the right. Show the assumptions behind the numbers. | Include key assumptions below the chart. Make them defensible. |
| Asking for money without milestones | "$2M" means nothing. "$2M to reach $500K MRR and hire the engineering team" means everything. | Tie the ask to specific milestones and a timeline |

## 2. Guy Kawasaki 10/20/30 Rule

A simplified format: 10 slides, 20 minutes, 30-point minimum font size.

### When to Use
- Demo Day presentations (strict time limits)
- First meetings with angels or early-stage investors
- When the audience is non-technical

### Slide Order

| Slide # | Title | Content |
|---------|-------|---------|
| 1 | Title | Company, tagline, presenter |
| 2 | Problem/Opportunity | The pain you're addressing |
| 3 | Value Proposition | How you solve it (one sentence) |
| 4 | Underlying Magic | Your secret sauce — technology, insight, approach |
| 5 | Business Model | How you make money |
| 6 | Go-to-Market | How you reach customers |
| 7 | Competitive Analysis | Who else, and why you win |
| 8 | Management Team | Key people and relevant backgrounds |
| 9 | Financial Projections | 3-year forecast with key metrics |
| 10 | Current Status & Ask | Where you are now, what you need |

### 10/20/30 Rules

- **10 slides maximum.** If you can't explain your business in 10 slides, you don't understand it well enough.
- **20 minutes.** Even if you have a 60-minute meeting, plan for 20 minutes of presentation and 40 minutes of conversation.
- **30-point font minimum.** Forces you to have less text per slide, which forces clarity.

## 3. Agency / Service Pitch Structure

For agencies, consultants, and freelancers pitching to a potential client.

### Slide Order

| Slide # | Title | Purpose | Content |
|---------|-------|---------|---------|
| 1 | **Title** | Set the tone | Your name/agency, the client's name, the project title |
| 2 | **Their Challenge** | Show you listened | Restate their problem in their language. Not your interpretation — their words. |
| 3 | **Context & Insight** | Show you thought about it | What you noticed about their market, competitors, or situation that they might not have articulated |
| 4 | **Your Approach** | How you'll solve it | Methodology overview. 3-4 phases. Clear and visual. |
| 5-6 | **Case Studies** | Prove you've done this | 2-3 relevant examples. Before/after. Outcomes with numbers. |
| 7 | **The Team** | Reduce risk | Named people who will do the work. Photos. Relevant experience. |
| 8 | **Timeline & Process** | Set expectations | Week-by-week or phase-by-phase plan |
| 9 | **Investment** | Be transparent | Price breakdown by phase. Payment schedule. What's included and what's not. |
| 10 | **Why Us** | Not a brag — a fit argument | Why you're the right fit for THIS project (not in general) |
| 11 | **Next Steps** | Drive action | What happens if they say yes. First step. Timeline. |

### Agency Pitch Rules

1. **Start with their problem, not your credentials.** "Who we are" is the wrong opening slide. "What we understand about your challenge" is the right one.
2. **Case studies must be relevant.** A hospitality brand doesn't care about your tech startup work. Show work in their industry or adjacent.
3. **Name the team.** "Our experienced designers will..." is weak. "Sarah Chen, who led the Acme rebrand, will lead your project" is strong.
4. **Never hide the price.** If the investment slide isn't in the deck, the prospect spends the entire presentation wondering about cost instead of listening.
5. **The "Why Us" slide should be about fit, not superiority.** Not "we're the best agency" but "here's why we're the right agency for this specific project."

### Common Agency Pitch Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| 5+ slides about your agency before discussing the client | Cut to 1 slide max. Lead with their problem. |
| Generic case studies | Use only cases relevant to their industry, challenge, or scale |
| "Our process" as a flow chart with 12 steps | Simplify to 3-4 phases. Each with one sentence and one deliverable. |
| No price in the deck | Include investment. The price conversation happens whether you control it or not. |
| Ending with "Questions?" | End with "Next Steps" — a specific CTA, not an open floor. |

## 4. Conference Talk Structure

For presentations at conferences, meetups, workshops, and webinars.

### Slide Types and Cadence

A 20-minute talk should have 15-25 slides. The cadence matters:

| Section | Slides | Time | Purpose |
|---------|--------|------|---------|
| Hook | 1-2 | 1-2 min | Grab attention. Surprising stat, provocative question, or personal story. |
| Context | 2-3 | 2-3 min | Why this topic matters. Set the stage. |
| Framework | 1-2 | 1-2 min | Your main idea, structured. "There are 3 things..." |
| Deep dive | 8-12 | 10-12 min | Supporting points, evidence, examples. 3-4 sub-topics. |
| Synthesis | 1-2 | 2-3 min | Bring it together. What does this mean? |
| Takeaway | 1 | 1 min | One thing the audience should do after this talk. |
| Close | 1 | 30 sec | Thank you, contact info, resources. |

### Conference Talk Rules

1. **The slide supports the speaker, not the other way around.** The audience should be listening to you, not reading slides.
2. **One idea per slide. Maximum.** No bullet point lists with 6 items. If you have 6 points, you need 6 slides.
3. **Images > Text.** A full-screen image with a single headline is more powerful than a slide with 5 bullets.
4. **Data needs a story.** Don't show a chart and let the audience interpret it. Show the chart and tell them what it means.
5. **The hook must work in the first 30 seconds.** If the opening slide is "About Me" or "Agenda," you've lost them.

### Conference Talk Diagnostic

| Question | If No... |
|----------|----------|
| Could someone understand the talk from the slide headlines alone? | Headlines need to be claims, not topics |
| Is there a clear "before and after" in the audience's understanding? | The talk isn't teaching anything |
| Does the talk have one central idea? | It's trying to cover too much — narrow down |
| Are there more images than text slides? | Too text-heavy — redesign for visual impact |
| Is there a specific, actionable takeaway? | The audience will forget everything within an hour |
| Could you present this in half the time if needed? | There's filler — cut it |

## Structure Diagnostic Tool

When reviewing any deck, check these structural qualities:

### The Headline Test
Read only the slide headlines in order. Do they tell a coherent story? If not, the headlines are wrong (they're topic labels instead of claims) or the slide order is wrong.

**Bad headline sequence:** "Problem" → "Solution" → "Market" → "Team" → "Ask"
**Good headline sequence:** "Small businesses lose 12 hours/week on invoicing" → "AutoInvoice generates compliant invoices in seconds" → "The invoicing automation market is $4.2B and growing" → "Built by the team that scaled FinanceApp to $10M ARR" → "$2M to reach 1,000 paying customers"

### The "So What" Test
For every slide, ask: "So what? Why should the audience care?" If you can't answer, the slide is either unnecessary or missing its key message.

### The Redundancy Test
Are any two slides saying the same thing in different words? If so, merge them. Every slide must earn its place.

### The "10-Second" Test
Can someone understand each slide's message in 10 seconds? If a slide requires 30+ seconds to parse, it has too much content.
