# Investor Objection Catalogue

20+ objections investors raise during and after pitch presentations. For each objection: what triggers it, how to preemptively address it in the deck, and how to respond if it comes up in Q&A.

---

## Category 1: Market Objections

### Objection 1: "The market is too small."

**What triggers it:** TAM under $1B, niche market, or the investor doesn't see a path to a venture-scale outcome.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Show the market you're entering (SAM) AND the adjacent market you'll expand into
- Include growth rate — a $500M market growing at 40% YoY is more interesting than a $5B market growing at 3%
- Show market catalysts: regulatory changes, technology shifts, behavioral trends that are expanding the market

**Q&A response:** "Today's SAM is [$X]. But our product is positioned at the intersection of [Market A] and [Market B]. As we expand from [current niche] into [adjacent segment], our addressable market grows to [$Y]. [Comparable company] started in a similarly sized niche and expanded to capture [$Z]."

### Objection 2: "Your market sizing methodology is unclear."

**What triggers it:** Top-down TAM without bottom-up validation. "The global CRM market is $60B" without explaining your slice.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Always show bottom-up math: [Number of potential customers] x [Average contract value] = SAM
- Source your numbers (industry reports, government data, your own customer research)
- If your bottom-up number is much smaller than the top-down number, explain the gap

**Q&A response:** "Our bottom-up calculation: there are approximately [X] companies in our ICP — [specific criteria]. Our average contract value is [$Y]. That gives us a directly addressable market of [$Z]. The top-down TAM of [$large number] represents the broader category, but we focus on the bottom-up number because it reflects customers we can actually reach."

### Objection 3: "Why now? This problem has existed for years."

**What triggers it:** No clear timing catalyst. If the problem has been around for a decade, why hasn't someone solved it?

**Preemptive slide content (the "Why Now" slide):**
Good "Why Now" answers include:
- New technology enables a solution that wasn't possible before (AI, APIs, mobile, cloud)
- Regulatory change creates new demand (GDPR, Open Banking, e-invoicing mandates)
- Behavioral shift makes customers ready (remote work, creator economy, generational change)
- Infrastructure maturity (Stripe made payments easy, AWS made hosting cheap, etc.)

**Q&A response:** "Three things converged in the last 18 months: [technology shift] made it technically feasible, [regulatory change] made it necessary for our customers, and [behavioral shift] made them receptive to a new approach. Two years ago, this product would have been possible but not timely. Two years from now, someone else will have built it. The window is now."

## Category 2: Product & Traction Objections

### Objection 4: "Where's the traction?"

**What triggers it:** No metrics, vague traction claims ("growing fast"), or metrics that don't demonstrate product-market fit.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Stage-appropriate traction. Pre-launch: waitlist size, LOIs, pilot commitments. Post-launch: MRR, growth rate, retention, engagement.
- If traction is early, show velocity: "0 to 100 customers in 3 months without paid marketing" is compelling even if 100 customers is small
- Non-revenue traction counts: user engagement, retention, NPS, referral rate

**Q&A response (pre-revenue):** "We launched [X weeks/months] ago. We have [Y] customers/users, [Z] on the waitlist, and [metric] engagement rate. Our growth has been entirely organic — [channel]. The signal we're tracking is [retention/engagement], which is at [X%], above the industry benchmark of [Y%]."

### Objection 5: "What's your moat? What stops someone from copying this?"

**What triggers it:** The product looks easy to replicate, or a bigger company could add this feature.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Don't claim a moat if you don't have one. "Our moat is our technology" is weak unless it's genuinely novel.
- Network effects, data advantages, switching costs, and speed of execution are real moats for startups
- "We will have a moat because [X]" (future moat) is fine if the strategy is clear

**Q&A response:** "At this stage, our primary advantages are [speed of execution / deep domain expertise / early customer relationships]. As we scale, our moat deepens through [network effects: each customer makes the product better for all customers / data: our training data improves with every user / switching costs: the more data customers store with us, the harder it is to leave]. We don't pretend to have an unassailable moat today — but we have a clear path to building one."

### Objection 6: "What if Google/Amazon/Salesforce builds this?"

**What triggers it:** The solution is in a space where big tech could expand.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Acknowledge it directly. Pretending big tech won't enter your space is worse than addressing it.
- Show why they haven't (different priorities, different business model, organizational inertia)
- Show why they wouldn't serve your customer well even if they did (too generic, too expensive, wrong distribution)

**Q&A response:** "[Big company] could build this, but they probably won't, and here's why: [1] it's not strategic for them — their core business is [X], [2] their product serves [enterprise/consumer] and ours serves [different segment], and [3] historically, they've acquired in this space rather than built — which is actually a positive signal for us."

### Objection 7: "Your churn is too high."

**What triggers it:** Monthly churn above 3-5% for SMB, above 1-2% for mid-market, or not discussing churn at all.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Show churn AND retention. Net revenue retention (NRR) is more important than logo churn for SaaS.
- If churn is high, explain why and what you're doing: "Early churn was 8% due to [reason]. After [change], churn dropped to 4% and is trending toward 3%."
- Cohort analysis showing improvement over time is very compelling

**Q&A response:** "Our current monthly churn is [X%]. We've identified the primary driver: [specific cause]. We've implemented [specific change] and are tracking the impact — in the most recent cohort, churn is [Y%, lower]. Our net revenue retention is [Z%] because customers who stay expand their usage over time."

## Category 3: Business Model Objections

### Objection 8: "How do you make money?"

**What triggers it:** Business model is unclear, or the pricing seems too low to build a large business.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- State the model simply: "SaaS subscription, $X/month per [unit]" or "Transaction fee of X%" or "Project-based, average engagement $X"
- Show unit economics if available: LTV, CAC, LTV/CAC ratio, payback period
- If the model is evolving, explain the current model and the target model

### Objection 9: "Your CAC seems high relative to LTV."

**What triggers it:** LTV/CAC ratio below 3:1, or long payback period (>18 months for SaaS).

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Show CAC by channel — blended CAC hides the story. One channel might be efficient while another drags the average.
- Show CAC trend: "CAC was $X in Q1, now $Y due to [improvement]"
- If CAC is high because you're early, say so: "Our current CAC reflects a pre-optimization acquisition strategy. We expect to reduce CAC by [X%] through [specific channel or tactic]."

### Objection 10: "The pricing seems low. Can you build a big business at this price point?"

**What triggers it:** Low ARPU (average revenue per user) combined with an SMB-focused customer base.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Show expansion revenue: customers who start at $X grow to $Y over 12 months
- Show volume: at [target number] customers x $[ARPU], annual revenue is $[X]
- Reference comparable companies: "[Company] built a $[X]M business at similar ARPU by achieving [Y] customers"

## Category 4: Competition Objections

### Objection 11: "How is this different from [competitor]?"

**What triggers it:** The investor knows a competitor you didn't mention, or your differentiation isn't clear.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Never claim "no competitors." The status quo (doing nothing, spreadsheets) is always a competitor.
- Position honestly: acknowledge where competitors are strong, then show your specific advantage
- Use specific differentiators, not vague ones: "We're faster" is weak. "We reduce onboarding from 6 weeks to 2 days because of our pre-built integration library" is strong.

**Q&A response:** "Good question. [Competitor] does [X] well. Where we differ is [specific capability]. Our customers choose us because [specific reason] — in fact, [X%] of our customers switched from [competitor] specifically because of [this gap]."

### Objection 12: "Your competition slide looks too favorable."

**What triggers it:** A 2x2 positioning map where the user's product is alone in the "best" quadrant.

**Q&A response:** Reframe: "Fair point. Let me be more specific about where they're strong and where we're different." Then give an honest assessment. Investors trust founders who can objectively assess competition.

## Category 5: Team Objections

### Objection 13: "The team doesn't have relevant experience."

**What triggers it:** No one on the team has domain expertise in the target market, or it's a first-time founder with no technical co-founder.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Highlight non-obvious relevant experience. "5 years at McKinsey" might not seem relevant to a developer tool, but "5 years advising enterprises on technology adoption" is.
- If you're a first-time founder, show compensating factors: advisors, speed of execution, domain obsession
- Show what you've already built and shipped — execution speed speaks louder than resume

### Objection 14: "You need a CTO / a co-founder / a head of sales."

**What triggers it:** Obvious gap in the team that the investor believes is critical for the next phase.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Address the gap directly: "Our first hire with this funding will be a VP of Sales"
- Show that you know the gap exists — this is better than pretending
- If you have candidates or advisors filling the gap temporarily, mention them

### Objection 15: "What happens if a co-founder leaves?"

**What triggers it:** Two-person team with no vesting, or visible co-founder tension.

**Q&A response:** "We have standard 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff. If either of us leaves, the company retains the unvested equity. We also have a documented operating agreement covering decision-making and dispute resolution."

## Category 6: Financial Objections

### Objection 16: "Your projections are too aggressive."

**What triggers it:** Hockey stick growth with no clear driver, or assumptions that don't hold up to scrutiny.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Label projections as "model" not "forecast"
- Show the assumptions: growth rate, conversion rate, churn, expansion revenue
- Include a sensitivity analysis or show bear/base/bull scenarios

### Objection 17: "How long does this money last?"

**What triggers it:** Unclear runway calculation, or a burn rate that feels too high for the stage.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- State runway explicitly: "This round gives us 18-24 months of runway"
- Show monthly burn rate (current and projected with new hires)
- Show the milestones you'll hit before the money runs out

### Objection 18: "You're raising too much / too little."

**What triggers it:** The raise amount doesn't match the milestones or the burn rate.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Tie the raise to specific milestones: "$2M gets us to $500K MRR and profitability, or puts us in position to raise a Series A at a $[X]M valuation"
- Show the use of funds breakdown: Engineering (40%), Sales (30%), Operations (20%), Buffer (10%)

## Category 7: Strategic Objections

### Objection 19: "This is a feature, not a company."

**What triggers it:** The product solves a narrow problem that a larger platform could absorb.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Show the expansion roadmap: this feature is the wedge, but the platform opportunity is [X]
- Reference comparables: "[Company] started as a single feature (Slack started as a game company's internal chat) and became a platform"
- Show why the feature is better as a standalone product: different buyer, different use case, different pricing model

### Objection 20: "What's your exit strategy?"

**What triggers it:** Standard investor question, especially for seed/Series A.

**Preemptive slide content:**
- Don't include an exit strategy slide (it signals you're building to sell, not to grow)
- In Q&A, acknowledge the question naturally: "Several larger players in this space have made acquisitions recently — [examples]. We're building a standalone business, but the strategic interest is clear."

## Using This Catalogue in Deck Reviews

When reviewing a deck, check each slide against this list:

1. Does the deck preemptively address the top 5 objections for this company's stage?
2. Are there objections the deck actively triggers? (e.g., a vague market sizing slide triggers Objection 2)
3. Are there objections the deck ignores that the audience will certainly raise?

For each gap, recommend either:
- A slide addition or modification that preemptively addresses the objection
- A Q&A prep note for the presenter
- Or both
