# Example: Freelancer Rate Analysis

## User Input

> I'm a freelance UX designer based in Düsseldorf. I've been freelancing for 4 years. I currently charge €85/hr and work about 30 hours a week, but I feel like I'm always busy and never ahead financially. My monthly expenses are about €2,800 (home office, software, insurance, accounting, health insurance). I want to take home about €65,000 net after taxes. I mostly work with mid-size companies and the occasional startup. Should I raise my rates? Should I switch to project pricing?

## 3. Recommendation

**Go with the Hybrid Model (Scenario C), implemented in phases.**

Your combination of 4 years of experience, mid-size company clients, and UX specialization positions you well for this transition. Here's why:

1. **Your experience supports project pricing.** After 4 years, you can estimate UX projects reliably. You know what a full UX design takes — price the outcome, not the hours.
2. **A retainer provides stability.** One client paying €3,000-4,000/month for ongoing UX work gives you a revenue floor while you build the project pipeline.
3. **Hourly at €125 for small work.** Small tasks (reviews, audits, one-off screens) don't justify project scoping. Bill hourly at the new rate.

## 4. Transition Plan

### Month 1-2: Foundation
- [ ] Calculate your project pricing for 3-4 standard UX service packages (audit, full design, design system, research)
- [ ] Update your portfolio and website with project-based pricing signals (show project outcomes, not hourly work)
- [ ] Inform existing clients: "Starting [date], my rate will be €125/hr for ongoing work, and I now offer project-based pricing for defined scopes"
- [ ] Give existing clients 30-60 days notice on the rate change

### Month 3-4: Implementation
- [ ] Quote all new projects at project-based rates (use your packages as starting points, customize per client)
- [ ] Approach your most consistent client about a retainer arrangement: "Instead of ad-hoc requests at €125/hr, I can offer a monthly retainer of €3,500 for up to 30 hours of UX work — that's €117/hr, a better rate for you, and predictable availability for both of us"
- [ ] Track your hours per project internally (even though the client sees a fixed price) — this helps you improve estimates

### Month 5-6: Optimization
- [ ] Review your first 2-3 projects: were you profitable? Did you estimate correctly?
- [ ] Adjust project pricing based on real data
- [ ] If a client pushes back on the new rate, do NOT discount. Offer a smaller scope instead: "I understand the budget is tight. Let me propose a focused UX audit instead of a full redesign — that's €4,500 and addresses your most critical usability issues."

## 6. Risk Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| Client leaves over rate increase | Expected — you may lose 1-2 price-sensitive clients. Replace them with clients who pay your new rate. Losing a €85/hr client frees time for a €125/hr client or a €10,000 project. |
| Project takes longer than estimated | Add 25% buffer to all estimates. For the first 3-4 projects, track actuals rigorously and adjust. |
| Retainer client underutilizes | Define minimum commitment (3-6 months). If they consistently use <50% of hours, proactively suggest additional work or adjust the retainer size. |
| Clients ask for hourly when you quote project | Explain: "I've moved to project pricing because it gives you cost certainty and me the flexibility to deliver the best result without watching the clock. If hourly is important to you, my rate is €125/hr." |
| Imposter syndrome about charging more | Your break-even analysis proves €85/hr is unsustainable. This isn't about confidence — it's about math. Market rate for a mid-level UX designer in Germany is €85-120/hr. You're not overcharging at €125; you're catching up. |
