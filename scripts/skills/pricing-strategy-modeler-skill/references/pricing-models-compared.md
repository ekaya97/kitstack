# Pricing Models Compared — Deep Reference

This document provides a detailed comparison of the four primary pricing models for freelancers and agencies. Use it when building model comparisons and when the user needs to understand the trade-offs.

---

## Model 1: Hourly Pricing

### How It Works
You charge a fixed rate per hour of work. The client pays for time, not outcomes. You track hours, submit timesheets or invoices, and bill accordingly.

### When to Use
- The scope is genuinely undefined or highly variable
- You're doing advisory, consulting, or support work
- The client prefers time-and-materials for budgeting flexibility
- You're early in your career and still learning to estimate project scope
- Short engagements (under 20 hours) where project pricing overhead isn't worth it
- The client has a strong internal PM who manages scope tightly

### When to Avoid
- You're fast and efficient — hourly penalizes speed
- The scope is well-defined and repeatable
- The client is cost-conscious and will micromanage hours
- You want predictable monthly revenue

### Rate Setting Formula
```
Target Annual Revenue ÷ (Billable Hours per Year) = Minimum Hourly Rate

Example:
€120,000 ÷ 1,380 billable hours = €87/hr → round to €90/hr
```

**Billable hours calculation:**
- 52 weeks - 4 weeks vacation - 2 weeks sick/admin = 46 working weeks
- 40 hour week × 75% utilization = 30 billable hours/week
- 46 × 30 = 1,380 billable hours/year

### Profit Margin Adjustment
Add 20-30% margin above your break-even rate:
```
Break-even rate: €65/hr (covers all costs + desired salary)
With 25% margin: €65 × 1.25 = €81/hr → price at €85/hr
```

### Common Mistakes
- Billing only "keyboard time" and not counting meetings, research, communication
- Not tracking non-billable hours, leading to an inflated sense of hourly productivity
- Setting rates based on what feels comfortable rather than what the math requires
- Discounting rates for "easy" work — your expertise makes it look easy
- Not including an estimate range — "approximately 30-40 hours" protects both sides

### Variations
- **Hourly with a cap:** "€90/hr, not to exceed €5,400" — gives the client a ceiling
- **Hourly with a minimum:** "€90/hr, minimum 10 hours/month" — gives you a floor
- **Day rate:** €650/day — simpler than hourly, feels more premium, reduces time tracking

## Model 2: Project-Based Pricing

### How It Works
You quote a fixed price for a defined scope of work. The client pays for the outcome, not the time. You bear the estimation risk; the client gets cost certainty.

### When to Use
- The scope is clearly defined and you can estimate confidently
- You have experience with similar projects (you know the real effort)
- Your efficiency advantage should benefit you, not the client
- The client needs budget certainty for internal approvals
- The project has clear start and end points with defined deliverables

### When to Avoid
- The scope is genuinely ambiguous (discovery projects, R&D, experimental work)
- You haven't done this type of project before and can't estimate reliably
- The client is notorious for scope changes
- The project depends heavily on third-party inputs you can't control

### Price Setting Framework
```
Step 1: Estimate hours honestly (add 20% buffer)
Step 2: Multiply by your target hourly rate
Step 3: Adjust for value and complexity
Step 4: Structure into phases

Example:
Estimated effort: 120 hours (96 actual + 24 buffer)
Target rate: €90/hr
Base price: €10,800
Complexity adjustment: +15% (new technology, integration work)
Project price: €12,420 → present as €12,500
```

### Phase-Based Structure
Break every project into phases with individual prices:

| Phase | Description | Price | % of Total |
|-------|-------------|-------|------------|
| Discovery | Research, strategy, planning | €2,500 | 20% |
| Design | Mockups, prototypes, review | €3,750 | 30% |
| Development | Build, integrate, test | €5,000 | 40% |
| Launch | Deploy, QA, handoff | €1,250 | 10% |
| **Total** | | **€12,500** | **100%** |

### Scope Protection Mechanisms
1. **Explicit scope document** — list what IS and IS NOT included
2. **Revision limits** — "Includes 2 rounds of revisions per phase"
3. **Change order process** — "Changes outside scope are billed at €95/hr with written approval"
4. **Kill clauses** — "Either party may terminate with 14 days notice. Work completed to date is billed proportionally."

### Common Mistakes
- Underestimating by 30-50% because of optimism bias
- Not defining "done" — when is a phase complete? When the client approves.
- Absorbing scope creep silently, then resenting the client
- Pricing on gut feeling instead of hours × rate × complexity
- Not front-loading payments — if the client pays 50% at the end, you're financing their project

## Model 3: Retainer Pricing

### How It Works
The client pays a recurring monthly fee for ongoing access to your services. The retainer can be hours-based ("40 hours/month") or deliverables-based ("4 blog posts + 20 social graphics/month") or access-based ("unlimited Slack access + 2 strategy calls/month").

### When to Use
- You have clients who need ongoing, recurring work
- You want predictable monthly revenue
- The work is continuous (marketing, design, development maintenance, advisory)
- The client values having a dedicated resource without hiring

### When to Avoid
- The client only needs you occasionally — retainers with low utilization breed resentment
- You can't commit to consistent monthly availability
- The scope is genuinely project-based with a clear end date

### Retainer Types

**Hours-based retainer:**
- "40 hours/month at €85/hr = €3,400/month"
- Client buys a block of hours, used on various tasks
- Hours may or may not roll over (specify in contract)
- Discount of 5-15% off your standard hourly rate is common for the commitment

**Deliverables-based retainer:**
- "€4,000/month for: 4 blog posts, 20 social graphics, 2 email templates"
- Client knows exactly what they get
- You control the hours — if you're efficient, you profit more
- Risk: client expectations grow beyond the defined deliverables

**Access-based retainer:**
- "€2,000/month for strategic advisory: unlimited Slack, 2 calls/month, quarterly strategy review"
- Highest margin, lowest hours commitment
- Best for senior consultants and strategists
- Risk: client may feel they're not getting enough for the money

### Pricing Formula
```
Hours-based: (Monthly hours) × (Hourly rate) × (Discount factor)
Example: 40 × €90 × 0.9 (10% discount) = €3,240/month

Deliverables-based: Sum of (deliverable value × quantity)
Example: (€400/blog × 4) + (€50/graphic × 20) + (€300/template × 2) = €3,200/month

Access-based: Value of availability + average hours × premium rate
Example: Availability fee €1,000 + (8 hours × €125) = €2,000/month
```

### Retainer Management Rules
1. Track hours or deliverables transparently — send monthly reports
2. Notify the client at 75% utilization mid-month
3. Define the rollover policy explicitly (recommended: no rollover, or 25% max)
4. Include a "pause" clause for months when the client doesn't need you
5. Require 30-day notice for cancellation (60-day for larger retainers)
6. Define rush work policy (work needed in <48hrs at 1.5x rate deducted from hours)

### Common Mistakes
- Not tracking hours, then discovering you worked 60 hours on a 40-hour retainer
- Allowing scope drift — the retainer was for design, now you're doing copywriting
- Not having a minimum commitment (3-6 months) — monthly cancellations destroy planning
- Underpricing to win the retainer, then regretting it for 6 months
- Not having a clear request process — Slack messages become an endless to-do list

## Model 4: Value-Based Pricing

### How It Works
You price based on the business outcome your work creates, not the time it takes. If your marketing strategy will generate €500,000 in revenue, you might charge €50,000 (10% of value) — regardless of whether it takes you 50 hours or 200.

### When to Use
- Your work directly impacts revenue, cost savings, or a measurable business outcome
- You have a track record of delivering measurable results
- The client can articulate the business value of the project
- You have a sophisticated sales process that can surface value in the discovery phase
- The project is strategic, not executional

### When to Avoid
- You can't quantify the value of your work
- The client can't or won't share business metrics
- You're early in your career without case studies to back up value claims
- The work is commodity/execution (a logo, a landing page, data entry)
- The client's budget is set and non-negotiable

### Value Calculation Framework
```
Step 1: Identify the business outcome (revenue increase, cost reduction, risk mitigation)
Step 2: Quantify the outcome in currency
Step 3: Apply a capture percentage (typically 10-20% of value created)
Step 4: Validate against project effort (ensure your effective rate is attractive)

Example — E-commerce conversion optimization:
Current revenue: €2,000,000/year
Expected improvement: 15% increase = €300,000 additional revenue
Capture rate: 15%
Value-based price: €45,000
Your estimated effort: 100 hours
Effective hourly rate: €450/hr
```

### The Value Discovery Conversation
You cannot do value-based pricing without a structured discovery:
1. "What business problem are we solving?"
2. "What is this problem costing you today?" (in revenue, time, or risk)
3. "What would success look like in 12 months?"
4. "Can you put a number on that success?"
5. "What have you already tried, and what did you spend?"

If the client can't answer questions 2-4, value-based pricing may not be appropriate.

### Value-Based Pricing Structures
- **Fixed value price:** €45,000 for the project (most common)
- **Performance fee:** €20,000 base + 5% of revenue increase above baseline
- **Gain share:** 10% of cost savings achieved in Year 1
- **Equity/revenue share:** Rare, but used for startup advisory (accept equity or revenue share instead of fees)

### Common Mistakes
- Pulling a percentage out of thin air without a discovery conversation
- Not having proof of past results — value pricing requires case studies
- Confusing "expensive" with "value-based" — value pricing is justified by outcomes, not ego
- Using value-based pricing for low-value commodity work
- Forgetting to define what happens if the outcome isn't achieved

## Model Comparison Summary

| Factor | Hourly | Project | Retainer | Value-Based |
|--------|--------|---------|----------|-------------|
| Revenue predictability | Low | Medium | High | Variable |
| Scope risk (yours) | Low | High | Medium | Low |
| Income ceiling | Hours × Rate | Per project | Monthly cap | Unlimited |
| Client trust required | Low | Medium | Medium | High |
| Sales complexity | Low | Medium | Low | High |
| Best for early career | Yes | After experience | After relationships | After track record |
| Best for scaling | No | Somewhat | Yes | Yes |
| Admin overhead | High (tracking) | Medium | Medium | Low |
