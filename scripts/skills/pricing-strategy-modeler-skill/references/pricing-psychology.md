# Pricing Psychology — Deep Reference

This document covers the psychological principles behind effective pricing. Use these techniques when helping users present their prices to clients, structure pricing tiers, and overcome pricing-related objections.

---

## Principle 1: Anchoring

### What It Is
The first number a person sees becomes the reference point for evaluating all subsequent numbers. If the first price they see is €20,000, then €12,000 feels reasonable. If the first price is €5,000, then €12,000 feels outrageous — even though the service is identical.

### How to Apply
- **Present the premium option first.** In proposals, show the high-end package before the standard one. The standard package feels like a deal by comparison.
- **Reference industry benchmarks.** "Agencies typically charge €40,000-60,000 for this scope. Our rate for this project is €28,000." The client now evaluates €28,000 against €40,000, not against €0.
- **Name the full value before the discounted price.** "This package includes €15,000 in strategic consulting and €10,000 in implementation. For this engagement, the total is €22,000." The client sees €25,000 of value for €22,000.
- **Use ranges that start high.** When asked "How much does this cost?", say "Projects like this typically range from €15,000 to €25,000 depending on scope" — not "It could be €8,000 to €25,000."

### When NOT to Use
- Don't anchor so high that you lose credibility. If the client's budget is clearly €10,000, anchoring at €50,000 just makes you seem disconnected.
- Don't use anchoring to be deceptive. The premium option must be real and valuable.

## Principle 2: Framing (Investment vs. Cost)

### What It Is
The same price feels different depending on how it's framed. "The cost is €15,000" triggers loss aversion. "Your investment is €15,000" triggers expectation of return.

### Framing Techniques

**Investment framing:**
- "Your investment" not "The cost" or "The price"
- "This investment generates..." not "This costs..."
- Tie every price to an outcome: "€15,000 for a website that converts 3x more visitors into leads"

**Time framing:**
- Break monthly costs into daily: "€3,000/month works out to about €100/day — less than the cost of a junior employee"
- Break project costs into outcomes: "€12,000 spread across 12 months of use is €1,000/month — or about €33/day"

**Comparison framing:**
- Compare to the cost of NOT doing it: "You're currently losing an estimated €5,000/month in missed leads. This project pays for itself in 3 months."
- Compare to inferior alternatives: "You could hire an in-house designer at €55,000/year plus benefits and equipment, or retain our team for €4,000/month with zero overhead."

**Risk framing:**
- Milestone payments reduce perceived risk: "You only pay for the next phase after approving the current one"
- Guarantees reduce fear: "If the homepage design doesn't meet your expectations after 2 rounds of revision, we'll refund the design phase"

## Principle 3: The Decoy Effect (Asymmetric Dominance)

### What It Is
When given three options, people tend to choose the middle one — but only when the options are structured so that the middle is clearly the best value relative to the extremes.

### The Classic Three-Tier Structure

| Tier | What's Included | Price | Purpose |
|------|----------------|-------|---------|
| Basic | Core deliverable only | €5,000 | Makes Standard look complete |
| **Standard** | Core + extras (most popular) | €8,500 | **This is the one you want them to buy** |
| Premium | Everything + extras + priority | €14,000 | Makes Standard look affordable |

### Design Rules
1. **The Basic tier should feel incomplete.** Missing something obvious that the client needs. Not useless — but clearly not enough.
2. **The Standard tier should include everything the client actually needs.** It's the "just right" option.
3. **The Premium tier should add genuine value** (not filler) but at a price that makes most clients pause. It exists to make Standard look reasonable AND to capture the occasional client who wants the best.
4. **Price ratios matter.** Standard should be 40-70% more than Basic. Premium should be 50-80% more than Standard. If the gaps are too even, the decoy effect weakens.

### Example: Web Design Three-Tier Pricing

| | Basic | Standard | Premium |
|--|-------|----------|---------|
| Homepage design | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Inner pages | 3 pages | 8 pages | Unlimited |
| Responsive design | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| CMS integration | No | Yes | Yes |
| SEO setup | No | Basic | Advanced |
| Content writing | No | No | 5 pages included |
| Training session | No | 30 min | 2 hours |
| Post-launch support | 2 weeks | 4 weeks | 12 weeks |
| Revisions | 1 round | 2 rounds | 3 rounds |
| **Price** | **€4,800** | **€8,200** | **€13,500** |

Most clients choose Standard because Basic doesn't include CMS (which they need) and Premium is €5,300 more for extras they're unsure about.

## Principle 4: Price Partitioning

### What It Is
Breaking a total into smaller components makes it feel less expensive. €12,000 feels like a lot. "€3,000 for strategy + €5,000 for design + €4,000 for development" feels like three manageable investments.

### How to Apply
- **Phase-based pricing:** Break projects into phases with individual price tags
- **Line-item breakdown:** Show what each component costs
- **Monthly equivalents:** "€12,000 over 4 months is €3,000/month"
- **Per-unit pricing:** "20 social graphics at €150 each" feels different from "€3,000 for social graphics"

### When to Use Partitioning vs. Bundling
- **Partition** when the total feels high and you need to justify each component
- **Bundle** when individual components feel expensive but the total with a discount feels attractive
- **Partition** for analytical clients who want to understand what they're paying for
- **Bundle** for clients who want simplicity and don't want to evaluate each line item

## Principle 5: Charm Pricing vs. Prestige Pricing

### Charm Pricing (€97, €4,997)
- Prices ending in 7 or 9 feel like a deal
- Signals: "We've calculated this precisely" and "you're getting a bargain"
- Best for: commodity services, competitive markets, price-sensitive clients
- Example: €4,997 for a website package

### Prestige Pricing (€5,000, €10,000)
- Round numbers signal quality and confidence
- Signals: "This is premium" and "we don't discount"
- Best for: luxury, premium services, high-end clients
- Example: €10,000 for a brand identity

### Calculated Pricing (€4,750, €11,200)
- Non-round numbers that aren't charm-priced feel calculated and specific
- Signals: "This price is based on the actual scope, not a round number"
- Best for: project-based work, technical services, detail-oriented clients
- Example: €11,200 for a web application (suggests specific hour estimates)

**Recommendation:** For freelancers and agencies, calculated pricing usually works best. It communicates that the price reflects the actual work, not an arbitrary number.

## Principle 6: Loss Aversion

### What It Is
People feel the pain of losing something roughly 2x more than the pleasure of gaining something equivalent. Losing €100 feels worse than gaining €100 feels good.

### Application in Pricing
- **Show what they're losing by NOT acting:** "Every month without this website optimization, you're losing approximately €4,000 in potential revenue"
- **Create urgency through scarcity:** "This rate is available for projects starting this quarter" (only use if true)
- **Frame payment terms as protection:** "The 50% upfront secures your spot in our production schedule for [month]"
- **Show depreciation of inaction:** "Your current site is losing 2% of traffic per month due to performance issues. In 6 months, that's a 12% cumulative decline."

## Principle 7: The "Too Cheap" Problem

### What It Is
Counterintuitively, pricing too low can lose you clients. Low prices signal:
- Inexperience or desperation
- Low quality or cut corners
- "If they're this cheap, what am I not getting?"
- The freelancer doesn't understand the complexity of the work

### Signs You're Priced Too Low
- Clients accept your quotes instantly without negotiation
- You attract clients who are demanding, unpleasant, and scope-creep-prone
- You're always busy but never profitable
- Clients ask "Are you sure that's enough?" (this actually happens)
- Other professionals in your market charge 50-100% more for similar work

### How to Diagnose
Ask the user: "When you send a proposal, how often does the client accept without negotiating?" If the answer is "almost always," they're underpriced. A healthy close rate with some negotiation is 40-60%.

## Principle 8: The Contrast Principle

### What It Is
People evaluate options relative to what's next to them, not in absolute terms.

### Application
- **Show your price next to the cost of an employee.** A €5,000/month retainer looks expensive alone. Next to "€65,000/year salary + €15,000 benefits + €5,000 equipment + management overhead = €85,000+/year," it's a bargain.
- **Show your price next to the cost of failure.** "The last agency underdelivered and it cost you €30,000 and 6 months. Our project investment is €18,000 with milestone-based payments."
- **Show ROI alongside the price.** Never present a price without context. €12,000 next to "projected €60,000 in new revenue" has a completely different feel than €12,000 alone.

## Applying Psychology Ethically

These techniques are tools, not tricks. The ethical use of pricing psychology:

1. **The underlying value must be real.** Psychology helps communicate value that exists. It does not create value that doesn't.
2. **Never use artificial scarcity.** "Only 2 spots left this month" when you have 10 is dishonest and eventually catches up with you.
3. **Anchoring must reference real comparisons.** "Agencies charge €40,000" is valid if true. "The value is €200,000" is only valid if you can demonstrate it.
4. **The client should feel good about the price after delivery, not just before.** If they feel tricked later, you've lost a relationship for a single sale.
5. **Price with confidence, not with manipulation.** The goal is to present your genuine value clearly, not to extract maximum money from confused buyers.
