# Milestone Frameworks

Phase structures and milestone patterns for common project types. Use these as starting frameworks and customize based on the specific engagement. Every milestone should have: a name, a duration, key deliverables, a completion condition, and a payment trigger.

---

## 1. Web Development (8-16 weeks)

| Phase | Duration | Key Deliverables | Completion Condition | Payment |
|-------|----------|-----------------|---------------------|---------|
| Discovery & Planning | 1-2 weeks | Requirements doc, sitemap, tech spec, project plan | Client signs off on requirements and sitemap | 20% |
| UX & Wireframes | 2-3 weeks | User flows, wireframes (all templates), interactive prototype | Client approves wireframes after 2 revision rounds | 15% |
| Visual Design | 2-3 weeks | High-fidelity mockups, design system, responsive specs | Client approves designs after 2 revision rounds | 15% |
| Development | 3-5 weeks | Functional website on staging, CMS integration, forms | All pages functional on staging, content entry complete | 25% |
| QA & Launch | 1-2 weeks | Cross-browser testing, performance optimization, go-live | Site live on production, passing acceptance criteria | 20% |
| Warranty & Handoff | 2-4 weeks (post-launch) | Bug fixes, documentation, CMS training | Warranty period expires with no outstanding defects | 5% |

**Decision gates:**
- Gate 1 (end of Discovery): Proceed to design? Requirements stable?
- Gate 2 (end of Design): Approve visual direction before development begins
- Gate 3 (Staging): Client QA approval before production deployment

**Typical client dependencies:**
- Final copy and media assets by end of Design phase
- CMS content entry or content review during Development phase
- 3-5 business day QA testing period on staging
- DNS credentials and hosting access before launch

## 2. Brand Strategy & Identity (6-12 weeks)

| Phase | Duration | Key Deliverables | Completion Condition | Payment |
|-------|----------|-----------------|---------------------|---------|
| Research & Discovery | 2-3 weeks | Stakeholder interviews, competitive audit, brand perception analysis, research report | Research report delivered and presented | 25% |
| Strategy & Positioning | 2-3 weeks | Brand positioning statement, messaging framework, audience personas, verbal identity | Client approves positioning and messaging | 25% |
| Visual Identity | 2-4 weeks | Logo system, color palette, typography, visual language, mood direction | Client approves visual identity after 2 revision rounds | 30% |
| Guidelines & Rollout | 1-2 weeks | Brand guidelines document (40-60pp), core templates, asset package | Final guidelines delivered in PDF + editable format | 20% |

**Decision gates:**
- Gate 1 (end of Research): Align on strategic direction before positioning work
- Gate 2 (end of Strategy): Lock positioning and messaging before visual exploration
- Gate 3 (Visual concepts): Select concept direction before refinement (typically 2-3 directions presented, 1 selected)

**Typical client dependencies:**
- Access to 4-6 stakeholders for interviews in Week 1
- Feedback on positioning options within 5 business days
- Concept direction selection within 5 business days
- Internal alignment on brand architecture (if multi-brand)

## 3. Marketing Campaign (6-14 weeks)

| Phase | Duration | Key Deliverables | Completion Condition | Payment |
|-------|----------|-----------------|---------------------|---------|
| Strategy & Planning | 1-2 weeks | Campaign brief, audience segments, channel strategy, content calendar, KPI framework | Client approves campaign brief and budget allocation | 20% |
| Creative Development | 2-3 weeks | Key messaging, ad concepts (3 directions), landing page wireframe, email sequences | Client selects creative direction, approves messaging | 20% |
| Production & Setup | 1-2 weeks | Final ad assets (all sizes/formats), landing page, ad platform configuration, tracking | All assets produced, platforms configured, tracking verified | 20% |
| Campaign Execution | 4-8 weeks | Campaign live, weekly performance reports, bi-weekly optimization | Campaign runs for agreed duration | 30% |
| Reporting & Wrap-Up | 1 week | Final performance report, learnings document, recommendations for next campaign | Report delivered and presented | 10% |

**Decision gates:**
- Gate 1 (end of Strategy): Budget allocation and channel mix approved
- Gate 2 (end of Creative): Creative direction selected (1 of 3 concepts)
- Gate 3 (Pre-launch): All tracking verified, assets approved, landing page QA passed

**Typical client dependencies:**
- Marketing budget confirmation and ad platform access
- Creative approval within 5 business days per round
- Landing page content approval before development
- Tracking pixel installation on client's website

## 4. Management Consulting (4-12 weeks)

| Phase | Duration | Key Deliverables | Completion Condition | Payment |
|-------|----------|-----------------|---------------------|---------|
| Diagnostic | 2-3 weeks | Stakeholder interviews (8-12), data analysis, current state assessment, diagnostic findings presentation | Diagnostic presented to leadership, alignment on problem definition | 30% |
| Analysis & Options | 2-4 weeks | Strategic options analysis (3-4 options), financial modeling, scenario analysis, recommendation report | Recommendation presented, strategic direction selected | 35% |
| Implementation Planning | 2-4 weeks | Detailed implementation roadmap, resource plan, budget model, risk register, 90-day action plan, board-ready executive presentation | Final deliverables accepted | 35% |

**Decision gates:**
- Gate 1 (end of Diagnostic): Agreement on the problem definition and analysis scope
- Gate 2 (end of Analysis): Strategic direction selected from options presented
- Gate 3 (Final): Implementation plan approved by leadership/board

**Typical client dependencies:**
- Access to stakeholders for interviews (scheduled within first 5 business days)
- Financial and operational data access (under NDA)
- Leadership availability for two 90-minute working sessions
- Decision on strategic direction within 10 business days of options presentation

## 5. Technology Implementation (10-24 weeks)

| Phase | Duration | Key Deliverables | Completion Condition | Payment |
|-------|----------|-----------------|---------------------|---------|
| Requirements & Architecture | 2-3 weeks | Business requirements doc, technical spec, system architecture, integration map, sprint plan | Client signs off on requirements and architecture | 15% |
| Core Build (Sprints) | 4-8 weeks | Core functionality per sprint backlog, bi-weekly demos, automated test coverage | All sprint deliverables accepted via demo sign-off | 35% |
| Integrations & Data | 2-4 weeks | Third-party integrations, data migration, API connections, end-to-end testing | Integration tests passing, data migration verified | 20% |
| UAT & Hardening | 2-4 weeks | Security audit, performance optimization, user acceptance testing support, defect resolution | UAT sign-off from client's test team | 15% |
| Deployment & Stabilization | 1-2 weeks | Production deployment, monitoring setup, post-launch support, documentation, knowledge transfer | System live and stable for 5 business days | 10% |
| Warranty | 2-4 weeks (post-launch) | Defect correction, performance monitoring | Warranty period expires | 5% |

**Decision gates:**
- Gate 1 (end of Requirements): Freeze requirements before build begins
- Gate 2 (end of each Sprint): Demo acceptance before next sprint
- Gate 3 (pre-UAT): Feature-complete confirmation before UAT phase
- Gate 4 (pre-Deployment): UAT sign-off before production deployment

**Typical client dependencies:**
- Subject matter experts available for requirements workshops (8-12 hours total)
- API documentation and sandbox access for third-party integrations
- Test environment provisioning within 5 business days of kickoff
- UAT test execution by client's team (dedicated 2-4 resources for UAT phase)
- Production deployment approval from client's IT/DevOps team

## 6. Retainer / Fractional Engagement (3-12 months)

| Period | Scope | Key Deliverables | Review Cycle | Payment |
|--------|-------|-----------------|-------------|---------|
| Month 1 (Onboarding) | Ramp-up, context building, quick wins | Onboarding complete, initial assessment, 90-day roadmap | End-of-month review | Monthly in advance |
| Months 2-3 (Execution) | Active advisory + deliverables per roadmap | Per monthly scope (defined at start of each month) | Monthly review | Monthly in advance |
| Quarter 1 Review | Retrospective and scope adjustment | Quarterly business review, updated roadmap, scope/fee adjustment | Quarterly | -- |
| Months 4-6+ (Ongoing) | Continued engagement per adjusted scope | Per monthly scope | Monthly + Quarterly | Monthly in advance |

**Retainer-specific milestones:**
- 30-day onboarding complete: context absorbed, quick wins delivered, roadmap set
- Quarterly business review: performance metrics, scope adjustment, fee review
- Minimum term completion: decision point to continue, adjust, or conclude

**Typical structure:**
- X hours per month allocated to advisory calls, async support, and deliverable work
- Unused hours do not roll over (or roll over up to 25% at provider's discretion)
- Overage hours billed at agreed hourly rate with prior written approval
- Monthly scope planning meeting at start of each month (30 minutes)
- Quarterly business review (60 minutes) with written summary

## Milestone Payment Best Practices

1. **Tie payments to deliverable acceptance, not calendar dates.** "Upon acceptance of Phase 1 deliverables" is enforceable. "On March 15" is arbitrary.
2. **Front-load payments slightly.** The first payment should be 20-30% to cover ramp-up costs and demonstrate client commitment.
3. **Never put more than 35% of total value in the final payment.** If the last payment is too large, the client has leverage to delay or dispute.
4. **Include a holdback for warranty only if necessary.** 5-10% held until warranty expires is reasonable. More than that creates cash flow problems.
5. **State payment terms explicitly.** "Payment due within 15 business days of invoice." Not "upon receipt" (which has no enforceable timeline).
6. **Define what triggers an invoice.** "Service Provider will issue an invoice upon client's written acceptance of the milestone deliverables, or upon deemed acceptance per Section 4."
