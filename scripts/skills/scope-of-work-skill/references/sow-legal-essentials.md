# SOW Legal Essentials

Protective clauses and legal language patterns for Scope of Work documents. These are practitioner-level templates -- not legal advice. Recommend the client have their legal counsel review the final document for jurisdiction-specific requirements.

---

## 1. Intellectual Property

### Default: Full Transfer on Payment

> All deliverables, work product, and intellectual property created under this SOW shall become the exclusive property of the Client upon receipt of full payment. Until full payment is received, the Service Provider retains all rights to the work product.

### Pre-existing IP Carve-Out

> The Service Provider retains ownership of all pre-existing intellectual property, tools, frameworks, libraries, and methodologies that existed prior to this engagement or were developed independently outside the scope of this SOW. Where pre-existing IP is incorporated into deliverables, the Client receives a perpetual, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to use such IP solely within the context of the delivered project.

### Open-Source Components

> Deliverables may incorporate open-source software components governed by their respective licenses (e.g., MIT, Apache 2.0, GPL). The Service Provider will document all open-source dependencies and their license types in the final deliverable handoff. The Client is responsible for ongoing compliance with applicable open-source license terms.

### Portfolio Usage

> The Service Provider may reference this engagement in their portfolio, including general descriptions and non-confidential visual samples, unless the Client provides written objection within 30 days of project completion. No confidential business data, proprietary processes, or trade secrets will be disclosed.

---

## 2. Limitation of Liability

### Standard Cap at Contract Value

> The Service Provider's total aggregate liability under this SOW shall not exceed the total fees paid or payable under this SOW. This limitation applies to all claims, whether in contract, tort, negligence, strict liability, or otherwise.

### Exclusion of Consequential Damages

> In no event shall either party be liable for indirect, incidental, special, consequential, or punitive damages, including but not limited to loss of revenue, loss of profits, loss of business, or loss of data, even if the party has been advised of the possibility of such damages.

### Carve-Outs (items NOT subject to the cap)

> The foregoing limitations shall not apply to: (a) breaches of confidentiality obligations; (b) infringement of intellectual property rights; (c) gross negligence or willful misconduct; (d) the Client's payment obligations under this SOW.

## 3. Confidentiality

### Mutual Confidentiality

> Each party agrees that all information disclosed by the other party in connection with this SOW that is designated as confidential, or that reasonably should be understood to be confidential given the nature of the information, shall be treated as confidential ("Confidential Information"). Each party shall: (a) use the other party's Confidential Information solely for the purpose of performing its obligations under this SOW; (b) not disclose Confidential Information to any third party without prior written consent; (c) protect Confidential Information using the same degree of care it uses for its own confidential information, but no less than reasonable care.

### Standard Exclusions

> Confidential Information does not include information that: (a) is or becomes publicly available through no fault of the receiving party; (b) was known to the receiving party prior to disclosure; (c) is independently developed by the receiving party without use of the disclosing party's Confidential Information; (d) is disclosed with the prior written approval of the disclosing party; (e) is required to be disclosed by law, regulation, or court order, provided the receiving party gives prompt notice to the disclosing party.

### Duration

> Confidentiality obligations shall survive the termination or expiration of this SOW for a period of two (2) years, except for trade secrets, which shall remain confidential for as long as they qualify as trade secrets under applicable law.

## 4. Termination

### Termination for Convenience

> Either party may terminate this SOW upon thirty (30) days' prior written notice. Upon termination for convenience: (a) the Service Provider shall deliver all work completed through the termination date; (b) the Client shall pay for all work completed and accepted through the termination date, plus any work in progress based on percentage of completion of the current phase; (c) pre-paid fees for unperformed work shall be refunded within thirty (30) days.

### Termination for Cause

> Either party may terminate this SOW immediately upon written notice if the other party: (a) materially breaches this SOW and fails to cure such breach within fifteen (15) days of written notice; (b) becomes insolvent, files for bankruptcy, or has a receiver appointed for a substantial part of its assets.

### Effect of Termination

> Upon termination: (a) all licenses granted to the Client for completed and paid-for deliverables shall survive; (b) all Confidential Information shall be returned or destroyed upon request; (c) the provisions of this SOW that by their nature should survive termination (including Confidentiality, Limitation of Liability, and IP ownership for paid work) shall survive.

## 5. Change Orders

### Formal Change Process

> Any modification to the scope, deliverables, timeline, or pricing defined in this SOW requires a written Change Order signed by authorized representatives of both parties before the changed work begins. A Change Order shall specify: (a) the description of the change; (b) the impact on deliverables; (c) the impact on timeline; (d) the impact on pricing; (e) any changes to assumptions or dependencies.

### Change Request Procedure

> 1. Either party submits a written Change Request describing the desired modification.
> 2. The Service Provider evaluates the request and responds within five (5) business days with a Change Order proposal including cost and timeline impact.
> 3. The Client reviews and either approves, modifies, or rejects the Change Order within five (5) business days.
> 4. No work on the change shall begin until the Change Order is signed by both parties.
> 5. If the parties cannot agree on the terms of a Change Order, the original SOW remains in effect.

### Emergency Changes

> In cases where a change is urgently required (e.g., critical defect in production, regulatory requirement), the Service Provider may begin work on verbal authorization from the Client's designated point of contact, provided a written Change Order is executed within three (3) business days. Verbal authorization shall be documented by the Service Provider via email summary within 24 hours.

## 6. Warranty

### Defect Correction Period

> The Service Provider warrants that all deliverables will conform to the specifications and acceptance criteria defined in this SOW for a period of thirty (30) days following final acceptance ("Warranty Period"). During the Warranty Period, the Service Provider will correct any defects or non-conformities at no additional cost. This warranty applies only to defects in the delivered work and does not cover issues caused by modifications made by the Client or third parties after delivery.

### Exclusions

> This warranty does not cover: (a) defects caused by the Client's modification of deliverables; (b) use of deliverables outside their intended purpose or specifications; (c) issues arising from Client-provided content, data, or third-party systems; (d) changes to third-party platforms, APIs, or services that occur after delivery.

## 7. Force Majeure

> Neither party shall be liable for delays or failure to perform its obligations under this SOW due to causes beyond its reasonable control, including but not limited to: natural disasters, pandemics, acts of government, war, terrorism, labor disputes, internet service disruptions, or infrastructure failures. The affected party shall provide prompt written notice and use reasonable efforts to mitigate the impact. If a force majeure event continues for more than sixty (60) days, either party may terminate this SOW without penalty.

## 8. Governing Law and Disputes

### EU-Based Engagements

> This SOW shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of [Jurisdiction]. Any dispute arising out of or in connection with this SOW shall first be attempted to be resolved through good-faith negotiation. If unresolved within thirty (30) days, the dispute shall be submitted to mediation. If mediation fails, either party may pursue binding arbitration under the rules of [Arbitration Body] or seek resolution before the courts of [Jurisdiction].

### Practical Note

> For most freelance and agency SOW documents, a simple governing law clause and agreement to negotiate in good faith is sufficient. Full arbitration clauses are typically reserved for engagements above EUR 50K or where the parties are in different jurisdictions. Always specify the governing law -- leaving it unstated creates ambiguity in cross-border engagements.

---

## 9. Entire Agreement

> This SOW, together with any governing Master Services Agreement and any executed Change Orders, constitutes the entire agreement between the parties with respect to the subject matter hereof and supersedes all prior or contemporaneous agreements, understandings, and communications, whether written or oral. No amendment to this SOW shall be effective unless in writing and signed by both parties.

---

## Usage Notes

- These clauses are templates. Adapt language to the specific engagement and jurisdiction.
- For engagements over EUR 50K, recommend both parties involve legal counsel in review.
- For international engagements, pay specific attention to governing law, IP transfer rules (which vary by jurisdiction), and data protection (GDPR for EU-based work).
- When the SOW is governed by an MSA, many of these clauses will already be covered. In that case, the SOW should reference the MSA and only include scope-specific terms.
