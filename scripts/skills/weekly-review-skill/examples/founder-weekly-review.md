# Example: Founder Weekly Review

## Context

The user is the solo founder of a B2B SaaS startup (12 employees, $600K ARR). They've been doing weekly reviews for a few weeks and are feeling pulled in too many directions.

---

## Conversation

**Coach:** Ready for your review. How are you feeling about this week?

**User:** Exhausted but wired. One of those weeks where I was busy every minute but I'm not sure I moved the ball forward on anything important.

**Coach:** Busy without progress — that's a specific kind of frustrating. What were you focused on this week?

**User:** We had a big customer escalation on Monday that ate most of Tuesday too. Wednesday I was in hiring interviews for the senior engineer role. Thursday was a board prep meeting and then a sales demo for a potential enterprise deal. Friday I tried to do product work but kept getting pulled into Slack threads.

**Coach:** So: firefighting, hiring, board prep, sales, and attempted product work. That's five categories in five days. Let's start with wins. What went well?

**User:** The sales demo went really well actually. The prospect — a Series C fintech company — said they'd never seen a product demo that well-tailored to their use case. They're moving to a technical evaluation next week.

**Coach:** That's a strong signal. An enterprise deal would be meaningful at your stage. Anything else?

**User:** The customer escalation — we resolved it by Wednesday and the customer actually said they were impressed by how fast we responded. Turned a potential churn into a positive NPS moment. And one of the engineering candidates was excellent. Probably the best we've seen.

**Coach:** Three real wins: a hot enterprise prospect, a save that improved customer sentiment, and a strong hire candidate. These aren't small. Now the other side — what was the hardest part?

**User:** I didn't touch the product roadmap. We have a strategic feature that I've been saying is a priority for three weeks now and I haven't written a single spec for it. Every week something else takes precedence.

**Coach:** Three weeks. Is this a new pattern or does this happen regularly — strategic work getting displaced by operational urgency?

**User:** Regularly. Honestly, since we hit 10 people, I've been doing less and less product work and more and more... everything else. Hiring, customer issues, sales, board stuff. But the product is where I add the most value. Nobody else on the team can do the product vision work.

**Coach:** So the thing that only you can do keeps losing to things others could handle. That's a structural problem, not a discipline problem. We'll dig into that. What did you learn this week?

**User:** I think I learned that I'm the bottleneck on the customer escalation process. The support team flagged the issue on Sunday evening but waited until I saw it on Monday morning to escalate. We lost 12 hours because they didn't have authority to act without me.

**Coach:** Is this a new realization or something you've known but haven't addressed?

**User:** I've known. But this week it cost us. The customer was upset that we didn't respond until Monday when they reported on Sunday.

**Coach:** So the lesson is clear and the cost is measurable. What would need to change?

**User:** I need to give the support lead authority to initiate the escalation playbook without my approval. And honestly, we need an actual escalation playbook — right now it's just "ask the founder."

**Coach:** That's a delegation issue and a process issue. Both fixable. Let's check energy. Scale of 1-10?

**User:** 5. But that's propped up by caffeine. Probably a real 3 or 4.

**Coach:** How does that compare to a month ago?

**User:** Lower. It's been trending down since we started hiring in earnest. The hiring process takes more out of me than I expected. Not the interviews themselves — the constant context of evaluating people, worrying about making the wrong hire, managing the recruiter.

**Coach:** So hiring is a sustained energy drain beyond the time it takes. What gave you energy this week?

**User:** The sales demo. When I'm in front of a customer showing the product and seeing them get it — that's electric. And the brief moments on Friday when I was sketching product ideas before Slack pulled me back.

**Coach:** Sales and product. Those are your flow states. What did you avoid this week?

**User:** The product spec. And honestly, I avoided having a conversation with my co-founder about splitting responsibilities. We've both been doing a bit of everything and it's not working. Someone needs to own ops and someone needs to own product. But that conversation feels loaded because it also means admitting we haven't been working well together lately.

**Coach:** That's a big one. We'll come back to it. Looking at next week — what are the 3 most important things?

**User:** Hmm. The enterprise deal technical evaluation prep — if we win this account it changes our trajectory. The product spec for the strategic feature — three weeks is too long. And... I want to say hiring, but honestly the engineering candidate either accepts our offer or doesn't. The ball is in their court.

**Coach:** So two clear priorities: enterprise deal prep and product spec. What about the third?

**User:** I think the third should be the conversation with my co-founder about role clarity. It's overdue and it's blocking everything else structurally.

**Coach:** That's a courageous choice for a third priority. For each of these — what does done look like?

**User:** Enterprise deal prep done means: a technical evaluation environment set up, demo data loaded for their use case, a one-pager answering their security questionnaire, and a follow-up email sent to schedule the evaluation. Product spec done means: a 2-3 page document covering the problem, the proposed solution, the technical approach, and success metrics — enough for the engineering team to estimate. Co-founder conversation done means: a 1-hour sit-down where we walk out with a written agreement on who owns what.

**Coach:** Clear outcomes. Is there anything you should drop from your plate next week?

**User:** I need to hand the remaining customer escalation follow-up to the support lead. And I should skip the team all-hands — the team doesn't need me in that meeting, they need the engineering manager to run it.

**Coach:** Two solid delegation moves. What's one thing you'll do differently?

**User:** I'm going to block Thursday as a full product day. No meetings, no Slack, phone on airplane mode. I'm going to a coffee shop and writing that spec.

**Coach:** An entire day. That's ambitious and exactly what the spec needs. What's one thing you'll say no to?

**User:** I'll say no to the recruiter who wants to show me three more candidates this week. I'll tell them we have a strong candidate in play and I'm pausing new interviews until we hear back. That recovers probably 4 hours.

**Coach:** Sharp prioritization. One more thing — the co-founder conversation. What's the worst realistic outcome?

**User:** The worst realistic outcome is that he wants product and I want product, and we can't agree on who gets it. But honestly, I think he knows he's better at ops and customer success. I think the conversation will go better than I'm imagining. It's the anticipation that's worse than the thing.

**Coach:** That's usually the case with avoided conversations. Having it might be the most valuable thing you do next week. Here's your review summary.

---

## Generated Review Summary

# Weekly Review — Week of [Date]

## Wins
- Enterprise prospect (Series C fintech) moving to technical evaluation after a strong demo — they specifically praised the tailored approach
- Customer escalation resolved by Wednesday; turned a potential churn into positive NPS — customer praised response speed
- Identified an excellent senior engineering candidate — potentially the strongest in the pipeline

## Challenges
- Product roadmap work displaced for the third consecutive week by operational demands (escalation, hiring, board prep, sales)
- Structural issue identified: the founder is doing work others could handle while the work only the founder can do (product vision) gets deprioritized
- Support escalation process has a single point of failure (founder approval) — cost 12 hours on this week's incident
- Co-founder role clarity conversation has been avoided — both founders doing "a bit of everything" is degrading effectiveness

## Lessons Learned
- Support team needs authority and a documented escalation playbook to act without founder approval. Current process: "ask the founder" is not scalable.
- Hiring is an energy drain beyond its time cost — the sustained cognitive load of evaluating people and managing the process is underestimated in founder time budgets.
- Being busy across 5 categories in 5 days is a sign of role sprawl, not effective leadership.

## Energy: 3-4/10 (reported as 5, adjusted for caffeine)
- Trending down over the past month, coinciding with intensified hiring
- Energy drains: context-switching across 5+ work categories daily, hiring process cognitive load, unresolved co-founder tension
- Energy sources: customer-facing product demos (flow state), product sketching/thinking, solving customer problems directly

## Next Week's Priorities
1. **Enterprise deal technical evaluation prep** — Done when: evaluation environment set up, demo data loaded, security questionnaire one-pager complete, follow-up email sent to schedule. High-revenue impact.
2. **Product spec for strategic feature** — Done when: 2-3 page document covering problem, solution, technical approach, and success metrics. Enough for engineering team to estimate. Three weeks overdue.
3. **Co-founder role clarity conversation** — Done when: 1-hour sit-down with written agreement on who owns what. Structurally unblocks everything else.

## Commitments
- **Start:** Blocking Thursday as a full product day — no meetings, no Slack, phone on airplane mode, coffee shop, spec gets written.
- **Stop:** Reviewing new engineering candidates until current top candidate responds. Saves ~4 hours.
- **Continue:** Doing customer-facing demos personally — it's a competitive advantage and an energy source. Don't delegate this yet.

## Delegations This Week
- Hand remaining customer escalation follow-up to the support lead
- Have engineering manager run the team all-hands instead of attending personally
- Pause new candidate reviews with recruiter
- Action item: create an escalation playbook and give support lead authority to initiate without founder approval

## Open Questions
- Co-founder conversation: what if both want to own product? Need a framework for that conversation beyond "I think he knows."
- Energy at 3-4 is unsustainable. If it doesn't recover with structural changes (delegation, role clarity), what's the intervention? Vacation? Coaching? Admitting the current pace is not working?
- Enterprise deal: if the evaluation goes well, do we have the capacity to onboard an enterprise customer right now? Or does winning the deal create a new fire?
