# Example: Freelancer Weekly Review

## Context

The user is a freelance UX designer with 3 active clients. They've had a stressful week and want to do their first weekly review.

---

## Conversation

**Coach:** Let's do your weekly review. What kind of work are you doing? Freelance, in-house, startup?

**User:** Freelance UX designer. Three clients right now.

**Coach:** Got it. Before we dive in — how are you feeling right now, in one word?

**User:** Scattered.

**Coach:** Scattered. Okay. Let's sort through it. What were you working on this week?

**User:** Client A (a fintech app) had me doing user testing sessions. Client B (e-commerce rebrand) needed wireframes for their checkout flow. And Client C (a small SaaS startup) just onboarded me this week, so I was doing discovery and kickoff meetings. Plus I had to do invoicing and a bunch of admin stuff.

**Coach:** Three clients at different stages, plus admin. That's a lot of context-switching. Let's start with what went right. What went well this week?

**User:** Honestly? The user testing sessions for Client A went really well. Found a major usability issue in their onboarding flow that nobody had caught. The product team was impressed.

**Coach:** That's a significant find. The kind of thing that justifies your rate. Anything else, even small?

**User:** I guess I finally sent the invoice to Client B that I'd been putting off for two weeks. And the kickoff with Client C went smoothly — they seem like good people.

**Coach:** Three wins: a high-impact finding for Client A, closing an admin loop with Client B's invoice, and a solid start with a new client. Don't brush past those. Now let's look at the other side. What was the hardest part of this week?

**User:** I couldn't get any deep work done on the wireframes for Client B. Every day I planned to start them and every day something interrupted me — a call, an email that "needed" a quick reply, the Client C onboarding prep. By Friday I still hadn't made meaningful progress on the wireframes and the deadline is next Wednesday.

**Coach:** That sounds frustrating. Is this a one-time thing because of the new client onboarding, or does this happen often — planning deep work that gets hijacked?

**User:** It happens often. Like, almost every week there's some project where I don't make the progress I planned. But this week was worse because I had the onboarding on top of everything.

**Coach:** So the new client was the extra pressure, but the underlying pattern — deep work getting displaced — that's been going on for a while.

**User:** Yeah. Weeks.

**Coach:** We'll come back to that. Let's pull out the lesson. What did you learn this week — about the work or about yourself?

**User:** I think I learned that three active clients is my max. Maybe even one too many. With two clients I can manage, but three means I'm constantly switching between different projects, different stakeholders, different design systems. I don't have enough blocks of uninterrupted time.

**Coach:** That's a valuable and honest observation. How will you apply this going forward?

**User:** I think I need to either stagger clients better — not have three in active production at the same time — or raise my rates so I can take fewer clients for the same income.

**Coach:** Both are good options. And they're not mutually exclusive. Let's check your energy. On a scale of 1-10, how's your energy right now?

**User:** Maybe a 4. I feel like I worked a lot this week but didn't accomplish enough for the effort I put in.

**Coach:** Higher or lower than Monday?

**User:** About the same. I started the week already tired from last week.

**Coach:** So you're entering next week on a 4. That's a flag. What drained you the most?

**User:** The context-switching. Going from a user testing session for Client A directly into wireframing for Client B with a completely different design system and problem space. My brain can't shift that fast. By the afternoon I'm useless.

**Coach:** Were there any tasks this week that gave you energy — where you were in flow?

**User:** The user testing sessions. When I'm observing real users and finding patterns, I lose track of time. That's my favorite part of the work.

**Coach:** Any tasks you kept putting off?

**User:** The Client B wireframes. And honestly, sending a scope document to Client C that I promised on Monday and still haven't done.

**Coach:** What's behind the avoidance on the wireframes?

**User:** Partly the switching cost — I need a big block to get into it. And partly... I think the checkout flow is genuinely hard. I don't love the constraints Client B gave me. Their business requirements conflict with good UX and I need to find a compromise. That takes creative energy I haven't had.

**Coach:** So it's not laziness — it's a complex problem that requires deep focus you haven't been able to access. Before we plan next week — is there anything you need to cancel, delegate, or postpone to recover? Planning on top of a 4 out of 10 doesn't work.

**User:** I should probably push back on the Thursday afternoon call with Client A. It's a status update that could be an async message. That would free up a 2-hour block.

**Coach:** Good instinct. Let's look ahead. What are the 3 most important things for next week?

**User:** Finish the wireframes for Client B — that's the Wednesday deadline. Deliver the scope document to Client C — it's already late. And... prepare the presentation of the user testing findings for Client A's stakeholder meeting on Friday.

**Coach:** Three clear priorities. For each one — what does done look like?

**User:** Wireframes done means a complete Figma file with the checkout flow — 6 screens, mobile and desktop — ready for client review. Scope document done means a PDF with project timeline, deliverables, and communication cadence sent to Client C. Testing presentation done means a 15-slide deck with findings, severity ratings, and recommendations.

**Coach:** Those are concrete. Is there anything on your plate next week that you should drop or delegate?

**User:** I was going to redesign my portfolio this weekend. I should postpone that — it's been on my list for months and another week won't hurt. And I should ask Client A if the Thursday call can be async.

**Coach:** Smart calls. One last thing. What's one thing you'll do differently next week?

**User:** I'm going to block Tuesday morning and Wednesday morning — 9am to noon — as no-meetings, no-email deep work blocks. The wireframes get those blocks.

**Coach:** Specific. Actionable. I like it. What's one thing you'll say no to?

**User:** I'll say no to the Client A Thursday call and propose a Loom video instead.

- Three active clients is the current max. Two in active production is sustainable; three creates a context-switching tax that degrades quality on all three.
- Options to address: stagger client phases better, or raise rates to reduce client count at the same income level
