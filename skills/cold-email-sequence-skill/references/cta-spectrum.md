# CTA Spectrum

Call-to-action options organized from lowest friction to highest friction. Match the CTA intensity to the email's position in the sequence and the prospect's familiarity with you.

---

## The Spectrum

### Level 1: Interest Check (Lowest Friction)
**Ask:** Is this topic even relevant to you?
**When to use:** Email 1 to cold prospects, C-suite targets, or when you're unsure of fit.
**Why it works:** Requires only a mental yes/no, not a calendar commitment.

**Examples:**
- "Is this something you're thinking about?"
- "Worth exploring?"
- "On your radar?"
- "Is [challenge] a priority right now?"
- "Sound relevant?"

**Best practice:** Follow with a silent qualifier: "If not, no worries at all." This removes pressure and paradoxically increases responses.

---

### Level 2: Soft Conversation (Low Friction)
**Ask:** Would you be open to a conversation?
**When to use:** Email 1 (alternative to Level 1), Email 2 follow-up.
**Why it works:** "Open to" is softer than "do you want to" — it asks about willingness, not commitment.

**Examples:**
- "Open to a quick chat about this?"
- "Would it make sense to talk?"
- "Happy to share more if you're curious."
- "Want me to send over a few specifics?"
- "I can elaborate — interested?"

**Best practice:** Don't specify a time length. "15 minutes" is fine, but "quick chat" feels even lighter.

---

### Level 3: Specific Ask (Medium Friction)
**Ask:** Can we schedule a specific time?
**When to use:** Email 3 (after you've provided value), or Email 2 if the prospect is warm.
**Why it works:** Specific asks get specific answers. Vague asks get ignored.

**Examples:**
- "Do you have 15 minutes this week?"
- "Could we do a brief call Thursday or Friday?"
- "Would Tuesday or Wednesday morning work for a quick call?"
- "I'm free [specific times] — any of those work?"
- "Can I walk you through this in 15 minutes?"

**Best practice:** Offer 2-3 specific time slots. This makes it a selection decision, not a scheduling decision.

---

### Level 4: Calendar Link (Medium-High Friction)
**Ask:** Book a time on my calendar.
**When to use:** Email 3 or 4, only after establishing relevance. Never in Email 1.
**Why it works:** Reduces back-and-forth scheduling. But it also asks the prospect to take an action (clicking, selecting, confirming) which is higher friction.

**Examples:**
- "Here's my calendar if it's easier: [link]"
- "Pick a time that works: [link]"
- "If this is relevant, grab 15 minutes here: [link]"

**Best practice:** Always pair a calendar link with a softer alternative: "Or just reply with a day that works and I'll send an invite."

---

### Level 5: Direct Commitment (High Friction)
**Ask:** Commit to a specific meeting, demo, or next step.
**When to use:** Only after multiple positive signals, or for warm inbound leads.
**Why it works:** Direct and efficient when the prospect is already interested.

**Examples:**
- "Can I schedule a 30-minute demo for your team?"
- "I'd like to present this to you and [other stakeholder]. Can we set that up?"
- "Ready to see this in action? I can have a demo ready by [date]."

**Best practice:** Almost never appropriate for cold email. Reserve for follow-up after they've expressed interest.

---

### Level 6: Binary Close (Breakup CTA)
**Ask:** Yes or no — should I keep reaching out or close the loop?
**When to use:** Final email in the sequence (Email 4 or 5).
**Why it works:** Gives the prospect a low-friction way to respond. Even a "not now" is useful because it opens a conversation.

**Examples:**
- "Should I keep this on your radar, or is this not a fit?"
- "Is the timing wrong, or is this not relevant?"
- "Happy to reconnect in Q[X] if now isn't right. Just say the word."
- "Should I close the loop on this?"
- "Not the right time? No problem — when should I circle back?"

**Best practice:** The breakup email often gets the highest response rate in the sequence. People respond to closure. Make it easy for them to say "not now" — many will turn into "actually, let's talk."

## CTA Selection Matrix

| Sequence Position | Prospect Temperature | Best CTA Level |
|-------------------|---------------------|----------------|
| Email 1 | Cold | Level 1 (Interest Check) |
| Email 1 | Warm (referral, inbound) | Level 2-3 (Soft to Specific) |
| Email 2 | Cold, no reply | Level 2 (Soft Conversation) or no CTA (pure value) |
| Email 3 | Cold, no reply | Level 3 (Specific Ask) |
| Email 3 | Warm, no reply | Level 3-4 (Specific or Calendar) |
| Email 4 (breakup) | Any | Level 6 (Binary Close) |
| Email 5 (breakup) | Any | Level 6 (Binary Close) |

## CTA Anti-Patterns

### Never Use:
- **"Let me know"** — Passive, easy to ignore, no specific action
- **"Feel free to reach out"** — Puts the burden on them with no incentive
- **"I'd love to connect"** — What does "connect" mean? Be specific.
- **"When would be a good time?"** — Too open-ended, decision fatigue
- **"Can I send you some information?"** — You're already sending information. This is a stall tactic from the prospect that you're preemptively offering.
- **"Let's jump on a call"** — "Jump on a call" is corporate speak. Say "quick chat" or "15-minute call."
- **"Are you the right person?"** — Makes you look lost. Research who you're emailing.
- **"Would you be interested in learning more?"** — Vague. About what? Why?

### Never Do:
- **Two CTAs in one email.** One email, one ask. Multiple asks = no action.
- **CTA in a P.S.** The P.S. is for a personal touch or secondary hook, not the primary CTA.
- **CTA that requires more than 10 seconds to respond to.** "Reply with a thumbs up if interested" < "Please review this deck and let me know your thoughts."
- **Same CTA in every email.** Vary the ask across the sequence. If Email 1 asked for a chat and got no response, Email 2 should try a different angle (provide value), not repeat the same ask.

## CTA + Subject Line Pairing

The subject line sets the expectation. The CTA delivers on it.

| Subject Line Type | CTA Type |
|------------------|----------|
| Question subject | Interest check CTA (Level 1) |
| Observation subject | Soft conversation CTA (Level 2) |
| Result subject | Specific ask CTA (Level 3) |
| Resource subject | No CTA (the value is the CTA) |
| Breakup subject | Binary close CTA (Level 6) |
