# Example: Insight Post

## User Input

> I'm a freelance UX designer. I noticed that my best-performing projects are always the ones where the client had a bad experience with a previous designer. I want to write a post about how "difficult" clients are actually the best clients.

---

## Post Analysis

**Post type:** Insight
**Hook strategy:** Counterintuitive claim (Pattern #1) — challenges the assumption that difficult clients are bad
**Target audience:** Freelance designers and consultants
**Goal:** Authority + engagement

---

## Generated Post

The clients everyone warns you about? They're my most profitable projects.

I've tracked this for 3 years. Out of my top 10 projects by revenue AND satisfaction, 7 came from clients who'd fired their previous designer.

They aren't "difficult." They're experienced buyers.

They know exactly what went wrong last time. They have specific expectations. They push back on timelines because they've been burned by missed deadlines. They ask hard questions about your process because the last person didn't have one.

The clients who've never worked with a designer before? Those are the ones who ghost after the second revision. Who change the brief mid-project. Who say "I'll know it when I see it."

The supposedly difficult client emails you at 10 PM with detailed feedback organized by priority. The "easy" client disappears for 3 weeks and then says "it's not what I imagined."

I'll take demanding and engaged over easy and absent every single time.

#uxdesign #freelancing

## Why This Post Works

1. **Hook is counterintuitive** — "The clients everyone warns you about" sets up a pattern break
2. **Backed by data** — "tracked for 3 years, 7 out of 10" gives credibility
3. **Reframes the narrative** — "not difficult, experienced buyers" is a fresh perspective
4. **Specific contrasts** — "emails at 10 PM with detailed feedback" vs "disappears for 3 weeks" — these are real, specific situations
5. **No engagement bait** — ends with a clear position, not "agree?"
6. **Under 900 characters** — scannable, respects reader's time
