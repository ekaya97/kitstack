# Anti-Patterns

A comprehensive list of banned phrases, formats, structural patterns, and writing habits that mark content as AI-generated, engagement bait, or LinkedIn cliche. Every post must be checked against this list before output.

---

## Banned Phrases (English)

| Phrase | Why It's Banned | What to Do Instead |
|--------|----------------|-------------------|
| "Agree?" / "Thoughts?" | Empty engagement bait | End with your conclusion, not a question begging for validation |
| "This." / "Read that again." | Fake emphasis; condescending | Let the content speak for itself |
| "Let that sink in." | Implies the reader is too slow to understand | Cut it |
| "I'm humbled to announce..." | Nobody is humbled by their own achievement | "Excited to share" or just announce it |
| "I'm grateful for the opportunity..." | Corporate cliche | Be specific about what you're grateful for, or skip it |
| "In today's fast-paced world..." | Generic filler | Start with the specific point |
| "Here's the thing." | Throat-clearing | Delete and start with "the thing" |
| "It's not about X, it's about Y." | Overused dramatic reframing | Just say what it IS about |
| "Let me be honest..." / "Can I be real?" | Implies you're normally dishonest | Just be honest without announcing it |
| "Game-changer" / "Disruptive" | Meaningless buzzwords | Describe the specific impact |
| "Thought leader" / "Thought leadership" | Self-applied title that sounds self-important | Demonstrate expertise, don't claim it |
| "If you're not doing X, you're falling behind" | Fear-based manipulation | "Here's why X matters: [evidence]" |
| "Who else needs to hear this?" | Engagement bait | Cut it |
| "Tag someone who..." | Explicit engagement bait | Cut it |
| "Follow me for more [topic]" | Needy and obvious | If the post is good, they'll follow |
| "I'd love to hear your take" | Often inauthentic | Ask a specific, genuine question instead |
| "Not X. Y." (dramatic reveal) | "He didn't give me a job. He gave me a purpose." Overused to the point of parody. | Write normally |
| "Most people think X. They're wrong." | Condescending setup | "The common approach to X has a problem..." |
| "Unpopular opinion:" | Usually followed by a popular opinion | If it's truly unpopular, the content will show that |
| "Stop doing X" (imperative) | Preachy and commanding | "I stopped doing X. Here's what happened." |
| "The truth about X" | Clickbait format | Just share the insight directly |

| Phrase | Why It's Banned | What to Do Instead |
|--------|----------------|-------------------|
| "Wer das versteht, versteht alles." | Gatekeeping; arrogant | Share the insight directly without gatekeeping |
| "Das ist keine X. Das ist Y." | Overused dramatic reframing (German LinkedIn meme) | Just describe Y directly |
| "Ich bin dankbar/geehrt..." | Same as English "humbled" — corporate cliche | Be specific or skip it |

## Banned Structural Patterns

### The LinkedIn Staircase
```
Every.
Single.
Sentence.
Is.
Its.
Own.
Paragraph.
```
**Why it's banned:** Artificially inflates post length, creates false dramatic pacing, and is the #1 signal that content was AI-generated or copied from a "LinkedIn growth" course.
**What to do instead:** Use natural paragraph breaks (2-3 sentences per paragraph). One-sentence paragraphs are fine for genuine emphasis — but not for every sentence.

### The Emoji Bullet List
```
Here are 5 things I learned:
🔥 Thing one
💡 Thing two
🎯 Thing three
🚀 Thing four
💪 Thing five
```
**Why it's banned:** Looks like content marketing, not personal insight. The emojis add nothing and reduce the content to a listicle.
**What to do instead:** If you need a list, use numbered text. Better yet, pick the ONE most interesting item and write about it in depth.

### The Fake Conversation
```
"You should take the job."
"But what about my startup?"
"Your startup isn't paying your bills."
"..."
That conversation changed everything.
```
**Why it's banned:** Usually fabricated or heavily dramatized. The "..." and "changed everything" are red flags.
**What to do instead:** If you had a real conversation that mattered, describe it in your own words with specific context.

### The Engagement Pod Format
```
Great insight from [tagged person]! 

[Copy-pasted quote from their post]

I agree 100%. [Tagged person] is such a [positive adjective].

What do you all think? Drop your thoughts below! 👇
```
**Why it's banned:** Obviously inauthentic, designed to trigger algorithmic engagement through tags and comments.
**What to do instead:** If someone's post genuinely inspired a thought, share your own original take. Credit them. Don't copy-paste their words.

### The Humble Brag
```
I never thought I'd say this, but we just hit $1M ARR.

I'm not sharing this to brag. I'm sharing because [several paragraphs of context that are definitely bragging].

Here are 10 things I learned on the journey:
```
**Why it's banned:** The disclaimer ("not to brag") is transparent. If you have a genuine achievement, own it — or share the specific lesson, not the number.
**What to do instead:** "We hit $1M ARR. The one thing that made the difference was..." — Be direct, focus on the insight, not the flex.

### The Infinite Hashtag
```
#leadership #management #growth #business #startup #entrepreneur #founder #sales #marketing #productivity #motivation #success #tech #innovation #AI
```
**Why it's banned:** Looks desperate and spammy. Dilutes your content's signal.
**What to do instead:** 0-3 hashtags that are genuinely relevant to the post's topic. Or none.

## Writing Habits to Avoid

### 1. Over-Qualifying
**Bad:** "I think it might be worth considering that perhaps..."
**Good:** "Here's what I've seen work."
Be direct. Hedging kills authority.

### 2. Starting Every Post with "I"
Vary your openings. Start with an observation, a number, a question, or a scene.

### 3. Using Corporate Vocabulary
Replace:
- "Leverage" → "use"
- "Ideate" → "brainstorm" or "think about"
- "Deliverables" → "what we produce" or "the work"
- "Synergize" → never use this word
- "Thought leadership" → "expertise" or "perspective"
- "Deep dive" → "detailed look" or just... go into detail
- "Pivot" → "change direction"
- "Value-add" → "useful" or describe the specific value

### 4. Padding for Length
If the post is 600 characters and it's complete, publish it at 600 characters. Short posts that make one point well outperform long posts that make one point slowly.

### 5. Formulaic Closings
Don't end every post the same way. Vary between:
- A concluding insight (strongest)
- A specific question for the audience (genuine, not "agree?")
- A forward-looking statement
- Nothing — sometimes the last sentence IS the ending

## The Pre-Publish Checklist

Before finalizing any post, check:

- [ ] Does it pass the "could someone else have written this?" test?
- [ ] Is there at least one specific detail (number, name, date, amount)?
- [ ] Are there zero banned phrases from the lists above?
- [ ] Is every paragraph 3 lines or shorter (on mobile)?
- [ ] Is the total length under 1,300 characters (unless exceptional)?
- [ ] Does the hook create genuine curiosity?
- [ ] Does the post deliver on the hook's promise?
- [ ] Would the user actually say these words out loud?
- [ ] Are there zero or minimal emojis (max 1-2, no emoji bullets)?
- [ ] Is the CTA (if any) genuine, not engagement bait?
