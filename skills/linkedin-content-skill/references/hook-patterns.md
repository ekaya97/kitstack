# Hook Patterns

20+ opening line patterns for LinkedIn posts, organized by post type. The first 1-2 lines determine whether anyone reads the rest.

---

## Principles

1. **The hook must work as a standalone sentence.** It should make sense and create curiosity without the rest of the post.
2. **Specific is always better.** Numbers, names, timeframes, amounts — the more specific, the more compelling.
3. **Create a gap.** The reader should feel: "I need to know more." This can be an information gap, a curiosity gap, or an emotional gap.
4. **Front-load the value.** LinkedIn shows 2-3 lines before "see more." Those lines must earn the click.
5. **No cheap tricks.** No clickbait, no misleading setups, no empty dramatic statements.

---

## Pattern 1: The Counterintuitive Claim
**How:** State something that contradicts what your audience believes.
**Best for:** Insight posts, contrarian posts.

**Examples:**
- "The best salespeople never pitch in the first meeting."
- "Your most profitable client is probably your smallest one."
- "Networking events are the worst place to network."
- "The most productive hour of my day is the one where I do nothing."

---

## Pattern 2: The Specific Result
**How:** Lead with a concrete, quantified outcome.
**Best for:** How-I posts, data posts.

**Examples:**
- "We increased proposal win rates from 23% to 61% with one change."
- "I write all my client proposals in 45 minutes."
- "One email generated $280,000 in pipeline last quarter."
- "Our churn rate dropped from 8% to 1.2% in 6 months."

---

## Pattern 3: The Confession
**How:** Admit to something specific that most people wouldn't say publicly.
**Best for:** Story posts, mistake/lesson posts.

**Examples:**
- "I fired our biggest client three months ago."
- "My first hire was a disaster. And it was entirely my fault."
- "I underpriced a project by $40,000 because I was afraid to say no."
- "I've been running my business for 5 years and I still hate networking."

---

## Pattern 4: The Timestamp
**How:** Anchor to a specific moment in time.
**Best for:** Story posts, behind-the-scenes posts.

**Examples:**
- "Three years ago, I was one month from shutting down my business."
- "At 2 AM on a Tuesday, I realized our entire pricing model was wrong."
- "Six months into my new role, I discovered a $200K problem nobody was talking about."
- "Last Thursday, a client said something that changed how I think about proposals."

---

## Pattern 5: The Pattern Recognition
**How:** Call out a pattern you've observed across multiple data points.
**Best for:** Insight posts, data posts, curated observation posts.

**Examples:**
- "After reviewing 200 contracts this year, one clause stands out as the most dangerous."
- "I've interviewed 50 agency founders. They all regret the same thing."
- "Every successful product launch I've seen shares three characteristics."
- "The same mistake shows up in 80% of the pricing strategies I review."

---

## Pattern 6: The Unexpected Juxtaposition
**How:** Put two things together that don't seem to belong.
**Best for:** Insight posts, framework posts.

**Examples:**
- "The best business advice I ever got came from a restaurant chef."
- "What poker taught me about pricing consulting services."
- "The military's planning framework is the best tool I've found for quarterly planning."
- "A children's librarian showed me the best presentation structure."

---

## Pattern 7: The Bold Statement
**How:** Make a strong, definitive claim.
**Best for:** Contrarian posts, insight posts.

**Examples:**
- "Cold calling is not dead. Your cold calling is dead."
- "Nobody needs a personal brand. They need a professional reputation."
- "The best marketing strategy is a great product."
- "Your team doesn't need more meetings. They need fewer, better ones."

---

## Pattern 8: The Question (Done Right)
**How:** Ask a genuinely interesting question that your audience has an opinion on. NOT a rhetorical question with an obvious answer.
**Best for:** Question posts, curated observation posts.

**Examples:**
- "Honest question: how do you decide when a client relationship has become unprofitable?"
- "When was the last time you raised your prices? And what happened?"
- "If you could only measure one metric in your business, what would it be?"
- "What's the most expensive lesson your business taught you this year?"

**AVOID:** "Want to grow your revenue?" / "Do you struggle with time management?" / "Have you ever felt stuck?" — These have obvious answers and aren't real questions.

---

## Pattern 9: The Observation
**How:** Share something you noticed — in your industry, in your work, in a meeting.
**Best for:** Curated observation posts, insight posts.

**Examples:**
- "Noticed something interesting in three pitch meetings this month."
- "I've been tracking one metric for 6 months that nobody talks about."
- "Something shifted in the freelance market this quarter."
- "The way enterprise buyers evaluate tools has changed — and most sellers haven't noticed."

---

## Pattern 10: The "I Used to / Now I"
**How:** Show a before/after shift in your thinking or approach.
**Best for:** Insight posts, mistake posts.

**Examples:**
- "I used to send 10-page proposals. Now I send 2 pages. Win rate went up."
- "Two years ago, I charged hourly. Now I charge project-based. Here's why."
- "I used to dread client feedback calls. Now they're my most productive hour."
- "My hiring process used to be 5 rounds. Now it's 2. Better hires."

---

## Pattern 11: The List Entry
**How:** Open with a provocative item from a list (then provide context).
**Best for:** Framework posts, how-I posts.

**Examples:**
- "Rule #1 of my consulting practice: never send a proposal the same day you get the brief."
- "The most important line in any contract isn't about money."
- "There's one question I ask in every single client meeting."
- "My business has three rules. The most important: never compete on price."

---

## Pattern 12: The Scene
**How:** Drop the reader into a specific moment.
**Best for:** Story posts.

**Examples:**
- "The Zoom call had been silent for 8 seconds. The client was thinking about my price."
- "I was reading the contract on a Sunday night when I saw it."
- "My phone rang at 7 AM. It was my biggest client's CEO."
- "Halfway through the presentation, I realized I had the wrong deck open."

---

## Pattern 13: The Reframe
**How:** Take a common framing and flip it.
**Best for:** Contrarian posts, insight posts.

**Examples:**
- "Your biggest competitor isn't another company. It's your prospect doing nothing."
- "The problem isn't that you have too much to do. It's that you haven't decided what not to do."
- "Freelancers don't have a sales problem. They have a positioning problem."
- "You don't need more leads. You need better qualification."

---

## Pattern 14: The Data Point
**How:** Lead with a single, striking number.
**Best for:** Data posts, insight posts.

**Examples:**
- "73% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free buying experience."
- "The average SaaS company loses 30% of its customers every year."
- "I've tracked every proposal I've sent since 2021. The data is clear."
- "47% of freelancers undercharge by more than 40%."

---

## Pattern 15: The "Most People" Opener
**How:** Describe what most people do, then pivot to what works.
**Best for:** Insight posts, how-I posts.

**Examples:**
- "Most freelancers set their rates by Googling 'average hourly rate for [skill].' That's backwards."
- "Most agencies pitch their capabilities. The ones that win pitch their process."
- "Most founders build a product and then look for customers. The successful ones reverse the order."
- "Most proposals are written from the seller's perspective. Winners write from the buyer's."

---

## Pattern 16: The Direct Address
**How:** Speak directly to a specific person in your audience.
**Best for:** How-I posts, framework posts.

**Examples:**
- "If you're a consultant who still charges hourly, this post is for you."
- "To every freelancer who's afraid to raise their prices:"
- "For the founder who's doing everything themselves and wondering when it gets easier:"
- "If your proposals keep getting ghosted, here's what's probably happening."

---

## Pattern 17: The Micro-Story
**How:** Tell a complete story in one sentence.
**Best for:** Story posts, mistake posts.

**Examples:**
- "I spent 3 months building a product nobody wanted."
- "A client once paid me $15,000 for advice I could have given in 10 minutes."
- "I turned down a full-time offer at Google to start a consulting practice from my kitchen table."
- "My worst quarter in business taught me more than my best year."

---

## Pattern 18: The Contrast
**How:** Place two things side by side to highlight a difference.
**Best for:** Insight posts, framework posts.

**Examples:**
- "Junior consultants sell time. Senior consultants sell outcomes."
- "Good proposals describe what you'll do. Great proposals describe what the client will achieve."
- "Average emails explain your product. Great emails explain the recipient's problem."
- "Busy is a status. Productive is a result."

---

## Pattern 19: The Trend Call
**How:** Name a shift that's happening in your industry.
**Best for:** Curated observation posts, insight posts.

**Examples:**
- "Something is shifting in how mid-market companies buy consulting services."
- "The freelance market just went through its biggest structural change in a decade."
- "AI didn't replace copywriters. But it did replace average copywriters."
- "Three trends that will reshape [industry] by 2027."

---

## Pattern 20: The "I Stopped"
**How:** Describe something you deliberately quit doing and why.
**Best for:** Insight posts, contrarian posts.

**Examples:**
- "I stopped attending networking events 18 months ago. My pipeline has never been stronger."
- "I stopped writing long proposals. My win rate doubled."
- "I stopped checking email before noon. Here's what happened."
- "We stopped doing free discovery calls. Revenue went up 40%."

---

## Hooks to Avoid

- "Have you ever wondered..." — Weak, passive
- "In today's world..." — Generic, boring
- "Let me tell you a story..." — Don't announce it, just tell it
- "I'm excited to announce..." — Skip the filler
- "BREAKING:" / "IMPORTANT:" — You're not a news wire
- "Hot take:" — If you have to label it, it's probably lukewarm
- "PSA:" — Unless you're genuinely sharing public safety information
